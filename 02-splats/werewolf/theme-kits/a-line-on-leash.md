# A Line on Leash

**Themebook:** Cutting Edge · **Category:** Noise · **Source:** custom

Somebody else's body is the beast. Yours is the count. You know what a week costs, which nights are cheap, which Almoner will take a call and which one will only take a name, what a bad batch smells like before it is in you, and how to make three doses last four nights and what the fourth night does to you afterwards. Everyone in the pack needs Leash. You are the one whose whole life is arranged around it.

**[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-48) **Cutting Edge rather than Augmentation.** The Brief allows either (§2.5). Cutting Edge is chosen because the theme is a *compound the character acquires, tests, doses, cuts, and can lose* rather than something installed — the book's own Cutting Edge Itch example is a superhuman drug cocktail (Core p. 241) — and because leaving Augmentation free lets a Howler take [[sculpted-beast]] and this kit side by side without two themes of the same type. A table that wants the dependency *implanted* instead should take **Reflex Booster Implants** (Augmentation, Core p. 239) as printed; see [[werewolf-existing-kits]].

## Itch

> Take more than I need.

An impulse to indulge, in the Noise register: the dose you did not have to take, the second one, the one out of somebody else's count. Scratching it keeps the theme and deepens the debt; stifling it — rationing, skipping, going without to make the supply last — marks Decay (Core p. 136; Brief §4.2). Kit-fixed per `build-choices` BC-2.

## Specials

Two of the kit's own, plus the Cutting Edge themebook's five (Core p. 241) — **Amp Up**, **Modular Design**, **Still in Prototype**, **Void the Warranty**, and **Occult Blueprints**.

- **Stretch It:** Once per session, when you would be cut off from your supply, you may burn a power tag from this theme to keep one dose in hand and take *rationed-2*, which hinders every action that uses the sculpt and does not expire until you resupply.
- **Who Cut This:** When you examine a dose, a batch, or a body that took one, you may ask the MC one question about where it came from and get a straight answer. The people who sold it hear that you asked.

**[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-49) **Occult Blueprints is not available on this kit.** It writes a Tao method into the construction of the tech (Core p. 241), and Leash is manufactured pharmaceutical chemistry with no Tao in it at any point in the chain (Bible §2). The other four themebook Specials apply as printed.

## Nascent form

**a line on Leash** + *the supply is not mine* is a theme on its own: a person who has a supply and does not own it. That is the whole of Bible §4 theme 3 on one card, and it works as a replacement or nascent theme (Plan A.5).

## Canon and flags

- Canon relied on: Bible §2 werewolf — a chemical trigger the packs depend on, resold by a syndicate that is not the manufacturer; Bible §3 — the syndicate is a separate entity from the corporations; Bible §4 theme 3 — ownership versus freedom.
- Tag voice checked against `style-guide` §2 and Core p. 192: *a line on Leash* keeps the proper noun's capital; no tag carries a number; *kitchen-table chemistry* is specific, not a downgrade candidate.
- **Rarity** (Bible §2; Brief §2.6): the kit describes a supply for sculpted bodies only — weakness D makes that explicit — so nothing here implies a wide market or a common condition.
- **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-46) display name and coined tags; (BC-48) Cutting Edge over Augmentation; (BC-49) Occult Blueprints excluded.
- **[OPEN]** (OQ-3) tag I names an Almoner and tag G names a batch's origin; neither reaches Meliora, and no tag or Special here may be used to answer what Leash is for. **[OPEN]** (OQ-18) the Almoners' business beyond Leash is WP4's.
