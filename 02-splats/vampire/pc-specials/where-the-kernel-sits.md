# Where the Kernel Sits

**Splat:** vampire · **Prerequisite:** Your character carries the Ferrante strain, and someone has come looking for their master node — a scan, a surgeon, a question asked by the wrong person.

Write on your character card where in your body the Kernel sits: behind the sternum, in the left hip, wrapped along the spine, wherever it settled or wherever you put it. You always know when something is scanning for it, cutting toward it, or reaching for it, and you know from how far away. During downtime you may move it, and change what is written on the card — a night of work, and a week of being wrong about where your own hands are.

If the Kernel is destroyed, the character dies and the strain does not bring them back. A Consequence that would destroy it cannot be mitigated with tags from any theme. It can only be prevented by not being where it happens.

## Persistence

Chosen instead of a theme upgrade in any of the four main themes (Tokyo p. 72). Recorded on the character card and untouched by theme replacement (Brief §2.4). **Not removable**: a carrier without a Kernel is not a carrier, and the strain is irreversible (Bible §1, §2).

A Bloodware PC without this Special still has a Kernel — it is a fact about the character, not a fact about the card (Brief §2.4). What this Special buys is *knowing where it is* and *being able to move it*. What it costs is that the table now knows too, and the MC can aim.

## Canon and flags

- The persistent condition carried: **the master node**. Bible §2 — a master node of nanites which, if destroyed, kills the carrier; the street name is **the Kernel** (`names` §2). With EM, this replaces the classic vampire vulnerabilities outright; nothing else on that list applies.
- **[RULES CONFLICT]** (CR-7) the condition persists whether or not any theme does.
- Pairs with [[field-bitten]]. Both together are the whole of what can end a Bloodware, written down where everyone can see it.
- NPC Bloodware must carry the same exposure as a Limit rather than a Special (Plan A.6) — see `bloodware-power-set`.
