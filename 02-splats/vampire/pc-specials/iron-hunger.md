# Iron Hunger

**Splat:** vampire · **Prerequisite:** Your character carries the Ferrante strain, and has gone a full job without taking blood or iron.

The colony replicates with iron and does not care where it comes from. Once per session, when you take blood or raw iron in a scene, remove up to two tiers of statuses representing physical injury or exhaustion — the strain has material to work with and spends it on you. At the end of any downtime in which you did not take blood or iron, gain `hungry-2`. It rises one tier at the end of each further downtime you do not feed. It hinders every action that is not taking blood or iron, it cannot be mitigated, and nothing removes it or reduces it except feeding.

Iron is quieter than blood and slower: a downtime spent on filings, rust, and scavenged rebar clears `hungry` two tiers rather than removing it. Blood clears it entirely.

## Persistence

Chosen instead of a theme upgrade in any of the four main themes (Tokyo p. 72). Recorded on the character card, not the theme card, and untouched by theme replacement (Brief §2.4). **Not removable.** Tokyo's rule lets a Megacity Special be dropped for an Evolution point when the narrative conditions stop being relevant; the conditions here never stop being relevant, because vampirism is irreversible (Bible §1). A Bloodware whose every Noise theme has Decayed away still has this card.

## Canon and flags

- The persistent condition carried: **the blood-or-iron dependency**. Bible §2 — the nanites replicate using iron, so a carrier needs blood or iron. Bible §4 theme 3 — ownership versus freedom, in the plainest form the setting has.
- **[RULES CONFLICT]** (CR-7, adopted by the Brief) The power lives in the theme and can Decay; the condition does not. This Special is where the second half of that resolution lives.
- This is not a humanity track (Brief §4.3). It measures nothing about the character's decency, it rises on one axis, and feeding is the only thing that touches it.
- Feeding is deliberately not costed in the fiction here — who you took it from, and whether they lived, is the table's business and the MC's.
