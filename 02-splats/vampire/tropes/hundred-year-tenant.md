# Hundred-Year Tenant

**Splat:** vampire · **Built toward:** Cyborg

You have lived under the Wall for a century in the same handful of blocks, under six or seven names, in rooms nobody remembers you leaving. You are not powerful. You never got sway, never got a clan, never got invited anywhere — you got old, which for one of you means you got careful. You found your own node, moved it, wrapped it, and learned to run the body from there. You keep three sets of papers, two ways out of every room, and enough iron to go a fortnight without touching anyone.

Then somebody sorted you into the Ledger for something you saw, which after a hundred years of not being noticed is very nearly funny.

## Theme kits

- [[hardened-kernel]] (CUTTING EDGE) — a century of work on the one thing that can end you
- Meticulous Planner (PERSONALITY, Core p. 214) — how you have stayed uninteresting for a hundred years
- Safehouse (ASSETS, Core p. 203) — the room, the ways out, the stash

**Choose one:**

- Enhanced Senses (AUGMENTATION, Core p. 239) — chrome you bought decades ago and never took out
- Escaped Servitude (TROUBLED PAST, Core p. 218) — the Ledger, most recent of a long list of places you got out of
- Zeroed Identity (CYBERSPACE, Core p. 247) — six or seven names, and the records to back the current one

[[the-ferrante-strain]] is **not** on this list: a fourth theme may not share a themebook with a fixed one (Core p. 177), and [[hardened-kernel]] is already Cutting Edge.

**Loadout:** mesh-lined longcoat (bulky), three sets of papers, iron ampoules (limited doses)

## On the sheet

Fixed: Cutting Edge (Noise), Personality (Self), Assets (Self). Every choice lands on **Cyborg** — Self and Noise (Core p. 135) — and satisfies the Essence minimum of at least one Noise theme (Brief §2.2, BC-1). Cyberspace is a Noise themebook, so Zeroed Identity makes the character two-Noise; the other two keep them three-Self.

This is the trope that can honestly take **a hundred years of practice** — but that tag lives on [[the-ferrante-strain]], which this trope cannot hold. That is deliberate: a Tenant's century shows up as *run myself from the core*, *read as a corpse* and *an older one taught me*, and as three sets of papers. If a player wants the long life on the card as raw capability rather than discipline, they should build from [[scrap-fed-newblood]] and age the character in the fiction instead.

If the GM opens **[OPEN]** (OQ-5), [[interwoven-strain]] (EXPOSURE) becomes a legal fourth kit and makes the character a **Nexus** — a hundred years is long enough to have slept in the wrong place at least once.

**PC Specials.** [[where-the-kernel-sits]] is this trope's Special: the prerequisite (someone has come looking) is the character's entire history. [[field-bitten]] fits a Tenant who has walked into exactly one field and never forgotten it.

## Canon and flags

- Canon relied on: Bible §2 (longevity by replication with a ship-of-Theseus body, the Kernel, EM, rarity), Bible §3 (the Masquerade — this character's whole method is not being noticed), Bible §5 (a street-level carrier without sway knows nothing about the upstart, and a century of survival has not bought this one sway), Bible §6 (the crew starts at the bottom); Brief §2.2, §2.5.
- **[OPEN]** (OQ-4) *an older one taught me* names a teacher and settles nothing about transmission; who made this character, a century ago, is the player's to answer and the MC's to complicate.
- Nothing here tells the player what a clan is or what happens to noteworthy ones. Age is not access ([[bloodware]] MC only).
- **Link note.** `fixed_kits` and `choice_kits` carry slugs for the book kits as stable identifiers; book kits have no vault file and are cited by name, themebook and page (`style-guide` §8.5).
- A hundred years of caution here is caution about fields, scanners, and anyone who asks where you keep it. Nothing else on the classic list applies (Bible §2).
- Book kits in `fixed_kits` / `choice_kits` carry the `book:` prefix and are indexed with page and themebook in [[book-kits-index]] (BC-126); they are offered as printed, never copied in.
