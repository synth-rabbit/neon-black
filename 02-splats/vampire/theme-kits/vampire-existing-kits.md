# Bloodware — Existing Kits

Book kits to put in front of a Bloodware player. These are offered **in addition to** the custom kits, never instead of them (Brief §0.1). Every kit below is printed in the core book; nothing here reprints its tags. The re-flavor line says what the printed kit means when the thing running it is the Ferrante strain.

## Cutting Edge (Noise) — the direct fits

| Kit | Page | Re-flavor |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-Healing** | Core p. 243 | The closest printed kit to a Bloodware and the one to hand a player who wants the classic package without a custom card. *toxic blood nanites* and *accelerated regen gel* are the strain itself rather than a treatment; *sacrifice a limb* is recalling the colony to the core and letting the limb go; *medical monitor* is whatever the character uses to watch their own iron. **Change the weakness reading, not the words:** *requires nutrients* is blood or iron and nothing else, and *sought-after blood* is what happens when someone works out what is in it. *Extreme cold* is a printed weakness with no canon behind it — swap it for a field-based weakness or take one of the other three. The kit's Itch, *"Ignore the danger, I can survive it,"* is legal but does not point at feeding; a Bloodware taking this kit should replace it with the fixed Itch from [[the-ferrante-strain]] so Decay stays adjudicable (Brief §2.5, §4.2). |
| **Cloud of Nanites** | Core p. 242 | The strain worked *outside* the body: pushing the colony into a lock, a wound, a wall. *alter matter*, *disintegrate objects*, *craft a tool* and *compositional data* all read as the swarm eating and re-laying metal. Its printed weaknesses are already canon-shaped: *strong magnetic fields* is the EM weakness in the book's own words, *rogue nanite contamination* is what the colony does when it gets away from you, and *wreaks havoc on my DNA* is the ship-of-Theseus problem said briskly. The Itch, *"Leave nothing as it was,"* is a legal Bloodware Itch but again does not point at feeding — pair it with a feeding Itch on the character's other Noise theme or replace it. |
| **Advanced Railgun**, **Cloaking Jumpsuit**, **Cryptographic Skeleton Key**, **Exoskeleton Suit**, **Force Fields**, **Experimental Vehicle** | Core p. 242–243 | Ordinary gear kits. A Bloodware may take any of them as a second Noise theme with no re-flavor at all — being what they are does not stop anyone buying a jumpsuit. **Force Fields** and **Exoskeleton Suit** are worth naming: both are one plausible answer to "how does a Bloodware survive a field," and neither one solves it. |

## Augmentation (Noise) — chrome, not strain

A Bloodware can buy implants like anyone else. The line to hold: **the strain is not an augmentation.** Anything from this themebook is hardware the character paid for, installed on top of what the strain already did. If a player wants the strain itself on the card, that is Cutting Edge.

| Kit | Page | Re-flavor |
|---|---|---|
| **Enhanced Senses** | Core p. 239 | Sensory chrome that a Bloodware bought because the strain's own senses are not selective — *pierce through concealment*, *hear heartbeats*, *lie detector*. Its weakness *sensory overload* pairs well with a strain that is already feeding the character more than they asked for. |
| **Armored Juggernaut** | Core p. 238 | Take it as installed plating and servos, not as the strain making the body bigger. Its weaknesses (*bulky*, *rusts when wet*, *treated like a brainless brute*) are hardware problems and should stay hardware problems. |
| **Reflex Booster Implants** | Core p. 239 | Offered with a warning. Its weaknesses — *withdrawal symptoms*, *bloodstream already flooded*, *stigmatized as a junkie*, *body can't self regulate* — read so cleanly as the iron hunger that a table may end up with the dependency living on a chrome kit instead of the strain. That is a legal build; make sure it is a deliberate one, because the hunger persists whether or not this theme does ([[iron-hunger]]). |
| **Boosted Mental Capacity** | Core p. 238 | For a century-old Bloodware who has stopped trusting their own memory and bought one that keeps records. The weakness *flood of memories* does a lot of work on a character who has been rebuilt plank by plank. |
| **Impossibly Good Looks**, **Chipped Weapon Mastery**, **Hidden Gadgets** | Core p. 238–239 | No re-flavor needed; take as bought. **Animalistic Modifications** (p. 238) is Howler content and should not be handed to a Bloodware player as a way to get claws — the strain grows bone, and that is [[hardened-kernel]]. |

## Exposure (Mythos) — conditional

**[OPEN]** (OQ-5) Whether Bloodware can use Tao at all is unresolved. Everything in this section is switched off unless the GM answers yes. **[TAO-REINTERPRETED]** In every kit below, the themebook's *Source* is a Tao gateway — a practice, an item, or a place where Tao concentrates (Brief §3.2) — never a legend, a Mythos or an Avatar of one, and the printed Rituals are rewritten as disciplines, prices or conditions (Brief §3.2, CR-3).

| Kit | Page | Re-flavor |
|---|---|---|
| **Elemental Body [iron]** | Core p. 234 | The best fit in the themebook: the bracketed element is **iron**, which is the one substance a Bloodware's body is already organized around. *absorb [iron]*, *sculpt [iron]*, *unharmed by [iron]*, *places rich in [iron]* need no adjustment. Rewrite the printed Ritual *"I must bathe in [element] daily"* as a discipline — *"I must take my iron cold, from the same source, at the same hour"* — so it is a practice and a price, not a legend's demand (CR-3). |
| **Speak with Machines** | Core p. 235 | A colony that already speaks to itself in signal, doing it outward. Rewrite the Ritual *"I must report routinely to [a powerful machine intelligence]"* — there is no such intelligence in canon and this vault does not add one. Use a discipline instead: *"I must sit in a dead room, without signal, until it is quiet."* Note that this kit's weakness *signal jamming* stacks with the character's EM problem rather than replacing it. |
| **Animate Shadows** | Core p. 234 | The most recognizably vampiric kit in the book and the one to be most careful with. It is legal only under OQ-5, its powers are Tao and not the strain, and and none of its weaknesses may be re-read as one of the classic vampire vulnerabilities canon removes — *bright ambient light* is a Tao condition on a Tao power, and it does not touch the strain, which is unbothered by light (Bible §2). Strike *light deities*: there are none (`style-guide` §6). |
| **Aura of Authority** | Core p. 234 | An old Bloodware with a century of practice at being obeyed. The printed Ritual, *"I must worship at the shrine of the entity that grants me my authority,"* is exactly the legend language the setting removes; rewrite it as a self-imposed condition — *"I must hold court at the same hour, in the same room, and never explain myself"* (CR-3). |

## Tropes and Specials

The book's printed character tropes (Core p. 178–187) are all available. None of them is a Bloodware; the two custom ones are [[scrap-fed-newblood]] and [[hundred-year-tenant]]. Tokyo's **Nanotech Residue** (Tokyo p. 73) is the pattern the Bloodware PC Specials are built on and is worth reading beside them, but it is not itself the strain — it protects a bloodstream from foreign nanites, which is very nearly the opposite problem ([[iron-hunger]], [[field-bitten]], [[where-the-kernel-sits]], [[made-by-the-strain]]).

## Canon and flags

- Canon relied on: Bible §2 (nanites, iron, EM and the Kernel as the only weaknesses, rarity), Brief §0.1 (existing *and* custom), §2.5, §3.2.
- **[TAO-REINTERPRETED]** every Exposure entry above; **[OPEN]** (OQ-5) gates the whole Exposure section.
- Rarity: none of these kits is described as purchasable, and no entry here places a supplier.
- No reused weakness tag may be re-read as one of the classic vampire vulnerabilities canon removes; where a printed kit invites that reading (*bright ambient light*), the entry says so. EM and the Kernel are the only two (Bible §2).
