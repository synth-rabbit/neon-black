# Casters

![[assets/splats/casters.png]]

A **Caster** is a pure Tao practitioner. Tao is older than the chrome, it is the only mystical force in Palisade, and it is the one power in this city nobody manufactures (Bible §1, §2). Casters use technology to *augment* the practice — never to produce it.

Casters are rare. Extreme modification is rare; Tao use is rarer (Bible §2). A practitioner can go a working lifetime without meeting another one who was not introduced to them.

## 1. What Tao is to a practitioner

Tao is a force. It flows, it concentrates, it can be channeled, and it can be interwoven with tech (`style-guide` §6). It has no will, no preferences, and nothing it is trying to accomplish. It does not choose you, it does not test you, and it does not take anything back when you fail. Everything that looks like Tao asking something of you is the **practice** asking, and the practice is a human thing.

Three words carry the whole of it at the table:

- **Discipline.** Tao does nothing on its own. A discipline is a repeatable human method — a form kept every morning, a set of measurements at a bench, a way of breathing before a door — that puts a person into the state where the force runs through them and comes out doing one specific thing. A discipline is *learned*, and a discipline can *lapse*.
- **Price.** Every discipline costs something to keep: hours, materials, stillness, sleep, the ability to be in a hurry. The price is not extracted by anything. It is simply what the method takes.
- **Condition.** Some disciplines only work under conditions — both hands free, silence, a bench, a month-fresh working. The condition is a fact about the method, like a fact about a machine.

Tao's gateways in Palisade are the four the pillar names (`pillars`): a **practice** kept with discipline, an **item** into which Tao has been worked, a **place** where Tao runs dense, a **being** Tao has been made to hold in shape. A Caster's home ground is the first; the others are what a practitioner makes, buys, finds, or stands up.

**[OPEN]** (OQ-6) The public does not have the word. To a bystander a working is a trick, a drug, a stunt, or a new implant, and the Noise does the rest (Brief §4.1). Whether anyone outside the practice knows Tao exists at all is the GM's.

## 2. What a Caster knows about the Wuji

The Bible gives three facts and no more: a secret society identifies talented people and brings them into the fold; it is secret; the corporations prefer it that way (Bible §2, §3). Its name and its front are in `names`: **the Wuji**, rumoured from outside as **the Quiet Hand**, keeping a members' club and cultural foundation called **Aldine House** on [[palisade-player-primer#Chancery Hill|Chancery Hill]].

**[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-53) What a starting Caster knows depends on which of three positions they hold, and no starting position knows very much:

| Position | Knows | Does not know |
|---|---|---|
| **Placed initiate** — found, offered a place, taught | That there is a house, that it teaches, that the practice is real and old, that there are others. An instructor who is never introduced by name. One or two other students by face. That the teaching is not free and the bill has not yet come. | The society's name. How many houses there are. Who decides anything. Anything about a corporation. |
| **Rebel or corp-side practitioner** — taught somewhere else, or found by somebody else first | That an organized practice exists and did not choose them. Usually the rumour name, *the Quiet Hand*, and rarely more than the rumour. That some employers pay for a practitioner and some employers hunt one. | Everything the initiate does not know, plus whether the house is real at all. |
| **Unfound street practitioner** — worked it out alone, or off a fragment somebody sold them | That what they do works, that it should not, and that nobody they can safely ask has ever seen it. | That there is anyone else. |

The society's **name** is not a starting-character fact in any of the three positions. A player may write a Caster who has heard *the Quiet Hand* said out loud in a bar; that is a rumour, and rumours on the Wall are cheap.

**Minimum: at least one Mythos theme** (Brief §2.2, `build-choices` BC-1). A Caster with no Mythos theme is not practising.

### Avatar and Conduit under Tao `[TAO-REINTERPRETED]`

**[TAO-REINTERPRETED]** (CR-8) The book's Avatar is "a beacon of your legend on earth" and the Conduit "a vessel for mythical forces" (Core p. 136). There are no legends and no forces with anything to say (Brief §3.1, §3.3). Restated:

- **Avatar** — *only Mythos, same Source.* Every theme draws on **one** gateway: one discipline, or one discipline and the items and beings that discipline made. The practitioner and the practice have stopped being separable; there is no longer a person who does the form and a form that gets done. Mechanically unchanged: suspend all Rituals and choose an **Agenda**, which if ever defied once replaces every theme. Under impersonal Tao the Agenda is a **law the practitioner imposed on themselves** — the discipline's price generalised until it governs a life. Nothing imposes it and nothing punishes its breach; the practice simply comes apart, because the practice was the law.
- **Conduit** — *only Mythos, different Sources.* Themes drawn from several gateways at once — a discipline, a worked item, a dense place, a made being — held in one body. Mechanically unchanged: replace themes at will with Mythos themes; any gateway in reach can become the next theme, starting as a full theme.

**[RULES CONFLICT]** (CR-8, restated in `conflict-register` as CR-11) The Avatar entry's *"you are a Source unto yourself"* survives; the *"beacon of your legend"* does not, and the MC-facing note about a "power trip" belongs to a legend's appetite the Bible removed. The two readings offered: (a) Avatar as written minus the legend language, above — adopted here; (b) Avatar unavailable to Casters on the grounds that a person cannot be a gateway under impersonal Tao, in which case a four-Mythos same-gateway Caster is a Conduit with a redundant clause. Not resolved.

## 5. Persistence — what a Caster keeps and what they lose

Every other one of the five carries a condition that cannot be undone (Bible §1). A Caster does not. Tao is not manufactured and nothing was done to a Caster's body to make them one.

**[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-55) **A Caster who lets a discipline Decay loses the practice, not the talent.**

- The **discipline** is the theme. Neglect its Ritual, mark Decay three times, and it is replaced like any theme (Core p. 136). What is gone is the method: the forms, the bench habits, the hours. In the fiction the practitioner has stopped, and stopping is the ordinary way a practice ends.
- The **talent** is not a theme and cannot Decay. Tao still runs through this person the way it always did. They can be taught again — by the same instructor, by a different one, by a book, by trial — and the next discipline may be nothing like the last.
- Therefore a replacement theme may be another Mythos theme (another discipline, an item, a being, an exposure), or it may be Self or Noise. A Caster who replaces their last Mythos theme with a Self theme has **stopped practising**. They are still someone Tao runs through; they are not a Caster in play until they take up a discipline again.
- **Tension with the Essence minimum:** the minimum (§4) is a **character-creation** rule, not a standing one. A Caster who practises their way down to zero Mythos themes has made a choice the fiction allows; a player who wants to start one has not. `[BUILD CHOICE]` (BC-55).

This is the one place the five diverge. It is not an asymmetry of power (Bible §2 forbids engineering one) — a lapsed Caster is exactly as capable as anyone else who dropped a theme. It is an asymmetry of *irreversibility*, and it follows from canon: bodies do not come back, practices do.

The two PC Specials in `pc-specials/` carry what *does* stick to a Caster across theme replacement: an obligation somebody is owed, and a trained eye that does not un-train.

## 6. Motivation

Every Mythos theme's motivation is a **Ritual**, and under impersonal Tao a Ritual is a **discipline, a price, or a condition** — never a legend re-enacted, never something Tao wants (Brief §3.2, `style-guide` §3).

Write it as a practice in the first person with *must*:

- Discipline — *"I must keep the morning form, wherever I wake."*
- Price — *"I must re-work every shell I spend before I sleep."*
- Condition — *"I must stand the form again each day it is used."*

Never: *"I must re-enact…," "I must satisfy…," "Tao demands…," "the practice wants…"* — a Ritual that reads as anything asking is a defect, flagged `[RULES CONFLICT]` under `conflict-register` CR-3.

Neglecting a Ritual marks Decay (Core p. 136). For a Caster that reads exactly as it sounds: you skipped the practice, and the practice is getting away from you.

## 7. How a Caster reads on the sheet

- **Four themes.** At least one Esoterica. Most often: one Esoterica, one Cutting Edge or other Noise theme, two Self.
- **Motivations.** Ritual on every Mythos theme; Itch on every Noise theme; Identity on every Self theme.
- **Essence Special** from §4, written on the character card.
- **Tag discipline.** *cast Tao*, *do magic*, and *the practice* are too broad to be tags (Core p. 192). A discipline's tags name what it *does* — *arrest a moving thing*, *load a discipline into a round*, *still a rising voice*. One broad tag per character across all themes, as for everyone.
- **Loadout.** Worked items are Loadout tags, not free effects; the Street Catalog's Tao-touched category is `09-loadout/`.
- **PC Specials** (`pc-specials/`) go on the character card, not a theme card, and survive theme replacement (Tokyo p. 72).
