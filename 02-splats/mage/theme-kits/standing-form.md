# A Standing Form

**Themebook:** Companion · **Category:** Mythos · **Setting name:** Caster · **Source:** custom

Tao can produce beings. It does not decide to; a practitioner does, and the practitioner keeps doing it, every day, or the being stops. A standing form is what comes up out of a discipline held long enough in one place: something about the size of a person, near enough to a person's shape that a crowd will not look twice, which stands where it is put and does the one thing it was stood up for. It is not a servant, because there is nobody in it to serve. It is a working that happens to be upright.

The book recommends pairing a Companion theme with another Mythos theme as its gateway (Core p. 224). Do that: pair this with [[aldine-forms]] or [[shellwork]] and the being is that discipline, standing.

## Ritual

> I must stand the form again each day it is used.

A condition (`style-guide` §3): the being exists because a practice is being kept, and it stops when the practice stops. Neglect is a day skipped — a job that ran two nights, a hospital bed, a cell. Nothing takes the form away as a penalty. It simply comes apart, the way anything does when nobody is holding it.

## Specials

Inherits the Companion themebook's five (Core p. 225): *Back-to-back*, *Caregiver*, *Hands Off*, *New Tricks*, *Strategic Positioning*. **[TAO-REINTERPRETED]** *New Tricks* (a power tag answered from Esoterica or Exposure "that reflects your Companion's new manifested powers") reads as **the practitioner working a new capability into the form** — nothing manifests on its own.

This kit adds one:

**Formed Again:** Once per job, if your standing form is destroyed or comes apart, you may stand it again at the end of the scene instead of waiting for downtime. When you do, you take *drained-2*, which you cannot mitigate.

## As a secondary character

The book makes a Companion a secondary player character that takes its own tags and statuses (Core p. 111, 224). That stands. What it does not get is a personality to play: this being acts, it does not want. In play it is directed, positioned, and spent, and when a scene asks it something it has no answer for, it does the last thing it was formed to do.

## Nascent form

Title tag **a standing form** plus one weakness reads as a theme: a practitioner who can stand something up and keep it up, with one clear cost. Everything else the form does can be added by upgrade.

## `[RULES CONFLICT]` — a made being's wants

**[RULES CONFLICT]** (CR-10) The Companion themebook states that Companions "have opinions and sometimes act on their own," that their assistance "always involves striving to achieve what the creature ultimately desires," and that many "have an ulterior motive or skewed agenda dependent on the myth they derive from" (Core p. 224). Canon says Tao is impersonal and has no will, and also that Tao can produce beings (Brief §3.1). Those two are not automatically contradictory — a made thing could have appetites its maker gave it without Tao having any — but the themebook's version derives the appetites *from the myth*, and there is no myth. Two readings, neither adopted as settled:

- **(a) Willed by the maker — adopted as this kit's default.** The form has standing instructions and nothing else: no goals, no preferences, tag J *it has no opinions*. Where the themebook's rules need a Companion to act on its own, it acts on its instructions, which is what makes weakness A and D bite. Cost: the themebook's crew-relationship options and some of its Specials assume a personality, and three of the four listed relationships ("a crew member has a real problem with your Companion", "your Companion takes a shine to one of your crew", "your companion is being overprotective") need rewriting as reactions to a thing rather than to a person.
- **(b) Willed by the working.** A being made out of Tao develops appetites of its own that the practitioner did not put there — hunger, preference, a direction it drifts. Cost: it reads as Tao having produced a will, which is one step from Tao having one, and the vault's hardest line is that Tao never wants (`style-guide` §6). If the GM takes this reading, the appetite must be stated as the *being's* and never as Tao's, and the kit's tag J and weakness A both change.

## Canon and flags

- Canon relied on: Tao can produce beings and Companion themes are allowed (Brief §3.1, §3.2); Tao is impersonal (Bible §1; Brief §3.1); Conjurations are gone and Companions are constructs (Brief §3.2).
- **[TAO-REINTERPRETED]** (CR-1) Conjurations → made beings; "spawned by a myth" → stood up by a practitioner; *New Tricks* → worked in, not manifested.
- **[RULES CONFLICT]** (CR-10) above.
- **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-61) The standing form's shape — upright, person-sized, instruction-bound, re-made rather than repaired — and its name. Registered in `build-choices`.
- **[OPEN]** (OQ-26) What happens to a standing form the day its practitioner dies. Canon does not say whether a working outlives the person keeping it; the kit's Ritual implies not, and the vault asserts nothing further.
- **Rarity** (Bible §2): standing a being up takes a discipline kept daily for as long as the being exists. There is no version of this that a person does casually, and there is no market in them.
