# Shellwork

**Themebook:** Esoterica · **Category:** Mythos · **Setting name:** Caster · **Source:** custom

Tao is older than the chrome and it does not care that the chrome exists — which is the whole opening, because it means the two can be put in the same object and neither one objects. Shellwork is the bench discipline of doing exactly that: a working measured out and seated into something manufactured, a cartridge or a cell or a length of somebody's arm, so that a thing which should be inert carries a practice around and spends it when it is used. It takes hours, it takes a room, and it takes the practitioner's own sleep. What comes off the bench looks like ordinary hardware right up until it is fired.

## Ritual

> I must re-work every shell I spend before I sleep.

A price (`style-guide` §3): the bench is not optional and the debt is same-day. Neglecting it means going to bed with an empty rack — after a long job, in somebody else's flat, with the tools still where you left them and no way back to them. The practice does not punish that. It just stops being ready.

## Specials

Inherits the Esoterica themebook's five (Core p. 229). This kit adds one:

**Off the Bench:** Once per job, during downtime or a Prep Sequence, name one worked item you made in advance — a shell, a cell, a focus, an implant tune — and gain a story tag for it. The tag burns when the item is spent, and the item cannot be re-made before the next downtime.

### The two Specials this kit is built to pair with

Shellwork is the theme that sits on the seam between the categories, and the books already have the two rules that cross it. A Caster running this kit should be pointed at both:

- **Occult Blueprints** (Cutting Edge, Core p. 241) — on a **Noise** theme: answer a power tag question from Esoterica or Exposure "that reflects magical methods used in the construction of this Cutting Edge tech." This is the practice reaching into a piece of gear. Take it on the Caster's optional secondary Noise theme ([[casters]] §3) and the gear stops being separate from the practice. **[TAO-REINTERPRETED]** — read "magical methods" as *the working the bench put in*.
- **Overcharged and Overclocked** (Artifact, Core p. 221) — on a **Mythos** theme: answer a power tag question from Cutting Edge or Drones "that reflects a technological upgrade to your Artifact." This is the gear reaching into the working, and it is the Special that turns [[worked-sidearm]] into a caster shell rather than a charmed antique.

Between them they are the printed mechanical statement of the Bible's "Tao can be interwoven with technology," pointing in both directions. Neither is invented here; both are offered as written.

## Nascent form

Title tag **shellwork** plus one weakness still reads as a theme: somebody who can put a working into an object, given a room and enough hours. The bench is the character.

## Canon and flags

- Canon relied on: Tao is real, older than the mods, and can be interwoven with technology, caster-shell style (Bible §1, stated influences; Brief §3.1); Casters are pure Tao users who use tech to augment the practice (Bible §2); the mage's optional secondary is Noise (Brief §2.1).
- **[TAO-REINTERPRETED]** (CR-1) Esoterica's "occult symbols and incantations," *Occult Blueprints*' "magical methods," and *Overcharged and Overclocked*'s "the Mythos within it" all read as **a working a person put there**. Nothing in a worked object wants anything, and a worked object that stops working has simply gone flat.
- **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-60) *Shellwork* as the name and shape of the tech-augmented discipline: bench-made, spent on use, same-day debt. The name is the reason the setting calls these practitioners **Casters** (`names` §1) read backwards — the shell is the work, not the person.
- **Rarity** (Bible §2): everything this kit produces looks like ordinary hardware, and almost all of the hardware in Palisade is ordinary. A worked shell is rare enough that the people who can recognise one mostly made it.
