# Switch and Handler

**For:** Doppels · **Prerequisite:** Your Doppel is owned — the kill switch and the tracker are still in you, and a named handler holds both.

Once per job you may draw on the people who own you. Gain four story tags for the scene from among *standing orders*, *unit credentials*, *a fitter on call*, *a car that comes when it is called*, and *somebody who will make a body disappear*. Whenever you do, your handler learns where you were and what you spent it on, and you take **owed-2** until the end of the next downtime, which hinders any action to keep something from your unit. While you carry this Special, any Challenge that can reach your handler may, as a Consequence, give you **switch-primed-3**: it hinders every action you take away from your handler's last order, and it can be mitigated only by obeying that order, by getting out of the unit's reach, or by having the switch cut out.

This is the whole shape of being owned. The leash is genuinely useful — a unit's resources are better than anything the crew can field at the bottom of the Wall — and every time you pull on it, it pulls back.

## Persistence

Survives theme replacement (Brief §2.4, `conflict-register` CR-7). A Doppel who replaces their face theme is still carrying a switch, and the person holding it does not care what is written on the character's cards.

**Removed only by cutting the switch and the tracker out.** That is surgery, it takes a place and a surgeon willing to do it — the Sump behind [[palisade-player-primer#Suture Row|Suture Row]] is the one on the map — and it is the single most dangerous thing a Doppel can do. When it happens: remove this Special, take [[retrieval-contract]], swap the weakness card on [[borrowed-face-owned]] for [[borrowed-face-cutloose]]'s, and mark an Evolution point (Tokyo p. 72).

## Canon and flags

- The persistent condition: **ownership**. Bible §2 — changelings are made and controlled by whoever built them, with kill switches and trackers. Bible §4 theme 3 — ownership versus freedom. The switch is a fact about the body, not a theme, and it does not go away when a theme does.
- Modelled on Tokyo's *Friends in the Bureau* (Tokyo p. 73), which grants four story tags for a scene at the cost of a tier-2 status from the institution that granted them.
- **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-83) *switch-primed* as a status and the three ways to mitigate it; the Bible gives the switch but not its behaviour.
- **[RULES CONFLICT]** (CR-13) The removal clause depends on the switch and tracker being removable. Bible §1 permits removal of cyborg implants generally, Bible §2 requires it for escapees; Brief §2.4 states the exception as hunter-only. The Bible wins (Brief preamble); the row records the alternative.
