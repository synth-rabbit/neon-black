# The Face Doesn't Come Off

**For:** Doppels · **Prerequisite:** You have worn one person's face long enough that people who knew them believed you — a full job, or longer.

Name a person whose face you have worn. They may be alive; it is usually better if they are not.

Once per session, when you act as that person in front of someone who knew them, you may treat a miss (6-) as a mixed hit (7-9) — not because you are performing well, but because some of what you copied is still in there and it answers before you do. Whenever you do, take **bleeding-through-1**, which stacks; at tier 2 or higher it hinders any action to be recognised as yourself. It clears only during a downtime spent alone in your own shape.

## Persistence

Survives theme replacement (Brief §2.4, `conflict-register` CR-7). It is not a power; it is what the work does to a person over time, and the sculpt is irreversible (Bible §1).

Removed only for narrative reasons — you have finished with them, or they have finished with you. Mark an Evolution point (Tokyo p. 72).

## Canon and flags

- The persistent condition: **the mods are permanent, and so is what they cost**. Bible §1 (body sculpting and bio-manipulation cannot be undone), Bible §2 (the Doppel is a body rewritten to be somebody else's), Bible §4 theme 1 (the price of climbing when the ladder is made of your own body).
- Available to owned and Cutloose Doppels alike. Pairs with [[borrowed-face-owned]] or [[borrowed-face-cutloose]]; the theme's weakness *my own face is guesswork* on [[held-shape]] is the same idea from the other side.
- **No humanity mechanic.** *bleeding-through* is a status on one named relationship with one named person, cleared by a downtime; it is not a track, it does not accumulate across the character, and nothing in this vault reads it as a measure of how human a Doppel is (Brief §4.3, `style-guide` §5).
- **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-86) *bleeding-through* as a stacking status, and the once-per-session miss-to-mixed conversion, modelled on Tokyo's *Helpful Kami* (Tokyo p. 73).
