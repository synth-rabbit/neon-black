# Standard-Issue Replacement

**Themebook:** Augmentation · **Category:** Noise · **Splat:** hunter · **Source:** custom

Fitted cheap, in a Suture Row chop-shop or off a mass-market AP&I loaner line, or bought with real money and a real warranty on the Crest — a Baseline's one door into cybernetics, and the only one Bible canon leaves open. This kit exists to prove a limb, an eye, or an organ can be replaced without becoming anything more than what it was. **Every tag above and every tag a table adds later must answer its question as a repair, never an upgrade** — if a tag would give a Baseline superhuman strength, speed, senses, or cognition, it belongs to one of the other four, not to a Baseline (see `existing-kits.md`'s Augmentation table for the book kits this one exists instead of).

## Itch

> Prove it still does exactly what the old one did — no more, no less.

Indulging this Itch means actively demonstrating baseline parity — using the replaced part exactly as the original worked, in front of someone who's watching. Resisting it means hiding the limb, favoring it, or letting doubt about whether it's "still you" show — the theme's Decay direction, same as any Noise theme, but here the pull is toward staying ordinary, not toward escalating.

## Specials

Inherits Augmentation's five (Core p. 237): Backstory, Formidable Tech, More than Human, Quick Reboot, This is Who I Am. **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-93) "More than Human" (ignore tier-3-or-lower harm statuses to the rest of the body while scratching the Itch) is available as printed — it protects the *rest* of the character's body, not the replaced part, and grants no capability beyond baseline, so it does not conflict with the replacement-only rule.

## Nascent form

Title tag **a working replacement** + weakness **flags every scanner it passes** still reads as a whole theme: a visibly repaired body that draws exactly the kind of attention Bible §3's cover story depends on.

## Canon and flags

- Bible §2: cybernetics only as replacements for lost parts; no sculpting, no bio-manipulation — every tag above is written to answer its themebook question without exceeding the function of the part it replaced.
- Modeled on Tokyo's **Cheap Prosthetic** Megacity Special (Tokyo p. 73) as the template Brief §2.5 names; see [[cheap-prosthetic]] for the persistent-condition PC Special this kit's power lives alongside (Brief §2.4, CR-7) — a Baseline can lose this theme to Decay and replace it with something else, and the prosthetic itself simply stays, until it's surgically removed.
- Rarity note: tag D (scanner flags) and the fitter's-part weakness (C) keep this humble rather than heroic — this kit is not a stealth upgrade or a power spike, it is what "still human" costs when the cost is visible.
