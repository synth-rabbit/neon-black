# Shared Lore Bible

**Working title:** Neon Black
**Purpose:** System-agnostic lore foundation for a cyberpunk / World of Darkness mashup. This document is the shared input for two parallel system skeletons (one in Otherscape, one in Year Zero Engine). Nothing here assumes either engine's mechanics.

**Conventions for the downstream builder**

- Every statement in sections 1–6 was given by the GM during the interview. Nothing has been invented to fill gaps.
- Items marked **[OPEN]** are unresolved. Do not resolve them silently; either leave them as explicit hooks or surface them for the GM to decide.
- "Magic" is written as **Tao** throughout (the GM's shorthand, after *Outlaw Star*). The GM's instruction is to **use the selected engine's own term for magic** in the system skeleton; "Tao" is a placeholder, not a setting name.
- Splat names are **TBD**. The campaign may use setting-specific names, but each must be immediately legible to a player as its classic World of Darkness counterpart (vampire, werewolf, mage, changeling, hunter).

**Stated influences**

- *Red Rising* — body sculpting, the general tech level across the series, fighting from the bottom, haves versus varying tiers of have-nots.
- *Outlaw Star* — Tao magic (a real mystical force that can be interwoven with technology, as with Gene's caster-gun shells), a mixed crew of characters, and the overall vibe.
- *Cyberpunk 2077* — corporate-city texture and cybernetics.

---

## 1. Core Conceit

### Tech and magic

**Technology creates the splats.** Vampires, werewolves, and changelings exist because of technology: combinations of genetic modification, cybernetics, bio-manipulation, and *Red Rising*-style body sculpting produce the classic World of Darkness tropes. Technology also lets hunters operate at the same level as the splats *without changing who they are*.

**Tao is real and older than the tech.** Tao is a real mystical force that predates the mod ecosystem. It can be interwoven with technology. Mages are pure Tao users who augment their magic with tech. Hunters and changelings may use Tao indirectly or at a lower level.

**[OPEN]** Whether vampires or werewolves can learn or use Tao at all. The GM described mages as pure users and hunters/changelings as indirect/low-level users, and did not address the other two splats.

### Becoming a splat

- It can be a **choice** (buying the mod, undergoing the procedure) or **something done to you** (corporate program, experiment, conversion).
- Methods: genetic modification, tech implants, body mods, body sculpting, bio-manipulation.
- **Vampirism is transmissible** (see section 2).
- **Irreversible**, with one exception: cyborg implants can be removed. Body sculpting and bio-manipulation cannot be undone.

### Who owns the tech

A mix. There are **corporate** options, **black market** options, and **DIY**. No single actor controls the mod ecosystem.

### The pitch

> Scrape it out in a city full of intrigue, politics, danger, and violence — to fix the city, or take it over.

This is a **sandbox**. The players decide what happens. The PCs **start at the bottom** with no institutional standing; the "fix it or take it" fork is left to play, not pre-decided.

## 2. The Splats

All five classic splats are in. None are merged, dropped, or replaced. Names are **[OPEN]** (must read clearly as the classic counterpart).

### Rarity and power

- All splats are **rare**. The average person may have some work done, but extreme transformation is rare, and Tao use is rare.
- **No intended power or scope asymmetry** between splats. The GM stated that power balance "won't come into play in the systems I am considering." The builder should not engineer a hierarchy (e.g., "hunters weaker but more numerous") into the fiction.

### Vampire

- **Origin:** Transmissible nanites from a hacked or failed corporate experiment. The corporation that created the nanites **no longer exists**. The outbreak is roughly **a century old**.
- **Sustenance:** They need blood or iron, because the nanites replicate using iron.
- **Powers:** The classic vampire package (strength, longevity, regeneration, etc.).
- **Immortality:** They live forever because of nanite replication — but it is a **ship-of-Theseus** situation; over time the body is progressively nanite-rebuilt.
- **Weaknesses:** **Electromagnetic** attacks/effects, and a **master "node" (core) of nanites** that, if destroyed, kills the vampire. These replace the classic sun/fire/stake weaknesses — the GM specified "EM or destroy the core."
- **Transmission:** Person-to-person via the nanites. **[OPEN]** Exact vector (bite, blood, direct nanite transfer) was not specified.
- **How the corps see them:** As a **disease** that occasionally flares up and every so often produces a clan noteworthy enough to justify wiping it out.
- **Secret:** The upstart corporation (section 3) is **vampire-controlled**. Vampires with power or sway know this. Street-level and newly made vampires do not.

### Werewolf

- **Origin:** A mix of bio-sculpt and cybernetics.
- **Shift:** Triggered by a **chemical trigger**, in the style of Bane from the Batman comics.
- **Supply chain:** The trigger is manufactured by **one of the three major corporations**, which makes it for a **different purpose** (**[OPEN]** — the GM has not yet decided the trigger's actual corporate use). A **syndicate** — a separate entity from the corp — sells the trigger to the packs.
- **Packs:** Werewolves organize in packs that function like **gangs**.

### Mage

- **Nature:** Pure Tao users who use technology to augment their magic. Mages are the one splat whose power source is not manufactured — Tao predates the mods.
- **Becoming one:** A **secret society** identifies talented people and brings them into the fold.
- **Position:** The Tao secret society **controls one of the three major corporations** (**[OPEN]** which one). The other corporations have a few rebel Tao users and **anti-Tao solutions** of their own.
- The society is secret, and the corporations prefer it that way.

### Changeling

- **Nature:** A mix of Tao and tech at the body level. Body mods allow, in some cases, **Face Dancer**-style (Dune) shapeshifting/impersonation.
- **Role:** Corporate special units, crime-syndicate assassins, spies, and the like.
- **Ownership:** Changelings are **owned** — made and controlled by whoever built them, with **kill switches and trackers**.
- **The escaped:** Some have gotten out and removed their kill switches and trackers. They live in constant danger for having done so and **form groups with other escapees**. Escaped changeling society is **purely underground** with no political pull.

### Hunter

- **Definition:** A deliberately **broad** splat meant to capture whatever players can think up that is **human** and doesn't fit the classic creatures — bounty hunters, anti-cybernetic extremists, corporate muscle, etc.
- **Tech line:** Hunters use various levels of tech — augments, weapon systems, battle suits — and use **cybernetics only as replacements for lost body parts** (arm, leg, eyes, etc.). **Body sculpting and bio-manipulation are not on the table** for hunters. This is the line between "still human" and "splat."
- **Tao:** May use it indirectly or at a low level.
- **Public role:** When extreme mods go "out of control" in public, **hunters are typically blamed** for the destruction — a cover for the secret splats.

## 3. The World

### Scale

- A **megacity with sprawl**. Invented, heavily *Night City*-inspired. **The city is the story.**
- **[OPEN]** City name.
- **[OPEN]** Whether anything outside the city matters (other cities, wasteland, orbital/off-world). Not discussed; treat the city as the whole world for play purposes unless the GM says otherwise.

### Power structure

**Three major corporations and one upstart.**

- **The Big Three** each have fingers in multiple pies, with something each is particularly known for — the GM suggested **weapons, bio, and cybernetics** as the three specialties ("maybe"). They have been in a **stalemate**, with basically stable agreements to keep things civil between them.
  - One of the three **manufactures the werewolf trigger** (for an undetermined purpose).
  - One of the three is **controlled by the Tao secret society**.
  - The third — neither the trigger manufacturer nor the Tao-controlled corp — **owns the secret work camp** where the campaign opens.
  - **[OPEN]** Which specialty (weapons / bio / cybernetics) maps to which of these three roles. Names of all three.
- **The Upstart** has multiple income streams to a lesser extent, and is best known for **security, crisis management, and hacking**. It is **secretly vampire-controlled**. It is a credible challenger but still too weak to easily take on one of the three one-on-one. **[OPEN]** Name.
- **The syndicate** that resells the werewolf trigger is a separate entity from the corps. **[OPEN]** Name, scope, and whether it has other significant business.
- **City government** exists but is **deeply corrupt**. Corporate security forces are exactly as legal as the city police.
- The **Tao society** wields power through its corp but is otherwise hidden. **Escaped changeling cells** are purely underground.

### Haves and have-nots

- There is the **beginning of a formal caste system**. It started as economic but is slowly becoming formalized, in part because of unequal access to cybernetics, gene mods, and sculpting.
- There is some mobility, but the lower you are, the harder it is to climb.
- The PCs start at the bottom.
- **[OPEN]** Whether castes have names, legal status, or visible markers.

### The Masquerade

- **Secret:** Vampires, werewolves, the Tao society, and escaped changeling society.
- **Public:** People know **extreme modding exists** — usually seen as bodyguards, sculpted entertainers, and arena fighters.
- **The cover:** Public incidents caused by the secret splats are blamed on hunters.
- **Who keeps it:** A mix. The **corporations** are largely responsible for keeping vampires and werewolves hidden. The **Tao society** keeps itself secret, and the corps prefer that.
- **[OPEN]** Whether the public knows Tao/magic exists at all (as distinct from the *society*). Not explicitly answered; the GM's framing implies it is hidden.

### Timeline (rough)

- Tao existed before mods.
- Mods: roughly **200 years** old.
- Vampire outbreak: roughly **100 years** old.
- The corporation that created the nanites no longer exists.
- Present day: Big Three stalemate under strain from the upstart (see section 5).

## 4. Theme and Tone

### Central themes (all four confirmed by the GM)

1. **The price of climbing when the ladder is made of your own body** — mobility through modification, and what it costs.
2. **Who counts as human when the mods decide your caste.**
3. **Ownership versus freedom** — changelings with kill switches, packs dependent on a syndicate's trigger, vampires bound to nanites.
4. **The city as something worth saving versus something worth seizing.**

The GM confirmed all four when offered; none was ranked as secondary.

### Darkness and hope

- **Bittersweet.** Real change is possible but always costs something.
- **As dark as any World of Darkness setting.**
- **Violent.** Violence is part of the appeal; body horror from nanite vampires and chemical werewolves is a feature, not something to keep at arm's length.

### Tone of play

- A **mix of heist-and-intrigue and action-forward**, depending on what the party is trying to accomplish. Neither mode is the "failure state."
- **Comedy** within the group and with some NPCs, but the setting is generally **serious**.

## 5. The Driving Conflict

### The spine

**The upstart corporation is destabilizing the status quo through its ruthlessness.** The Big Three had stable agreements keeping things civil; the upstart is breaking that. The destabilization creates a **sense of opportunity for every faction — including the secret ones** — which drives the year's conflict, politics, and intrigue.

### The upstart's goal

**Power.** That could mean an equal fourth seat, replacing one of the three, or eventually subjugating all three. Whichever it is, they must first **weaken the current three**. The GM is deliberately leaving the specific end-state loose.

### The secret

The upstart is **vampire-run**. Within vampire society, anyone with power or sway knows; street-level and newly made vampires don't. To the corporations, vampires are still just a recurring disease. **[OPEN]** What it costs, and to whom, if this becomes known — not discussed.

### The clock

- **The one confirmed clock:** The conflict between the Big Three and the upstart escalates until the **public knows there is a war going on** rather than a secret one.
- Other clocks (elections, mergers, caste deadlines, turf wars) are expected but **[OPEN]** — the GM found it hard to define them at this stage.

### The inciting incident

The upstart strikes a **secret work camp** belonging to the third corporation (the one that neither makes the trigger nor is Tao-controlled).

- The camp is **debt labor and penal**. It produces something generic; the camp itself is not the mystery.
- The upstart's real objective is to **extract one specific person**. The general breakout is cover for that person's importance.
- The PCs escape in the breakout.

**The important person:** The PCs' **crew leader** inside the camp. They are a **vampire spy** who was caught leaving one of the corporations' research facilities and played it off as a random street thug paid by a gang to grab tech. Inside the camp they helped acclimate the PCs and protected them. The party knows this person — but does not know why they matter or the full picture. After the escape, this person **directs the PCs toward a fence** who can help them get set up on the outside.

**[OPEN]** Which corporation's research facility the spy was caught at, and what they were after. Whether the fence has any connection to the upstart.

## 6. The Player Group

### Shape

- A **mixed-splat crew from session one.** All five splats may be present side by side.
- **How much the PCs know about each other's secrets is left to the players.**
- **[OPEN]** No in-fiction reason has been set for why a debt/penal camp happened to hold a vampire, a werewolf, a mage, a changeling, and a hunter on one crew. The GM has left this to the players; the builder should not invent a "the camp was for people like them" explanation unless the GM confirms it.

### Why they're together

- They were **all on the same work crew** in the camp.
- Before the camp, they were **freelance low-level criminals who saw something they shouldn't have** — that is *why* they were sent to the camp, whether or not they understood what they saw.
- **What they saw is a character-creation question** for the players to answer, not a pre-written secret. **[OPEN]** Whether it should connect to the spine (the upstart, the trigger, the Tao society) is unresolved.

### Standing after the breakout

- **At the bottom.** No institutional standing.
- They are on the camp corporation's **list of escapees**, but were not important enough to warrant a bounty or targeted recapture.
- Their **one day-one contact** is the fence the crew leader points them to.
