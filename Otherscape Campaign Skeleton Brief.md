# Otherscape Campaign Skeleton Brief

**Campaign:** Neon Black (working title)
**Engine:** :Otherscape (Mist Engine), per *Metro:Otherscape Core Book* (2024) and *Tokyo:Otherscape Setting Book*
**Canon input:** `Shared Lore Bible.md` — fixed setting canon. Do not contradict it. Where this brief and the Bible seem to disagree, the Bible wins and the disagreement is a bug in this brief.
**Audience:** a build agent producing a pilot campaign skeleton.

Page references are to the Core Book unless marked *Tokyo*.

---

## 0. Standing Rules for the Build Agent

These were stated by the GM during the interview and apply to every section below.

1. **Custom in addition to existing.** Wherever the book offers a kit, trope, Special, crew kit, or Challenge that fits, *offer it* — and *also* write a setting-specific custom version. Never substitute one for the other. This applies to theme kits, crew kits, character tropes, Megacity Specials, Crew Theme Specials, and Challenges.
2. **No GM advice.** The GM is an experienced MC (Legend in the Mist background). Do not restate the book's advice on running scenes, presenting hard choices, pacing, or safety tools. Produce content, not coaching.
3. **Flag, don't resolve.** Where the book's rules and the Bible's canon conflict, mark it **[RULES CONFLICT]** and present the options; do not silently pick one. Section 10 collects the ones already found.
4. **Interviewer's choices are labeled.** Where the GM deferred a decision to the interviewer, the recommendation is marked **[INTERVIEWER'S CHOICE]** and the GM may overrule it. Section 11 collects them.
5. **Invent what you were told to invent, nothing else.** The GM authorized the build agent to invent: names (§9.4), Key Player twists (§6.4), and district content via the Megacity Generator (§6.2). Anything else not in the Bible or this brief is an open question (§12).
6. **Vocabulary.** Use the book's terms: *Series* (not season), *Job* (not case), *Crew theme* (not crew type), *Challenge* (not threat — a Threat is one line inside a Challenge profile), *MC*, *Megacity*, *district story tag*, *Key Player*, *vector*, *core moment*, *Essence*, *Decay*, *Identity / Ritual / Itch*.

## 1. Series Concept (Series Step 1, p. 146–149)

### 1.1 Crew concept

Per the Bible: freelance low-level criminals who saw something they shouldn't, sent to a secret debt-labor/penal work camp, all on the same work crew, who escape when the upstart corporation strikes the camp to extract their crew leader (a vampire spy). They start at the bottom, on an escapee list with no bounty, with one contact (a fence the crew leader points them to). Sandbox: fix the city or take it over.

### 1.2 Series pole (p. 281)

**Start Paycheck, pivot to Misfits.** Early jobs are episodic and personal; the build agent should plant, inside the first two jobs, the twist that lets the table pivot to a Misfits structure against Key Players (the upstart and/or the camp-owning corp). The book's own example of this pivot (p. 281) is the pattern.

### 1.3 Thematic pillars **[INTERVIEWER'S CHOICE — GM said "pick how the themes map best"]**

The Bible's four themes mapped to the book's pillars (p. 146):

| Bible theme | Primary pillar | Secondary pillar |
|---|---|---|
| The price of climbing when the ladder is your own body | Transhumanism | Technological Overdrive / Moral Bankruptcy |
| Who counts as human when the mods decide your caste | Transhumanism | Oligarchic Corruption |
| Ownership vs. freedom (kill switches, trigger dependency, nanite bondage) | Oligarchic Corruption | Gangs, Cults & Counterculture |
| The city as something worth saving vs. seizing | Oligarchic Corruption | Gangs, Cults & Counterculture |

**Emphasize:** Transhumanism, Oligarchic Corruption, Gangs/Cults/Counterculture. **Present but not central:** Technological Overdrive, Sprawling Cyberspace (cyberspace runs as written, §6.5, but is not a pillar). **Sidelined:** Cities in Ruins. **Reinterpreted:** *Mythology Weaponized* becomes **Tao Weaponized** — see §3.

### 1.4 Tone (from the Bible)

Bittersweet; as dark as any World of Darkness setting; violence and body horror are features; mix of heist/intrigue and action-forward; comedy inside the crew and with some NPCs, otherwise serious.

## 2. Theme Framework and Splat Mapping (p. 74–75, 134–141, 175–177, 196–251)

### 2.1 Mapping model: Hybrid

Each splat is a **pairing**: a required primary theme in one category plus an optional secondary theme in another, expressed through theme kits. Splat identity lives in theme kits, character tropes, Megacity-style Specials, and Essence guidance — **not** in the Mythos category as such.

| Splat (name TBD, §9.4) | Required primary | Optional secondary | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vampire | **Noise** (Cutting Edge — nanites) | Mythos (Exposure) or further Noise | Tech-made per canon |
| Werewolf | **Noise** (Augmentation — bio-sculpt + cybernetics) | Noise (trigger dependency) or Self (pack Affiliation) | Tech-made per canon |
| Changeling | **Noise** (Augmentation — body-level mods) | **Mythos** (Exposure or Esoterica — the Tao side) | Canon: "Tao and tech at the body level"; leans Transhuman |
| Mage | **Mythos** (Esoterica — Tao) | Noise (tech that augments the magic) | Canon: pure Tao users who augment with tech |
| Hunter | **Self** (Expertise / Assets / Affiliation) | Noise (Cutting Edge gear; Augmentation only as replacement) | Canon: no sculpting or bio-manipulation |

### 2.2 Essence minimums **[INTERVIEWER'S CHOICE — GM deferred]**

Option (ii) *Minimum*: a splat requires at least one theme of its primary category; Essence is otherwise free.

- Vampire, werewolf, changeling: ≥1 Noise theme.
- Mage: ≥1 Mythos theme.
- Hunter: **no Augmentation theme** unless the kit is framed as a replacement limb/organ/eye; no bio-sculpt or bio-manipulation content in any hunter theme.
- Expect most starting PCs (one splat theme + three Self) to be **Nexus**; a pure-Noise vampire would be **Singularity**; a pure-Mythos mage **Avatar** (single Source) or **Conduit** — see §3.3 for what that means under Tao.

### 2.3 Hunters and Essence (GM decision)

Design hunter kits and tropes **toward Real (Self only) and Cyborg (Self + Noise)** on purpose, so hunters mechanically embody the "just human" question. The Real Essence Special (roll with Self to uphold an Identity, p. 135) and the Cyborg Special (resist intangible mythical effects) are the intended payoffs.

### 2.4 Persistence of the splat condition (GM: "the system that doesn't contradict canon")

Canon: vampirism, sculpting, and bio-manipulation are irreversible; only cyborg implants can be removed. Therefore:

- The **power** lives in the theme kit and can Decay and be replaced like any theme.
- The **condition** persists regardless. A vampire whose nanite theme Decays to replacement is still a vampire (still needs blood/iron, still EM-vulnerable, still has a master node). Model this as **narrative fact**, with optional **Megacity-style PC Specials** (Tokyo p. 72–74 pattern: chosen instead of a theme upgrade, prerequisite required, recorded on the character card, survives theme replacement, removed only for narrative reasons for an Evolution point) carrying the inescapable mechanical hooks.
- Exception: a hunter's replacement cybernetics *can* be removed, consistent with canon.

### 2.5 Per-splat build spec

For **each** splat, deliver: (a) the existing kits recommended, (b) at least one custom theme kit in the book's card format, (c) at least one custom character trope (three fixed kits + one choice, with a Loadout line — p. 178 format), (d) candidate Megacity-style PC Specials for the persistent condition, (e) the kit-fixed motivation. Custom kits must follow the book's format: **title tag + 9 further power tags (10 total), 4 weakness tags, one Identity / Ritual / Itch**, and answer the themebook's power/weakness tag questions.

**Vampire**
- Canon: transmissible nanites from a hacked/failed experiment by a corp that no longer exists; ~100 years old; need blood or iron (nanites replicate with iron); classic power package; immortal via replication, ship-of-Theseus body; weaknesses are **EM** and the **master node/core** (destroy it to kill) — these *replace* sun/fire/stake; the upstart corp is secretly vampire-run (known to vampires with power, not to street/new vampires); corps regard vampirism as a recurring disease and occasionally wipe out a noteworthy clan.
- Existing kits: **Self-Healing** (Cutting Edge, p. 243: *toxic blood nanites, self-resuscitating, sacrifice a limb*; weaknesses *sought-after blood, requires nutrients*); **Cloud of Nanites** (Cutting Edge, p. 242: weaknesses *rogue nanite contamination, wreaks havoc on my DNA, strong magnetic fields*). **Changeling**

- Canon: a deliberately broad human splat — bounty hunters, anti-cybernetic extremists, corporate muscle, anything human that isn't one of the other four; gear, augments, weapon systems, battle suits; cybernetics **only as replacements** for lost parts; no sculpting or bio-manipulation; may use Tao indirectly/low-level; hunters are publicly blamed for the damage the secret splats cause.

## 3. Tao as the Only Source (§ "Mythology Weaponized", p. 20–21, 32–33) **[RULES CONFLICT — see §10.1]**

### 3.1 Canon decisions

- **Tao is the only mystical force. There are no legend-Mythoi.** No gods, monsters, heroes, or places out of story living in the Otherscape substratum.
- **Tao is impersonal** — a force with disciplines, no will or agenda.
- **Tao can produce beings.** Companion themes (familiars, constructs, spirits) are allowed under Tao.
- Tao predates the mods (~200 years of mods; ~100 years since the vampire outbreak; Tao older than both).
- Tao can be interwoven with technology (caster-shell style).

### 3.2 Consequences for Mythos themes

- All four Mythos theme types remain available: **Artifact** (a Tao-imbued item), **Companion** (a Tao-made being), **Esoterica** (a Tao discipline — the society's practices), **Exposure** (Tao-touched body/being; the changeling's Tao half).
- **Sources** are reinterpreted as *Tao gateways*: a practice, an item, a place where Tao concentrates (Thin Places may exist as places where Tao is dense; whether they do is **[OPEN]**), a Tao-made being. Avatars as *incarnations of a legend* do not exist; whether a person can be a Tao "Avatar" in the mechanical sense (Essence) is addressed in §3.3.
- **Rituals** cannot be "re-enact your legend." Under impersonal Tao they are *disciplines, prices, and conditions*: practices to maintain, sacrifices the discipline demands, conditions under which Tao flows (the book's own examples "I must gather obscure ingredients," "I must replenish the wand's power," "I must practice the visceral ways of magic" already fit). The build agent should write kit-fixed Rituals in that register.
- Strip or reinterpret: **Conjurations** (no legend to conjure from — Companion beings are Tao constructs instead), **Source-hunting jobs** (become Tao-artifact/Tao-site acquisition), **Exorcism / Ritual Performance jobs** (only if reinterpreted as Tao operations; not in the pilot — §7.3), the **Source-Touched & Conjurations** Challenge category (p. 315–318 — reuse only after re-flavoring as Tao-touched), and the **Mythoi pillar** results of the Megacity Generator (§6.2).

### 3.3 Essence under Tao

- **Avatar** (only Mythos, same Source) and **Conduit** (only Mythos, different Sources) remain mechanically valid. Narratively, "same Source" = one Tao discipline/gateway; "different Sources" = multiple Tao gateways. The Avatar's *Agenda* replaces Rituals; under impersonal Tao the Agenda is a self-imposed law, not a legend's demand. Flag any place the build agent finds this strained.

## 4. Noise, the Masquerade, and Humanity (p. 22, 74, 122, 134–139, 176)

### 4.1 Cosmological Noise vs. the Masquerade (GM: "in-between")

Keep the book's **Noise** (sensory oversaturation → public apathy) *as the reason the Masquerade is cheap to maintain*, layered with active **corporate cleanup** and each secret group's self-protection. Canon Masquerade: vampires, werewolves, the Tao society, and escaped-changeling society are secret; the public knows extreme modding exists (bodyguards, sculpted entertainers, arena fighters); public incidents by the secret splats are blamed on hunters; corps are largely responsible for hiding vampires and werewolves; the Tao society hides itself and the corps prefer it. Whether the public knows *Tao* exists (as distinct from the society) is **[OPEN]**.

### 4.2 Losing yourself: Decay, Itch, Ritual

Otherscape has no Humanity meter and Noise is not one. "Losing yourself to power" is modeled by **Decay + Essence drift**: resisting an Itch or neglecting a Ritual marks Decay; three marks replace the theme; category shifts change Essence. Surrendering to the Itch *keeps* the theme — the inverse of WoD Humanity. The build agent should write splat Itches so that indulging them is the pull toward the ownership/dependency theme (feed, trigger, become someone else) and weakness tags carry the cost.

### 4.3 Humanity: narrative only (GM decision)

No added track. Self themes ("what you could lose should you pursue magic or technology at the expense of your personal life," p. 176) and the Real / Singularity poles are the only mechanical expression. The build agent must **not** invent a humanity mechanic.

### 4.4 Self kits (GM: "create custom kits whenever possible")

Write custom Self kits for the Bible's institutions, in addition to recommending existing ones. Candidates from the Bible: werewolf pack (Affiliation); escaped-changeling cell (Affiliation); Tao society initiate (Affiliation); corporate citizenship in each of the Big Three and the upstart (Affiliation); the syndicate (Affiliation); the work camp (Troubled Past); caste registration / being sorted down the ladder (Troubled Past or Affiliation); "saw something I shouldn't" (Troubled Past — content left to character creation); the fence's network (Assets or Affiliation); climbing the caste ladder (Horizon); fix the city / take the city (Horizon). Crew relationship tags stay per the book (p. 177 list).

## 7. Jobs (Handling Jobs, p. 284–293)

### 7.1 Session one: the breakout (canon)

- Open **in medias res inside the camp** during the upstart's strike; use **Flashbacks** to establish the crew (GM decision, option (a)).
- Facts: camp is debt-labor and penal, secret, produces something generic, owned by Corp C. The upstart's true objective is to extract the PCs' **crew leader** — a vampire spy caught leaving a corp research facility (which corp: **[OPEN]**), who passed as a street thug hired by a gang to grab tech, and who acclimated and protected the PCs inside. The general breakout is cover. The PCs know this person but not why they matter. Afterward the crew leader directs them to a **fence** who can set them up outside.
- Aftermath facts: PCs are on Corp C's escapee list, not important enough for a bounty; they were in the camp *because* of what they saw (what: **character-creation question for the players**; whether it ties to the spine: **[OPEN]**).
- The session ends with **crew theme creation** (§5.1).
- Deliver: scenes with set pieces, Challenges (camp security, the strike force, the extraction), vectors (the upstart's strike lead; Corp C's camp authority; the crew leader), core moments, and denouement questions. The starter job's prison-complex acts (p. 346–353) are a structural reference only; do not copy its Mythos content.

### 7.2 Two multi-session jobs (GM decision)

- **Job 1: Investigation.** **Job 2: Acquisition.** Each multi-session (the four-act starter job is the scale reference).
- Both begin **Paycheck** (hired through the fence or the fence's network) and at least one carries the **twist that enables the Misfits pivot** (§1.2).
- Use the book's job structure: hooks → goal → scenes with Challenges → climax → denouement/aftermath; vectors with want/push/face; core moments; complications from the job-type lists (p. 285–287). Jobs must tie to PC themes (Identity/Ritual/Itch challenges) and to the Key Players.
- Job types **not** in the pilot: Exorcism, Ritual Performance, Source-hunting (all need Tao reinterpretation; deferred).

### 7.3 The spine and the clock

- Spine (canon): the upstart destabilizes the Big Three's civil stalemate through ruthlessness; every faction, including the secret ones, smells opportunity.
- The one confirmed clock (canon): the secret war escalates until the **public knows it is a war**.
- **[INTERVIEWER'S CHOICE — GM: "go with what makes sense"]**: write this as a **series-level Countdown Challenge** with a **progress Limit at tier 6** (the hardest, p. 298), advanced by job aftermaths and by Consequences the build agent specifies; when it maxes, the war is public. Other clocks are **[OPEN]** — the build agent may propose job-level Countdown Challenges but must not add series-level ones.

The **Mist Engine HUD** module (`mist-hud`) supports that Otherscape mode (Self/Mythos/Noise dice, Loadout tags, Tracked Outcomes). Custom Themebooks are created as Items in that system.
- **Sqyre** installs modules by name from public listings or **by manifest URL**; premium/Patreon modules need access. No zip upload is documented. So the eventual custom content must be packaged as a Foundry **module** (`module.json` manifest + compendium packs) hosted at a stable URL (e.g., a GitHub release).
- Therefore the Markdown frontmatter above should map cleanly onto that system's data types: **themebook / themekit → Item**, **Challenge → Actor (threat/danger)**, **districts, Key Players, jobs, scenes → JournalEntry pages**, **loadout items → Item**. Verify field names against the `city-of-mist` repository before conversion; that verification is **[OPEN]** and out of scope for the skeleton itself.
- Son of Oak has publicly recruited developers to build official Foundry support for all three games (City of Mist, :Otherscape, Legend in the Mist); no release date is known. Re-check before packaging.

## 10. Rules-vs-Canon Conflicts (do not resolve silently)

1. **Tao-only cosmology vs. "Mythology Weaponized."** The book's central pillar and much of its content (Mythoi, Conjurations, Avatars, Source-hunting, Source-Touched Challenges, the Generator's Mythoi pillar) assume legend-Mythoi. Canon removes them. Resolution adopted: reinterpret as Tao (§3.2); mark each reinterpretation in the output.
2. **Tech-made splats in Noise vs. the Exposure themebook's vampire example** (p. 233, Ritual "(Vampire) I must consume the blood of the living"). Resolution adopted: Hybrid mapping (§2.1). Note in the vampire file that the book's default would have been Exposure.
3. **Rituals as legend re-enactment vs. impersonal Tao.** Resolution adopted: Rituals as disciplines/prices/conditions (§3.2). Flag any kit where this reads strained.
4. **Masquerade vs. "supernatural exists openly but nobody cares"** (p. 22). Resolution adopted: in-between (§4.1).
5. **Crew exists before play vs. crew forms in session one** (p. 148, 154). Resolution adopted: nascent crew theme at end of session one (§5.1).
6. **Dropping the crew into a job without prep** (p. 291 warning). GM accepts; open in medias res with Flashbacks (§7.1).
7. **Irreversible splat condition vs. themes that can Decay away.** Resolution adopted: condition persists as narrative fact + optional Specials (§2.4).
8. **Avatar/Conduit Essence under impersonal Tao** (§3.3) — mechanically valid, narratively re-based; flag if strained.
