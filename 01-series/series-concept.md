# Series Concept

The high concept of the Series, in the shape of the book's Series Step 1 (Core p. 146–149): a crew concept, a Series pole, and a Megacity. The Megacity is [[palisade|Palisade]]; the pole is Paycheck pivoting to Misfits; the crew is below.

## Crew concept

Five people who were **freelance low-level criminals who saw something they shouldn't have** and were sent, for it, to a secret debt-labor and penal work camp — [[names|Reconciliation Facility 4]], which its inmates call **the Ledger** — where they were put on the same work crew (Bible §6). What each of them saw is a character-creation question the players answer, not a pre-written secret; whether it ties to the Series spine is **[OPEN]** (OQ-12).

They escape when [[upstart|Continuity Risk & Response]] strikes the Ledger. The strike is a general breakout as cover for one extraction: the crew's leader inside the camp, **Tomas Adair, "Rook"** ([[tomas-adair]]), who acclimated them and protected them and who, unknown to them, is a Bloodware spy caught leaving one of the corporations' research facilities — which one, and after what, is **[OPEN]** (OQ-10) — who passed himself off as a street thug paid by a gang to grab tech (Bible §5). The crew knows Rook. They do not know why he matters. After the breakout he points them at a fence — **Marisol Okonkwo, "Tally"** ([[marisol-okonkwo]]) — and that referral is their single day-one contact (Bible §6).

Where they stand afterward: **at the bottom.** No institutional standing; on [[corp-c|Amalgamated Prosthetic & Interface]]'s escapee list, but not worth a bounty or a recapture team (Bible §6). Stock, or Patched at best, in the caste slang the city uses ([[names]] §4). Their bond is the work crew: they already know each other, which satisfies the book's assumption that a crew "already know each other, at least tangentially" (Core p. 147), and the crew theme is created at the end of session one as a nascent theme (Brief §5.1; [[conflict-register]] CR-5).

A mixed crew from session one: all five — Bloodware, Howlers, Casters, Doppels, Baselines — may sit side by side (Bible §6). How much they know about one another's secrets is the players' business (OQ-19). Nobody in this vault explains why the Ledger happened to hold all five on one crew (OQ-13).

In the book's terms the nearest crew concepts are **Wanted** ("renegades, escaping the law, a corp, or a crime syndicate … you come together for mutual protection", Core p. 149) and **Found Family** (Core p. 148) — with the Bible's caveat that the crew is *not* hunted hard enough to be Wanted in the book's sense (Brief §5.2). The custom crew kits that answer this are WP3's ([[existing-crew-kits]] and its custom companions).

## The sandbox statement

> Scrape it out in a city full of intrigue, politics, danger, and violence — to fix the city, or take it over. (Bible §1)

**The players decide.** The fork — fix Palisade or seize it — is left to play and is never pre-decided by any file in this vault (Bible §1). Every Key Player, district, and job is built so that both answers stay open: a corp can be broken or bought, a district can be protected or ruled, the fence's network can become a conscience or a syndicate. The fourth theme of the Bible (§4) — *the city as something worth saving versus something worth seizing* — is the Series' question, not its answer.

## Series pole: Paycheck → Misfits

The book maps every Series between two poles (Core p. 281): a **Paycheck Series** is episodic and character-driven, jobs "don't really matter" beyond what they do to each PC; a **Misfits Series** is story-driven, the crew against "constant and repeated opposition" from a Key Player they have come to agree "needs to be taken down."

This Series **starts Paycheck and pivots to Misfits** (Brief §1.2). The book's own example of the pivot is the pattern: a crew delivers a cybernetic implant, watches the exec they delivered it to commit a murder while testing it, goes home paid — and then the table decides the exec's corp is the villain, the corp is fleshed out as a Key Player, and the Series becomes about taking it down (Core p. 281).

### The pivot mechanism

1. **Session one is the breakout** (the `07-jobs/00-breakout/` job, pending WP7a): in medias res inside the Ledger, Flashbacks for who the crew were, the extraction of Rook as a core moment the PCs *witness* without understanding, the referral to Tally, the crew theme. It is not a job anyone hired them for; it is the thing that happened to them.
2. **Job 1 (Investigation) and Job 2 (Acquisition) are Paycheck** — hired through Tally or her network, personal, episodic (Brief §7.2). Each PC's Identity, Ritual, or Itch gets tested by the work, not by the plot.
3. **Inside those two jobs the build plants the twist** that lets the table pivot (Brief §1.2): the discovery that a job's real employer, target, or victim is a Key Player the crew already has a personal reason to hate — Continuity, which used them as cover and took the man who protected them, or AP&I, which owned the pit they climbed out of. Job 2 carries the twist explicitly (`twist_for_pivot: true`, Plan WP7c); Job 1 plants the thread Job 2 pulls.
4. **The table pivots when it wants to.** The pivot is a decision the players and MC make together, as the book describes it (Core p. 281) — never a scripted reveal that forces the Series onto rails. If the crew shrugs and takes the next paycheck, the Series stays Paycheck and the Key Players keep moving without them (see [[spine-and-clock]]).

What the pivot is *not*: it is not the revelation that Continuity is Bloodware-run. That secret is the Bible's (§5) and stays behind the Masquerade unless the GM decides otherwise (OQ-17). The crew can hate Continuity for what it did to them long before they know what it is.

## The Megacity as the whole world

Palisade is the world for play. "The city is the story" (Bible §3), and nothing outside it — other Megacities, wasteland, orbit — is defined. **[OPEN]** (OQ-7) Whether anything outside Palisade matters is the GM's; every file in this vault treats the sprawl's edge as the edge of the map, and a job that would leave the city is out of scope for the pilot.
