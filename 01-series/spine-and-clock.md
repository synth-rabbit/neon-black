# Spine and Clock

The one story the Series is built on, and the one clock the Bible confirms. Everything here is Bible §5 restated in the book's vocabulary; the Countdown Challenge itself is [[secret-war-goes-public]].

## The spine

**Continuity Risk & Response is destabilizing the status quo through its ruthlessness** (Bible §5). For as long as anyone on the street remembers, the Big Three — [[corp-a|Meliora Bioworks]], [[corp-b|Orison Defense Systems]], [[corp-c|Amalgamated Prosthetic & Interface]] — have been in a stalemate held together by "basically stable agreements to keep things civil between them" (Bible §3). [[upstart|Continuity]] is breaking those agreements: a security, crisis-management, and hacking firm with smaller fingers in many pies, a credible challenger, and still too weak to take on any one of the three alone (Bible §3).

**Continuity's goal is power.** An equal fourth seat, or one of the three chairs, or — eventually — all three subjugated. The Bible leaves the end-state loose on purpose (§5), and this vault does not pick one. What is fixed: whichever it is, **Continuity must first weaken the current three**, and it cannot do so in a straight fight. So it does what a security-and-crisis firm can do — provoke, expose, sabotage, insert itself as the cure for the emergencies it manufactures, and pick at the agreements until one of the three breaks them first.

**The destabilization is everyone's opportunity.** "The destabilization creates a sense of opportunity for every faction — including the secret ones — which drives the year's conflict, politics, and intrigue" (Bible §5). So each of the nine Key Players (Brief §6.4) is written by WP4 with a line on *what it wants from the crack*:

| Key Player | The stalemate held it… | The crack offers it… |
|---|---|---|
| Meliora Bioworks (corp-a) | …to its lane, with a trigger it makes for some other purpose (OQ-3) leaking to the street. | …a chance to renegotiate what the lanes are. |
| Orison Defense Systems (corp-b) | …quiet, which the Wuji behind it prefer (Bible §2). | …a war, which sells weapons — and a risk that quiet ends. |
| AP&I (corp-c) | …as the one with the Ledger and the mass-market ladder. | …cover for what it already does in the dark, and a grudge to settle over the Ledger. |
| Continuity (upstart) | …out. | …everything. |
| The Almoners (syndicate) | …to the Leash trade. | …corporate customers, or a corporate war to sell into. |
| The Chancery (government) | …bought by three parties who agreed on the price. | …four bidders. |
| The Wuji (tao-society) | …hidden inside a corporation that nobody examined. | …exposure, and the chance to move first. |
| The Run (packs) | …dependent, gang-sized, contained under the Wall. | …turf, if the corps are busy shooting each other. |
| The Cutloose (changeling-cells) | …hunted, underground, without pull. | …chaos to hide in — and buyers for what they know. |

These are one-line prompts for WP4's agendas, not canon. The Bible fixes only that every faction smells opportunity.

**The secret under the spine.** Continuity is Bloodware-run. Within Bloodware society, anyone with power or sway knows; street-level and newly made Bloodware do not; the corporations still see the Ferrante strain as a recurring disease that occasionally produces a clan worth wiping out (Bible §2, §5). **[OPEN]** (OQ-17) What it costs, and to whom, if this becomes known — not discussed by the GM, and not decided here. No file in `01-series/` or `05-megacity/` reveals it; the districts do not place Bloodware society anywhere visible (Plan WP1).

## The inciting incident

Continuity strikes the Ledger — [[coldwater-outfall|Reconciliation Facility 4]], AP&I's secret debt-labor and penal camp — to extract one person, [[tomas-adair|Tomas Adair, "Rook"]]. The general breakout is cover. The PCs escape in it (Bible §5). This is the Series' *now*; see [[timeline]].

Why it matters to the spine: the strike is the first act of the year that the Bible describes, and it is exactly the kind of act that the civil agreements exist to prevent — one corporate power striking another's secret facility. Whether AP&I ever admits the Ledger existed, whether Continuity is ever named, and what the Chancery is told, are the first questions the clock below asks.

## The one confirmed clock: the secret war goes public

> The conflict between the Big Three and the upstart escalates until the public knows there is a war going on rather than a secret one. (Bible §5)

**[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-5, interviewer's choice) This is written as a **series-level Countdown Challenge** — [[secret-war-goes-public]] — with a single progress Limit, `public-war`, at **tier 6**, "incredibly difficult" and the hardest the book allows (Core p. 298). It is advanced by job aftermaths and by Consequences the Challenge file specifies. When it maxes, the war is public. No other series-level clock may be added (Brief §7.3); other clocks — elections, mergers, caste deadlines, turf wars — are **[OPEN]** (OQ-9), and WP7 may propose job-level Countdown Challenges only.

### What kinds of events advance it

No specific future event is scripted here. The clock moves on *kinds* of events; the jobs (WP7) say which of their aftermaths count, and the Challenge file gives the tiers.

- **Job aftermaths.** What the crew leaves behind — a body that is obviously not a Baseline's work, a facility that is obviously not a gas leak, a recording, a witness, a name said aloud to the wrong person. Every job's `aftermath.md` (WP7) states whether it advances `public-war`, and by how much.
- **Public incidents blamed on Baselines that fail to hold.** The Masquerade's cover story is that extreme mods "out of control" in public are the work of Baselines (Bible §2 hunter, §3 Masquerade). Each time the cover is tried and *does not stick* — because the feeds got there before cleanup, because the Baseline named has an alibi, because what happened cannot be done by a man with a gun — the corporate war shows through.
- **Corporate security in open combat.** Corp security is exactly as legal as the police (Bible §3); the public expects to see it *policing*. When one corp's security exchanges fire with another's, or with Continuity's crisis-response teams, in a place with witnesses, the war is visible for as long as the footage lasts.
- **A civil agreement broken in the open.** One of the Big Three publicly withdraws from, or publicly accuses another of breaking, one of the agreements that keep things civil — and does not walk it back.
- **The Chancery forced to speak.** The city government has to say something — a statement, an emergency, a curfew — and what it says either names the war or is laughed at for not naming it.

### What does *not* advance it

Private violence, however extreme. The Noise is why the Masquerade is cheap (Brief §4.1): what nobody sees, nobody cares about. A dozen dead in a Fringe Zone with no feed and no witnesses is a rumor, and a rumor is tier 0.

### Clock arithmetic through Job 2

**[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-172, WP8) The three pilot jobs' notch tables, read against the Challenge's own Threats, so the arithmetic is one table. Each job's aftermath lists the events that add tiers; the rows in a table stack, so a job's ceiling is their sum; a quiet job adds nothing; *Cleanup Crews* may remove one tier once between any two jobs, never below the tier at which the last job ended.

| Job | Aftermath | Adds | Ceiling | Running maximum at the end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — [[breakout]] | [[breakout-aftermath]] | 0 — a Fringe Zone with no feed (BC-149); the one exception is the crew's to cause in a later job | 0 | **0** |
| 1 — [[investigation]] | [[investigation-aftermath]] | open combat between a cell and an Envelope Detail in public (+2); something no Baseline could have done, on a feed (+1); the room burning visibly in a residential district (+1) | 4 | **4** |
| 2 — [[acquisition]] | [[acquisition-aftermath]] | something no Baseline could have done, on a feed (+1); a cell and an Envelope Detail drawing on each other in public (+2); the two pages put on a feed (+1); a fight between two freelance crews adds nothing | 4 | **6 — the Challenge maxes** |

So a table that plays every job loud and is never cleaned up after can reach `public-war-6` at the end of Job 2 and the war is public before the pivot has been chosen — the countdown working as written; a table that plays carefully enters Job 3 at 0. Job-level countdowns ([[the-strike-hour]], [[the-consignment-window]], [[collection-run]]) never feed this clock. **[OPEN]** (OQ-56) whether the GM wants a per-job cap — for instance, no job adds more than 2 — so the clock cannot max before the pivot; no file assumes one.

### What becomes true when it maxes

Stated in full in [[secret-war-goes-public]]. In one line: **the corporate war is public; the Masquerade for the secret splats is not broken by it.** Palisade learns that the Big Three and Continuity are at war. Palisade does not learn what Continuity is, or that Howlers, Bloodware, the Wuji, or the Cutloose exist. The cover story changes shape — corporate war becomes the new thing incidents are blamed on — but the secrets the Bible lists as secret (§3) stay secret unless the GM says otherwise.
