# Tone

What Palisade feels like, in words the vault can reuse: for prose, for district story tags and motifs, and — later — for the image briefs WP6 writes from this file (Plan WP-I: "palette/tone words from `tone.md`"). The tone is the Bible's (§4) restated through Brief §1.4; the palette words are this package's.

## The four registers (Bible §4)

1. **Bittersweet.** Real change is possible, and it always costs something. Nothing in this Series is hopeless — the Bible forbids it ([[style-guide]] §4) — and nothing is free. A district saved is a district someone else lost. A rung climbed is a piece of yourself paid.
2. **As dark as any World of Darkness setting.** The corporations are not cartoon villains; they are institutions that "can't die, [have] no will of [their] own, and [are] too big to fight" (Core p. 282). The city government is bought. The camp exists. People are sorted by what was done to their bodies.
3. **Violent, and body-horror-forward.** Violence is part of the appeal. Body horror is a feature, not something held at arm's length: Bloodware rebuilt from the marrow out by nanites that eat iron; Howlers whose sculpt tears open on Leash; Doppels wearing a face that is not a mask; chrome that shows, on the Patched, because it was never meant to be seen. Write the wound. Write what the nanites do to a body over a hundred years.
4. **Heist-and-intrigue and action-forward, in a mix.** Neither mode is the failure state (Bible §4). A job can be a quiet week of surveillance and a forged permit or a running gunfight up a tier lift, and the Series does not prefer one. The crew decides what kind of job it is by how it approaches it.

**Comedy** lives inside the crew and with some NPCs — dry, bickering, the kind that comes from five people who shared a work block in the Ledger and know exactly how each other snores. The setting around them is serious. Tally is funny; AP&I is not.

## The influences, as texture (Bible stated influences)

- *Red Rising* — sculpted bodies as caste; fighting from the bottom; haves against tiers of have-nots. Palisade's Wall is this made into geography ([[palisade|Palisade]]).
- *Outlaw Star* — Tao as a real force woven into tech, caster-shell style; a mixed crew; a ship's-crew looseness between people who would not otherwise be in a room together.
- *Cyberpunk 2077* — corporate-city texture, chrome, and the way a corporation's logo is on everything you touch.

## Palette, lighting, rendering — words for image briefs

**[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-14) These words are the vault's visual register; WP6 fills the style preamble of `image-briefs.md` from them.

**Palette.** Neon over concrete: *sodium orange, arc-lamp white, bruise violet, coolant green, blood-black, wet asphalt, rust, oxidized copper, surgical teal, warranty-card cream* (the Fitted tiers), *bone and chrome* (the Sculpted). The lower the tier, the fewer the colors: the Crest is lit in white and gold; the Sinks are lit in whatever still works.

**Lighting.** *Rain-scattered, underlit, backlit by advertising, tier-shadowed* (the Wall throws a shadow that reaches the sprawl by mid-afternoon), *floodlit checkpoints, flickering, emergency-red, clinic-fluorescent, one working streetlamp*. Daylight exists on the Crest. Under the Wall it is a rumor.

**Rendering.** *Painterly concept art, grounded and worn rather than glossy, film grain, no photoreal faces, scuffed surfaces, visible seams on chrome, wet reflections, industrial scale* — the Wall should read as enormous in every establishing shot. Not clean, not chrome-perfect, not anime-bright.

**Motif words** (for district motifs and Key Player emblems): *ledger, tally marks, weigh-scale, leash, suture, seam, tier, lift-cage, wall, palisade stakes, greenhouse glass, prayer-bead cable, ampersand, kernel/seed, iron filings, flood line, pump-house, quarantine tape.*

**Negative list** (everything Plan WP-I forbids): no readable text, no real brands, no legend-Mythos iconography — no gods, angels, demons, dragons, no mythological creatures of any culture. Tao, when it must be shown, is *density* — heat-shimmer, iron filings aligning, a shadow that falls the wrong way — never a figure.

## What this campaign is not

- **Not epic, heroic, or chosen.** Nobody has a destiny. The crew starts at the bottom and stays there until they buy, steal, or bleed their way up (Bible §3, §6). The forbidden tone words are in [[style-guide]] §4.
- **Not a monster hunt.** There are no legend-Mythoi to hunt; Tao is a force, not a bestiary ([[pillars]]). "Monsters" in Palisade are people who bought or were sold a transformation.
- **Not a Masquerade drama in the classic sense.** The secret splats are secret because the Noise makes nobody care and the corps clean up the rest (Brief §4.1) — not because a hidden court enforces silence. The corporations keep Bloodware and Howlers hidden and the Wuji keep themselves hidden (Bible §3); a PC who shows what they are in public meets cleanup, a Baseline blamed on the feeds, and a city that will not believe them — until the war makes it look ([[spine-and-clock]]).
- **Not hopeless.** The Bible says real change is possible. A Series that ends with the city exactly as it began has failed the brief.
- **Not a humanity-meter game.** No track measures how human a PC still is (Brief §4.3). Self themes, Decay, and the Real / Singularity poles do that work.
- **Not a game about what the Ledger was for.** The camp is debt labor and penal; it produces something generic; "the camp itself is not the mystery" (Bible §5).
