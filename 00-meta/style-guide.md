# Style Guide

Binding on every package. Where this guide and `Neon Black Build Plan.md` Part A differ, Part A wins and the difference is a bug here.

## 1. The naming register

Every name in the vault must sound like it belongs on the same page as the books' Key Players. The register below is derived from the names actually printed in *Metro:Otherscape* (Core p. 47–56) and *Tokyo:Otherscape* (Tokyo p. 24–27, 150–228). Names go in [[names]]; nothing else may coin a name without adding a row there (Plan A.7).

### 1.1 Why the book's corporate names read cyberpunk

| Book name | What it is doing | Rule it gives us |
|---|---|---|
| **Epis International** (Core p. 47) | A coined root (*epis-*, knowledge; tagline "Know. It. All.") bolted to the blandest possible suffix. | **Coined root + bland suffix.** The root hints at the specialty or the sin; the suffix says "we are everywhere." |
| **New Michigan Motors & Machining — NM3** (Core p. 48) | A legacy place-name, two greasy industrial nouns, an ampersand, and a street acronym with a numeral. | **Legacy-industrial mouthful that collapses to an acronym.** Old money sounds like a mouthful; the street shortens it. |
| **KOCMOC** (Core p. 49) | A loanword (*космос*) rendered in Latin capitals so the logo *is* the name; origin story tied to a place and a regulatory gap. | **Loanword-as-logo, all caps.** One language borrowed from another signals a globalized economy. |
| **Cryocharm / CryoCharm Enterprises** (Core p. 55) | A specialty prefix (*cryo-*) fused to a soft consumer word (*charm*). The friendlier the name, the colder the company. | **Specialty prefix + warm consumer word.** Warmth in the name is a warning. |
| **Chimaerics** (Core p. 56) | A mythic root turned into an *-ics* discipline, so an ideology sounds like a science. | **Scientific-suffix coinage.** Movements and ideologies get *-ics*, *-ism*, *-ology*. |
| **Stratosphere Strategies** (Core p. 54) | Alliterative abstract nouns; a consultancy that tells you nothing and everything. | **Alliterative consultancy abstraction.** Security, crisis, and "solutions" firms hide behind abstractions. |
| **Taiyō Solartech** (Tokyo p. 26, 201) | A foreign word for the specialty plus a transparent English compound. | **Bilingual, transparent specialty.** |
| **Ishii-Harada, Sōma Medical Corporation, Kojima Security Tech, Shinogi Security** (Tokyo p. 26) | Family name + sector noun. | **Surname + sector.** Family corps sound like families. |
| **Santa Lucia Academy** (Core p. 51) | A saint's name + an institution type, hiding a cult. | **Respectable institution name over a secret.** |

Cross-cutting principles for corporations:

1. **Every corp has a street name.** NM3, "the Knot," Epis. If the formal name cannot be shortened to one or two syllables in a bar, it is wrong. Give both forms in [[names]].
2. **The specialty is legible** from a root, a prefix, or the sector noun. A reader should guess what the company sells before reading its entry.
3. **Mixed origins.** Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, Japanese, Chinese, Spanish, English surnames — the Megacity is global. Do not cluster every name in one language.
4. **Warmth is a tell.** Consumer-friendly names belong to the cruelest players (Cryocharm funds terrorists; Epis "really, truly don't care about you").
5. **No real brands, no real companies, no real people.**

### 1.2 Why the book's society and gang names read cyberpunk

| Book name | What it is doing | Rule it gives us |
|---|---|---|
| **The Gordian — "the Knot"** (Core p. 52) | Definite article + a single classical allusion; people are "careful about saying" the one-syllable street form. | **"The [allusion]" + a one-syllable street name.** Underground movements are named by allusion and spoken by nickname. |
| **Ghost Market** (Tokyo p. 159) | Two plain English nouns, concrete and spooky. | **Two plain words.** What outsiders call a secret thing is always plain. |
| **Tokoyo** ("eternal life", Tokyo p. 210) | One foreign word with a meaning, doubling as a respectable company name (Tokoyo Digital Think Tank). | **One meaningful word that works as a front.** Secret societies name themselves for what they want. |
| **Returned Sōma Clan** (Tokyo p. 192) | Participle + family name + "Clan": a lineage that came back. | **Participle names for the changed.** *Returned*, *Numbed*, *Touched*: a group named for what happened to it. |
| **Veera Bhangers** (Core p. 50) | A cultural word (*veera*, valor) + a slangy plural (*bhang* + *bangers*). | **Cultural word + slang plural.** Gangs are named the way members say it, in the plural. |
| **Oni Motorcycle Gangs, Neo-Samurai, Steam Punks, Iribe-gumi, Maikubi-kai** (Tokyo p. 27, 177–191, 220) | Plain descriptors, or a family name + a suffix meaning "group." | **Gangs are plural and plain.** |
| **Bureau of Onmyō, Nano-butai** (Tokyo p. 25) | Bureaucratic formal name with a clipped nickname. | **Government units: formal name + clipped nickname.** |
| **the Numbed, Cozies, "seeing the Eye," scribbing** (Core p. 57) | Street culture uses participles, diminutives, and verbs-turned-nouns. | **Slang is short, participial, a little contemptuous.** |

Cross-cutting principles for societies, gangs, and slang:

1. **Secret groups have two names**: what they call themselves (meaningful, often foreign, often one word) and what rumor calls them (plain, two words, English). Both go in [[names]].
2. **Gangs and street cultures are plural and self-named.** Nobody in a pack says "the werewolf community."
3. **Slang is contemptuous or bitter and never more than two syllables.** The people who coined it were not being kind.
4. **A name for the changed describes what was done to them** (participle) rather than what they are (noun).

### 1.3 People

Book NPCs carry realistic, globally mixed full names, sometimes with a handle: Skye Carlin, Bahram Tehrani, Raj Shaan, Delphine Archambault (Core p. 55–59), Kaneko Michi, Izumi Koji (Tokyo p. 192, 210). Rules: given name + surname from different or the same origins; a handle in quotation marks only if the street would actually use it; no puns that break the tone; no names that duplicate a Key Player or a book NPC.

### 1.4 Checklist for any new name (Plan A.7)

Before adding a row to [[names]], the name must pass all of these:

- [ ] It follows one of the patterns in 1.1–1.3 and the row says which.
- [ ] It has a "reads as" line: what a newcomer guesses it is, in one sentence.
- [ ] It has a street form (corps, government units, secret groups) or is already one syllable.
- [ ] It does not reuse a book name, a real brand, a real person, or a City of Mist / Otherscape game term.
- [ ] It does not assert canon the Bible leaves open (a founding date, a location, a specialty) — names may *resonate* with canon; they may not *add* to it.
- [ ] It is marked *proposal* and flagged `[BUILD CHOICE]`.

## 2. Tag voice (Plan A.5; Core p. 177, 192)

Model every tag on the book's own: *toxic blood nanites, self-resuscitating, sacrifice a limb, sought-after blood, requires nutrients* (Self-Healing, Core p. 243); *water cooler rumors, corporate logo tattoo, monitored by the company* (Corporate Citizenship, Core p. 177).

1. **Two to five words.** One-word tags are allowed only when the word is unmistakable in play (*hackable*, *squishy allies* is two).
2. **Lower-case except proper nouns.** Setting names from [[names]] keep their capitals inside a tag (*Amalgam ID chip*).
3. **No numbers.** Tiers belong to statuses (`name-tier`), never to tags.
4. **Useful in play.** A tag names something a player can *invoke*: an ability, a piece of gear, a body of knowledge, a relationship, a reputation, a place. If you cannot picture the action it helps, cut it.
5. **Specific, not broad** (Core p. 192). A character may hold only one broad tag across all themes, so kit tags must be specific: *cunning negotiator* not *cunning*; *nanite omnitool* is too broad and must be downgraded.
6. **The title tag (A) is a noun phrase that names the whole theme** and reads as a theme on its own with one weakness beside it (the nascent-kit test, Plan A.5).
7. **Weakness tags name a cost or an exposure, not a plot.** *withdrawal symptoms*, *stigmatized as a junkie* (Core p. 239) — something the MC can press in any scene.
8. **No game vocabulary inside tags.** No *Challenge*, *Mythos*, *status*, *Scale* in a tag.
9. **Tags do not restate the motivation.** The Itch says what you crave; the tags say what you can do.
10. **Body horror is welcome, whimsy is not.** *rebuilt from the marrow out* fits; *sparkly nanites* does not.

## 3. Motivation voice (Plan A.5; Brief §3.2, §4.2)

- **Itch** (Noise): an impulse to *indulge*, written as an urge in the imperative or first person. Book examples: "Fight big enemies, do big things." "Break the rules and don't get caught." "Ignore the danger, I can survive it." (Core p. 243). Splat Itches must pull toward the ownership/dependency theme — *feed*, *take the trigger*, *become someone else* — so that indulging keeps the theme and resisting marks Decay (Brief §4.2).
- **Ritual** (Mythos): a *discipline, price, or condition* — a practice to maintain, a sacrifice the discipline demands, a condition under which Tao flows. Book examples already in this register: "I must gather obscure ingredients," "I must replenish the wand's power," "I must practice the visceral ways of magic" (Brief §3.2). **Never** "re-enact my legend," "satisfy my Mythos," or anything that gives Tao a will (Brief §3.1). If a Ritual reads as a legend's demand, flag `[RULES CONFLICT]` (register CR-3).
- **Identity** (Self): a *conviction* to uphold, stated flatly. Book example: "The company is always right." (Core p. 177). Write it so that acting against it is obvious at the table.
- **Crew motivation** (Crew theme): any of the three forms; prebuilt statements each carry a one-line "what acting against it looks like" (Plan WP3).

## 4. Tone words (Bible §4)

Use these words and the mood they carry; they are the palette for prose, district story tags, motifs, and image briefs.

**Use:** bittersweet; worn; scraped; bought; owed; leashed; sculpted; chrome; ledger; price; cost; climbing; sorted; owned; disease; flare-up; cleanup; quiet; secret war; opportunity; body horror; violent; grim; serious; dry comedy inside the crew.

**Avoid:** epic; heroic; destiny; chosen; legend; whimsical; wondrous; cozy (except the book's *Cozies*); hopeless (the Bible says real change is possible — it costs).

The four themes in one line each, for reference when a file needs to say what it is about: *the ladder is your body; the mods decide who is human; owned or free; save the city or seize it.*

## 5. Forbidden terms

| Do not write | Write instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| season | **Series** | Brief §0.6 |
| case | **Job** | Brief §0.6 |
| crew type | **Crew theme** | Brief §0.6 |
| threat (as a category of foe) | **Challenge** (a Threat is one line inside a Challenge profile) | Brief §0.6 |
| Danger | **Challenge** | City of Mist term, not Otherscape (Plan C.6) |
| Rift, Logos, Mist, Mythos-as-legend, Mythoi, Avatar-of-a-legend | see §6 | City of Mist terms or legend cosmology the Bible removes |
| GM | **MC** | Brief §0.6 |
| splat (in any player-facing file) | the setting name from [[names]], or "the five" | "Splat" is builder vocabulary; it may appear in MC-only files and registers |
| magic, mana, spell (as the *force*) | **Tao** | Bible conventions; see §6 |
| Source-hunting, Conjuration, re-enactment | Tao-artifact / Tao-site acquisition; Tao construct; a Ritual as discipline | Brief §3.2 |
| sun, fire, stake (as vampire weaknesses) | **EM** and **the master node** | Bible §2 |
| Humanity (as a track) | Self themes, Real / Singularity poles | Brief §4.3 |

**Legend language is allowed only as a mechanical name**: the theme category *Mythos* (and its Foundry key `Mythos-OS`), the themebooks *Artifact, Companion, Esoterica, Exposure*, the Essences *Avatar* and *Conduit*, and the pillar name *Mythology Weaponized* when quoting the book before reinterpreting it as *Tao Weaponized*. Every such use in prose that is not a plain mechanical label carries `[TAO-REINTERPRETED]`.

Book vocabulary to use freely: Series, Job, Crew theme, Challenge, Limit, Threat / Consequence, MC, Megacity, district story tag, Key Player, vector (want / push / face), core moment, Essence, Decay, Identity / Ritual / Itch, Quick / Tracked Outcome, Mitigate, Loadout, Scale, Evolution, Flashback, Downtime, Nearspace, Domain, the Tangle, Harnessing.

## 6. "Tao" as the word for magic

The Bible writes magic as **Tao** and instructs the builder to use the engine's own term. The engine's terms for mystical power (*Mythos*, *Source*, *Mythoi*) name legends, which the Bible removes (Brief §3.1). There is therefore no engine term to substitute, and **Tao stays** as the setting's word for the force, its disciplines, and its practitioners' craft. `[BUILD CHOICE]` (register BC-6). Tao is impersonal: never "Tao wants," "Tao chooses," "Tao demands." Tao *flows*, *concentrates*, *is channeled*, *is interwoven*.

## 7. Marker conventions

Four inline markers, each paired with a register entry and a `flags:` value in frontmatter.

| Marker | When | Register | Frontmatter |
|---|---|---|---|
| `[RULES CONFLICT]` | The book's rules and the Bible's canon disagree. Present the options; never pick silently. | [[conflict-register]] row `CR-n` | `flags: [RULES CONFLICT]` |
| `[BUILD CHOICE]` | A judgment call the Brief did not make and the GM may overrule: a name, a specialty assignment, a slug convention, a re-flavor. | [[build-choices]] row `BC-n` | `flags: [BUILD CHOICE]` |
| `[TAO-REINTERPRETED]` | A book element that assumed legend-Mythoi (a themebook question, a Special, a Generator result, a Challenge entry) is restated under impersonal Tao. | none required; grep-able (Plan C.2) | `flags: [TAO-REINTERPRETED]` |
| `[OPEN]` | A fact the setting needs that neither Bible nor Brief provides. Leave a hook; do not fill it. | [[open-questions]] row `OQ-n` | `flags: [OPEN]` |

Format: the marker in bold at the start of the sentence or bullet, followed by the register id in parentheses, then the text.

> **[RULES CONFLICT]** (CR-2) The Exposure themebook's vampire example would place this kit in Mythos; canon places it in Noise.

Rules: one marker per sentence; markers are never nested; a file's `flags` list contains each marker type it uses at least once; a `[BUILD CHOICE]` or `[OPEN]` without a register row is a defect for the integration passes (WP6, WP8). `[INTERVIEWER'S CHOICE]` is a Brief-only marker; in the vault those five decisions live in [[build-choices]] (BC-1 to BC-5) and are cited by id, not re-marked.

## 8. Wikilink conventions (Plan A.3)

1. `[[file-name]]` — the slug only, never a path, never the `.md`.
2. Link a named entity **on its first mention in each file**; later mentions are plain text.
3. Display aliases are allowed: `[[corp-a|Meliora Bioworks]]`.
4. A link to a file another package will write is allowed if the Plan assigns it; add `(pending WP#)` after the link on first use and let WP6/WP8 clear it.
5. Never link to book pages; cite them as `(Core p. 243)` or `(Tokyo p. 73)`.
6. Image embeds use `![[assets/...]]` and belong to WP-I only.
7. Every district, Key Player, character, Challenge, kit, job, and scene is linkable by its slug; slugs are kebab-case ASCII and never change after WP6.
8. **File names are unique vault-wide and `slug` equals the basename** (BC-125). No `overview.md`, `README.md`, `reuse.md`, `membership.md`, `vectors.md`, `aftermath.md`: a folder index is `<folder>-index.md`; an entity's overview carries the entity's slug (`corp-a.md`, `bloodware.md`, `palisade.md`, `breakout.md`); companions are prefixed (`corp-a-membership.md`, `corp-a-reuse.md`, `<splat>-existing-kits.md`, `breakout-vectors.md`). An NPC and their Challenge never share a slug (`<given-surname>-challenge.md` or a distinct title/handle); a Challenge that would share a Power Set's name takes `-challenge`. Book kits are `book:<slug>` in frontmatter, never linked ([[book-kits-index]], BC-126).

## 9. Slug conventions

`[BUILD CHOICE]` (BC-7) — Folder and file slugs use **stable role words**, not proposed names, so a renamed proposal never moves a file:

- Splat folders: `02-splats/vampire/`, `werewolf/`, `mage/`, `changeling/`, `hunter/`. The display `name` uses the adopted name from [[names]].
- Key Player folders: `06-key-players/corp-a/`, `corp-b/`, `corp-c/`, `upstart/`, `syndicate/`, `government/`, `tao-society/`, `packs/`, `changeling-cells/` (the `kp_role` codes of Plan A.4). If WP1 gives the fence's network its own folder it is `fence-network/`.
- People: `given-surname` (`marisol-okonkwo`), never the handle.
- Districts, kits, Challenges, jobs, scenes: the display name in kebab-case.
- Overviews and companions: see §8 rule 8 (BC-125) — `bloodware.md`, `corp-a.md`, `corp-a-membership.md`, `corp-a-reuse.md`, `palisade.md`, `breakout.md`.

## 10. File shape (Plan A.2, A.4, A.8)

YAML frontmatter first; then a level-1 heading equal to `name`; one entity per file; `status: review` on completion; `owner` set to the package; `canon_refs` populated with the Bible/Brief sections the file relies on; `type` from the closed set in Plan A.4 (BC-127); `player_safe: true|false` on every file (BC-128) — false whenever the file carries an `## MC only` section, a twist, or a supply-chain fact. Templates for every `type` are in `99-templates/` (see [[README]]).

## 11. What the vault does not contain

No GM advice (Brief §0.2): no restatement of how to run scenes, present hard choices, pace, or use safety tools. No invented canon outside Plan A.1 rule 5. No humanity mechanic (Brief §4.3). No series-level clocks beyond the public-war Countdown (Brief §7.3). No explanation of why the camp held all five splats on one crew (Bible §6).
