# Two Seals, Different Inks

![[assets/jobs/investigation/investigation-06-the-lower-gate.png]]

**Job:** [[investigation]] · **Order:** 6 · **District:** [[chancery-hill]] · **Story tag:** *everything has a price list* · **Core moment:** **yes — the Job goes deeper (2)**

## Set piece

The **lower gate**: where the Hill's road meets the Crest's ring road and the public lift-head. A checkpoint of Chancery police, a row of "permit assistance" kiosks that are forgers working three metres from the government they forge, and a café with four outside tables where the clerks take their envelopes over coffee ([[chancery-hill]]). It is the only place on the Hill a Patched crew can stand without a reason, and everybody standing there knows it.

Above it, if the crew go up: five floors of stone around a glass atrium, a queue of people holding envelopes, and the registry in the basement — on paper, on shelves, held by people, because paper cannot be Harnessed ([[chancery-process]], *Not On The Net*).

## Getting to Vann

[[emeric-vann]], Deputy Registrar, takes his coffee at the second table at half past four. He sells looks at lists, at a rate card price, and the price goes up every time the same faces come back ([[chancery-process]], *The Price Goes Up*). He can be reached three ways and all three cost:

- **Pay.** The rate card is real and the crew are broke. Tally will advance it ([[weighhouse-line-of-credit]]) and it goes on the Slate.
- **Queue.** Go up, take a number, and be in the registry's own building — where the clerk looks up the requester before looking up the request ([[chancery-process]], third Threat). That is `exposed-on-paper-3` and their own page read back to them.
- **Be somebody else.** Which is the Doppel hard choice below.

**Doppel hard choice.** The fastest way past the process is to be a clerk who already has a queue number. The Hill's atrium is the most photographed room in Palisade and the registry's own cameras are Chancery property, which means their footage is a *page* and pages here get sold twice ([[doppels]]; [[chancery-process]], *Two Copies*). A face used on the Hill is a face on somebody's shelf afterwards, and the crew will not know whose until [[acquisition]].

## The core moment — two copies

Prepared as a situation (Core p. 290). The set is a café table with a paper cup on it; the atmosphere is four in the afternoon on the Crest with the light going; the story tags are *everything has a price list* and *two seals in different inks*.

Vann brings what the crew paid for: AP&I's escapee list, the Coldwater filing, the page their own names are on. He puts it on the table face down out of habit and turns it over.

Then, because they asked the right question or paid the second price, he brings **the other one**.

Same list. Same corporation. Same date. Two seals, in different inks. On the first page, the crew's names are struck through. On the second page, the same names are not.

He explains it the way a man explains a thing he has stopped finding strange. The registry has two of most pages. It has had for years. The **Office of Concurrent Jurisdiction** sold the same windows, the same leases, the same filings to buyers who were never going to be in the same room — and the working duplicates were never reconciled, because reconciling them would establish that the Chancery's record of Palisade has not been true for a decade, and the first person to prove that will not be thanked (BC-122; [[government]]).

Then the part that turns the Job: for eighteen months, somebody has been **buying the second copies**. Systematically. By category. Vann has sold nine of them himself and has never asked why anybody would want the copy that does not match, because the answer to that question is a thing he does not want to be a witness to. He does not reach them from his own shelves — the counterparts left the Hill years ago, and what he brings to the table came to him the way everything on the Hill comes, bought from somebody lower down a chain and marked up. Where that chain runs is a separate price, and he does not name it tonight ([[investigation-09-what-the-book-cannot-say]]).

The crew are struck off one copy and standing on the other. They are, on paper, simultaneously not on AP&I's escapee list and on it, and which one is true depends entirely on which page anybody happens to read (Bible §6; [[escapee-list]]).

**This is the second place the Job goes deeper** (Core p. 286). The crew came looking for a buyer. What they have found is that the official record of who Palisade has lost is broken on purpose by the office that sells the only lawful account of any event in the city — and that somebody is assembling the broken half.

## The other thing on Vann's table

If the crew brought the concurrence number off the Factor's payment instrument ([[investigation-05-the-back-room-at-lumen]]), Vann reads it and stops talking for a moment.

It is a **concurrence bought in advance** over an address in [[kilbride-stretch]], for a four-hour window two nights from now, in which nothing has yet happened. Buying in advance for districts where nothing has happened yet is a pattern with exactly one heavy customer on the rate card ([[government]], standing toward [[upstart]]) — and it is also, now, the Factor's method, and the file does not say those are the same thing.

The instrument is on [[halima-boyce]]'s desk, unsigned. She has started refusing these. She has not refused this one.
