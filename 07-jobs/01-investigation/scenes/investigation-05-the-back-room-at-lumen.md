# The Back Room at Lumen

**Job:** [[investigation]] · **Order:** 5 · **District:** [[corbel-gallery]] · **Story tag:** *judged by your brand* · **Core moment:** no

A set piece for the arcade half of the Job: the crew have to be Fitted for an afternoon, or be seen not to be. It is the scene where the AP&I side of the join gets a seller.

## Set piece

**Lumen**, fourth deck of the Corbel arcade: an implant bar, all white leather and blue light, where an hour buys a drink, a subdermal and a warranty card that will bounce next month ([[corbel-gallery]]). Its back room does uncatalogued work for people who can pay and do not want a record, and its owner buys the Gallery's discarded last-season implants by the sack and sends them down to [[suture-row]] — which is why the Weighhouse's network has a way in and a Patched crew can be walked up the freight lift instead of the Mile.

Everything on this deck reads brands. A crew wearing the wrong ones is watched from the moment the lift opens, politely, by people with earpieces, and the arcade's own drones do not need to be told.

## Who is here

A middle-grade clerk from **AP&I's Contracts Office**, in the back room, doing exactly what the back room is for: getting work done with no record of it. She is not important and she is not brave. She sells data remediation contracts — the paperwork by which aged, valueless, written-off account files leave the corporation legitimately, in year-blocks, invoiced as archive disposal — and she has sold eleven of them in eighteen months to one buyer through two intermediaries, at a price that first made her laugh and now frightens her.

That is where the telemetry half of the join came from ([[investigation-04-the-mirror-rig]]). It was not stolen. It was **sold**, legally, cheaply, by a corporation that had priced those files at nothing (BC-101; [[corp-c]]).

She will trade what she knows for the one thing she wants, which is for none of this to have her name on it.

## Novak's answer

If the crew push at AP&I from this end — and the clerk will give them the route — the answer comes back through an intermediary within a day, and it comes from [[rasheeda-novak]]'s desk.

It is short. The Recovery Desk buys provenance pages too. It buys them **narrowly**: a district, a month, a category of chrome, against a named file it is pricing. It has never bought a year-block, does not want one, and cannot use one — a roster of everybody the record has lost is not a pricing instrument, it is a liability the size of the Ledger.

She has no reason to lie about this and no way to prove it. **[OPEN]** (OQ-37) — [[corp-c]] remains a live candidate for the bulk buyer at the end of this Job, and this scene is written so that a crew who believe Novak and a crew who do not are both playing correctly.

## The hard choice here

**Baselines.** This is the scene the port would have paid for. If the crew took Meszaros's bench reader instead of a splice ([[investigation-03-the-cold-room]]), the clerk's files have to be read on her own terminal, in the room, while she is in it, which takes long enough that somebody with an earpiece has time to walk the fourth deck twice. Present the exposure and let it cost — a Consequence, a story tag, a Security Guard at the freight lift — and do not remove the option: [[api-enhancement-splice]] is still on sale two decks down, at a price a broke crew can nearly afford, all afternoon ([[baselines]]).
