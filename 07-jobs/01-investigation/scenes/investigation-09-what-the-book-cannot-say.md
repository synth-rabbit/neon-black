# What the Book Cannot Say

**Job:** [[investigation]] · **Order:** 9 · **District:** [[gullet-market]] · **Story tag:** *everything falls to the Gullet* · **Core moment:** no

The denouement scene (Core p. 292), and the handoff. It is short, it is quiet, and it is where the Job's actual payment is handed over — which is not the fee.

## Set piece

The Weighhouse before dawn, the counter door shut, the water door open on a black river. [[bohdan-adeyemi|Bohdan]] outside. [[dessa-rahimi|Dessa]] on the step with her knees up, because she has not gone home since [[investigation-04-the-mirror-rig]]. [[marisol-okonkwo|Tally]] puts whatever the crew brought out of the Stretch on the brass balance, because that is what she does with everything.

She reads the consignment ledger the way she reads a docket, which is quickly and once. Then she stops, and goes back, and reads the fourth category twice.

She pays. She pays the agreed figure and she chalks it and she takes her percentage, and none of that is the scene.

## The scene

The scene is the question she asks, and the fact that the Book cannot answer it.

Everything Tally knows, she knows because it crossed her balance. The Book is twenty years of what has physically moved under the Wall. And what this Job has established is that a set of pages exists which has never moved under the Wall, is not for sale in the Gullet, does not go through her counter, and is the whole of what the buyer is assembling: **the copies of the Chancery's registry that no longer match the registry.**

She asks the crew the question directly, because she has no page for it: *where are they?*

## The lead

The answer comes from [[emeric-vann]], and he gives it because he is now more frightened of the crew than of his own department — or because they paid a third time, or because [[halima-boyce]] asked him a question this week and he would like very much to be somewhere else when the next one comes.

**Second copies do not live in the Chancery basement.** They were separated years ago — physically, deliberately, one clerk at a time, "as a clerical convenience" — so that no room in the registry would ever contain a page and its own contradiction. When the basement filled, the doubled pages went out of the building under a **records-retention lease** to a leased bay in a distribution warehouse four kilometres inland in [[kilbride-stretch]], where floor space costs a hundredth of what it costs on the Hill. Its name on the lease is the **Chancery Records Repository, Kilbride**. Nobody calls it that. The street calls it **the paper barn** ([[acquisition-06-the-paper-barn]]).

That is where the nine second copies Vann sold came from — up a supply line he has never followed to its end, marked up at every step — and it is where everything the buyer has been assembling by category, page by page, at a rising price, through three intermediaries, for eighteen months, has been coming from. Including AP&I's escapee list, second copy, on which the crew's names are not struck through. Vann adds one more thing, because it is free: the bay is under a digitization contract, and the Chancery is having the counterparts scanned for the first time in sixty years — which is the Hill's word for *pulped*.

It is paper. It cannot be Harnessed ([[chancery-process]], *Not On The Net*). It is a **room, in a building, with a door**, and it can be taken.

That is the lead, and it is the last line of this Job.

## What is not answered

**Who is buying.** The Factor did not know. The shells check out to shells. The money arrives as pre-purchased concurrences from an account that treats lawfulness as change, and there are exactly two kinds of organization in Palisade that could plausibly hold such an account and want such a roster — and one of them sent a crisis-response cell to the Stretch and the other one sold the telemetry that made the roster possible. **[OPEN]** (OQ-37, OQ-11). Nobody at this counter says a name and the file does not have one.

Tally will keep selling pages. She says so, out loud, and does not apologise for it.
