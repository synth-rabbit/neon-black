# A Full Set of Names — Vectors

Every major force in [[investigation]], with a **want** (what it is trying to achieve in this Job), a **push** (what it is able and willing to spend), and a **face** — because vectors always have a face (Core p. 290). The employer is always a vector; the crew never is. The exception to the face rule is the last entry, which is time running out.

## 1. The employer — Marisol Okonkwo, "Tally"

**Face:** [[marisol-okonkwo]], across the brass balance at the Weighhouse ([[the-weighhouse]]). **Key Player:** [[fence-network]].

- **Want.** To know who has been buying pages out of the Book for eighteen months, without the Gullet ever learning that she did not already know. A fence whose own paper goes missing is a fence with a price on her.
- **Push.** Everything she gives a crew on day one and nothing she cannot take back: the back room, a meal, a surgeon on the Row who will not ask, a berth at Lock Three, a runner who knows the Labyrinth ([[dessa-rahimi]]), a line of credit ([[weighhouse-line-of-credit]]), a favour called in ([[weighhouse-runner-favor]]) — and a figure chalked on the Slate where the Foot can read it. She will not push muscle: [[bohdan-adeyemi]] stands in a doorway and does not leave the quay.
- **What she will not spend.** The Book itself. She will answer questions about pages she sold; she will not let anyone read a shelf. If the crew ask for the ledgers she says no, pleasantly, once, and the subject is closed for the Series.
- **In play.** Tally opens the Job ([[investigation-01-the-counter-door]]) and closes it ([[investigation-09-what-the-book-cannot-say]]). Between those she is a phone call, a chit, and a price. The *Percentage* accrues the whole way.
- **[OPEN]** (OQ-11) Nothing about her conduct in this Job resolves whether she is connected to [[upstart|Continuity]]. Both readings work: she is genuinely blind and hiring out of alarm, or she is confirming something she already suspects and wants a crew of nobodies to carry the risk. The scenes are written so the MC never has to choose.

## 2. The opposition — the Factor

**Face:** [[anselm-boateng|Anselm Boateng]], whom the Foot calls **"the Factor."** Profile: [[the-factor]]. **Key Player:** none — and that is the point of him.

- **Want.** To close a standing order. Four categories, delivered as one consignment, invoiced in full, on a delivery window he did not set and cannot move.
- **Push.** Money that is not his, in instruments from the Hill; a bonded room and a weigh-in desk ([[the-counting-room]]); day-rate collectors he hires by the evening and does not know the names of; and — the dangerous one — the ability to buy lawfulness over an address before anything happens at it, because his principal settles in pre-purchased concurrences and he has been given a few to spend ([[government]], BC-122).
- **What he will not spend.** Himself. The Factor has never once been in a room when it went badly, and every arrangement he makes has a person in it who is not him.
- **The limit of him.** He does not know who is buying. He has three intermediaries, a category list, a delivery address that changes, and a payment method. He believes — and will say, and is not lying — that his principal is an insurance-forensics consolidator. He has never tested it. When the crew press him for a name he gives them one, and it is a shell, and it is checkable, and it checks out to another shell.
- **In play.** Named in row 2, felt in rows 3–6, present in [[investigation-07-the-counting-room]]. He is the confrontation and not the answer.

## 3. The Hill, below — Emeric Vann

**Face:** [[emeric-vann]], Deputy Registrar. **Key Player:** [[government]].

- **Want.** To retire out of the registry before anybody audits it, with enough put by to leave the Hill and never be a witness ([[emeric-vann]]).
- **Push.** Access to every list, lease, licence and contract in Palisade, sold a page at a time, at a price that rises each time the same people come back ([[chancery-process]], *The Price Goes Up*) — and the standing threat of selling the crew's page to somebody else instead.
- **In this Job.** He is the man who has been selling the second copies. He does not think of it as a crime and will explain, patiently, why it is not: the pages were sold before he had the desk, the discrepancy predates him, and a page that is already double is not made worse by being read. He is frightened of exactly one thing, which is [[halima-boyce]] finding out, and the crew can use that.
- **What he gives up.** Under pressure, in [[investigation-06-the-lower-gate]]: that the second copies do not live in the Chancery basement at all — and, in [[investigation-09-what-the-book-cannot-say]], where they do. What he brings to the café table is not from his own shelves: the counterparts left the Hill years ago under a records-retention lease, and the nine second copies he has sold came up a supply line from the paper barn at a mark-up, two removes below him ([[ileana-boakye]]). He has never been inside the building. Where the line runs is the last thing he sells (WP8 reconciliation, BC-171).

## 4. The Hill, above — Halima Boyce

**Face:** [[halima-boyce]], Superintendent, Office of Concurrent Jurisdiction. **Key Player:** [[government]].

- **Want.** To know what she is authorizing before she authorizes it — a want she acquired recently and which is going to cost her everything ([[halima-boyce]]).
- **Push.** The Office's instruments: a concurrence granted, delayed, split, or refused, and the Chancery police who still answer to her, for now ([[envelope-detail]]).
- **In this Job.** She is not an ally and does not become one. She is a lever with a temperature. Somebody bought a concurrence over an address in [[kilbride-stretch]] for a window in which nothing had yet happened, which is the pattern she has started refusing, and she has held this one on her desk unsigned. Whether it is signed, refused, split between two claimants, or sold retroactively to a second buyer is the pivot of [[investigation-08-the-cordon]] — and it turns on what the crew did at the lower gate.
- **She never learns the whole shape.** If the crew hand her proof that the registry is double-sold, she does not act on it in this Job. She reads it, thanks them, and files it, and the Series is different afterwards.

## 5. The rival buyer — Rasheeda Novak

**Face:** [[rasheeda-novak]], the Escapee Recovery Desk. **Key Player:** [[corp-c]].

- **Want.** Accurate pricing: for each name on the list, what recovering it would cost and what it would return, and to be right ([[rasheeda-novak]]).
- **Push.** Money for information, subcontracted collectors she does not have to meet, and warranty telemetry that most of the list does not know is still reporting ([[escapee-recovery-desk]]).
- **In this Job.** AP&I's Contracts Office has been selling aged write-off ledgers to the Factor by the year-block, and Novak has separately been buying provenance pages of her own — a smaller, narrower, entirely explicable pattern. She is a **rival buyer, not the buyer**, and the file says so without proving it. She will tell the crew, through an intermediary at [[investigation-05-the-back-room-at-lumen]], that the bulk order is not hers, and she has no reason to lie and no way to demonstrate it.
- **Why she is a vector at all.** Every hour the crew spend touching AP&I chrome, AP&I clinics, AP&I paper and AP&I people is an hour the Desk's `priced` clock is running. She does not hunt them. She waits to see whether they become worth collecting, and this Job is the crew making themselves interesting.
- **[OPEN]** (OQ-37) AP&I must remain a live candidate for the bulk buyer at the end of this Job. Nothing Novak says or does removes it.

## 6. The corner nobody looks at — a Continuity crisis-response cell

**Face:** none, deliberately. Six to eight people in matte soft-shell with a serial where a name should be ([[continuity-crisis-response-cell]]). **Key Player:** [[upstart]].

A cell is a vector only in the last scene, and only as a force with a want and a push — it has no named officer in this Job and the crew never speak to one.

- **Want.** The scene. Not the consignment, not the crew: the *scene* — declared, cordoned, cleaned, and reported in Continuity's words ([[continuity-crisis-response-cell]], *Cleanup Is The Product*).
- **Push.** Soft-shell, breaching frames, a van with the side door already open, and an authority exactly as legal as the police (Bible §3).
- **What it is doing there.** Not stated, in this Job or its aftermath. A cell arriving first at an address in a district where Continuity holds ground ([[kilbride-stretch]]) is exactly what Continuity does everywhere ([[upstart]]). It reads as a coincidence, or as the buyer's cleanup, and the file does not know which. **[OPEN]** (OQ-37, OQ-11).

## 7. Time running out — the consignment window

**No face.** This is the book's stated exception: a vector that is not a thinking agent but still has a want and a push (Core p. 290). Profile: [[the-consignment-window]] (role: countdown, progress Limit `consignment-clears`, tier 5).

- **Want.** To close. The consignment is assembled, weighed, sealed and moved on a window the Factor did not choose, and once it clears the bonded room it stops being one object anybody can find.
- **Push.** Everything that happens on its own: hired hands showing up on schedule, shelves filling, a haulier booked, a concurrence dated. The run pushes by *continuing*.
- **How it advances.** Named in [[the-consignment-window]]: every Downtime the crew take, every scene they end without a lead, every time they are seen asking about the Factor by somebody who invoices him. It does not advance for being fought.
- **When it maxes.** The consignment ships. The Job does not end — it changes: the crew can still reach the paper-barn lead through [[emeric-vann]], but they reach it without proof, without the Factor's ledger, and with Tally's percentage against them. See [[investigation-aftermath]], "If the run cleared."

## Canon and flags

- Vectors have want, push and face; the employer is always a vector; the crew never is; time-running-out vectors have no face: Core p. 290. Delegating power down a Key Player's hierarchy rather than using its principals: Core p. 291 — which is why AP&I appears as a desk, the Chancery as two officials, and Continuity as a cell.
- **[BUILD CHOICE]** the Factor, the consignment window, and the assignment of Novak as rival-not-buyer; registered in [[build-choices]] "Added by WP7b".
- **[OPEN]** (OQ-37) the bulk buyer is unresolved and both [[upstart]] and [[corp-c]] stay possible; (OQ-11) Tally and Continuity untouched; (OQ-17) the cell's presence is not explained.
