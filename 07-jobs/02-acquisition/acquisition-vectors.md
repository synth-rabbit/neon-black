# The Second Copy — Vectors

Every major force in [[acquisition]], with a **want** (what it is trying to achieve here), a **push** (what it is able and willing to spend), and a **face** (the person; Core p. 290 — "even if the main foe is *Megacorp International*, the company is not itself the vector"). The crew is never a vector. One vector is not a thinking agent, because it is time running out.

Three of these people are new to the vault and are registered in [[build-choices]] "Added by WP7c" (BC-161): [[margit-nakagawa|Margit Nakagawa]], [[ileana-boakye|Ileana Boakye]], and [[ilya-sarpong|Ilya Sarpong, "Barrow."]] Each has an `npc` file in this job's `characters/` folder (BC-174); this file carries their vector, and the `npc` file carries the rest.

---

## 1. The employer — [[halima-boyce]], Superintendent, Office of Concurrent Jurisdiction

**Face:** [[margit-nakagawa|Margit Nakagawa]], a solicitor's clerk from one of the law firms behind the Hill, who does the meeting, carries the cash, and has never met her principal's principal either.

- **Want.** To hold, in her own hands and off the registry, the one volume that shows in order what she has spent two years signing. She has begun keeping her own copy of everything she signs, in a place that is not the registry ([[halima-boyce]]); this is the part she cannot copy, because it is the part that was already written when she started.
- **Push.** The Office's instruments and nothing else. A concurrence granted, delayed, split, or refused: she can make a corporate crew lawful, unlawful, or merely late, and she can do it retroactively at a surcharge. She will spend a concurrence on the crew's night if they ask well ([[acquisition-05-the-prep-sequence]]). She will not spend a Chancery officer, will not admit the job exists, and will not be in the room.
- **From Job 1.** She held a concurrence over an address in the Stretch unsigned, and what the crew did at the lower gate decided its shape ([[investigation-08-the-cordon]]). If they put a second-copy page on her desk in that Job, this job is what she did with it ([[investigation-aftermath]]): she read it, filed it, and went looking for the rest of the volume through people who do not work for the Hill.
- **What she will not do.** Protect them afterwards. If the crew are taken with the volume, Nakagawa's firm has never heard of them, and Boyce is a name they cannot spell.
- **The seam.** She has not told them, and will not, that the volume carries her signature. She does not know that it also carries a line about their own work line — she has never read the schedule of persons, because nobody reads the schedule of persons. **She is the twist's face**: when the crew put the page in front of her in [[acquisition-09-what-a-page-is-worth]], she reads it and stops talking, and what she says next is the pivot.

**Nakagawa.** Thirties, brisk, expensive shoes and a cheap coat over them, a clerk who has learned to look like a partner. Want: the file closed and the fee accounted. Push: cash, a firm's letterhead, and the ability to lose a crew's name permanently. She is not brave and she is not lying — she genuinely does not know what is in the volume, and says so, which is the most reassuring thing anybody says to the crew all job.

## 2. The target's owner — [[government|the Chancery]]

**Face:** [[emeric-vann]], Deputy Registrar, for the paper; the Repository's own duty clerk and the posted [[envelope-detail]] for the building.

- **Want.** The counterpart set scanned, certified, and pulped, on schedule, with a completion bonus, before anyone reads a run of it in order. Nobody at the Hill has decided this in those words. It is what a records-retention contract looks like when the institution signing it has a decade of double-sold pages behind it and no appetite for an audit ([[government]]).
- **Push.** The records lease, the fee schedule, the queue, referral to another bureau, the Envelope Detail on the gate, and — as a last resort nobody will authorize in time — a Chancery police response that arrives forty minutes after it is called, into a district where nobody official comes down ([[kilbride-stretch]]).
- **Vann's own want and push** are in his file: out, with enough put by, before anyone audits the registry; access sold a page at a time, and the standing alternative of selling the crew's page to whoever else is asking. He sold the crew the building at the end of Job 1 ([[investigation-09-what-the-book-cannot-say]]) and charged more for that sentence than for anything else he has ever sold them. He has never been inside it: the second copies he sells come up a supply line from the bay at a mark-up, two removes below him, and the bottom of that line is [[ileana-boakye]], whom he has never met.

## 3. The rival — the Carters

**Face:** [[ilya-sarpong|Ilya Sarpong, "Barrow."]]

A freelance crew of five who move things for people who cannot be seen moving them: bonded transit boxes, a van with a records-contractor plate that is real, and a reputation for never once having been in a building at the same time as its owner. The street calls them the Carters because that is what they do and because they named themselves after it, the way the Smokes and the Sidings did ([[cinder-yards]]).

- **Want.** The Coldwater counterpart, whole, delivered to a client they have never met, through three intermediaries, paid half in advance eleven weeks ago. They do not know what is in it. They have been told it must arrive **unread**, which is the only detail about the job that has ever bothered Barrow.
- **Push.** Patience, a real contractor's plate, an inside line to the collection schedule, and — if the paper is going to leave the building in somebody else's hands — force on the route. Barrow's standing preference, said out loud to his own crew: *we are not thieves in buildings, we are thieves on roads.* Core p. 291's worked example is exactly this: if the crew let the volume fall to them, the crew get a chase; if the crew get it first, the Carters set the ambush on the escape route.
- **Barrow.** Forties, Fitted, a docker's build and a clerk's manners, drives himself. Handle from the barrow he started with in the Gullet twenty years ago and still keeps. He does not enjoy hurting people and is entirely willing to. He will talk, at length, on the route, and everything he says is true except the name of his client, which he does not have.
- **Profiled as** [[the-carters]] (pursuer).

## 4. Time running out — [[collection-run]]

Not a thinking agent (Core p. 290: "the exception to the rule are vectors that represent time running out"). It has a want and a push anyway.

- **Want.** To close the shelf-run: collect, scan, certify, pulp. Nine days at the start of the job, and fewer after [[acquisition-05-the-prep-sequence]], because the contractor has a completion bonus and moved the date.
- **Push.** A van on a schedule, a scanning bay in the Lattice with a slot booked, a certification form, and an industrial pulper that runs on Thursdays. Nobody is protecting the volume. It is simply going to stop existing.
- **Profiled as** [[collection-run]] (countdown, job-level only — **[OPEN]** OQ-9; Brief §7.3 forbids new series-level clocks).

## 5. The insider — [[ileana-boakye|Ileana Boakye]], records assistant, scanning contract

- **Want.** To be clear of the Slate and off the contract before it ends, with her name on nothing. She owes the Weighhouse for a surgery her brother had on [[suture-row]] and she has been paying it off in pages.
- **Push.** A badge that opens the bay, a shift pattern, the shelf list, and the fact that she has already lifted pages out of eleven volumes and sold them — up a chain of intermediaries that ends, two removes later, at [[emeric-vann]]'s café table, which is where Job 1's nine second copies came from — which means she can be leaned on by anybody who works that out. Her push against the crew is the same thing pointed the other way: she can hand a shelf list to two crews as easily as one, and has.
- **Face.** Late twenties, tired, careful, a laminated badge she keeps turning over. Thirties by the end of this job.
- **What she does not know.** Who buys the pages. Three intermediaries, a chit, cash in a locker at the Kilbride Interchange, and a category list that makes no sense to her: corporate shipping losses, chrome with dead tags, and things nobody can explain (**[OPEN]** OQ-37 — she is a supply line into it, not an answer to it).
- **In play.** She is the *insider pulling the same job* complication (Core p. 285). She can be bought, turned, replaced, or burned, and each of those costs something in [[acquisition-aftermath]].

## 6. The fence — [[marisol-okonkwo|Tally]]

- **Want.** The volume across her balance for one hour before it goes on, and the percentage. Also, and more than she will admit, the answer to the question she has been carrying for eighteen months: who is buying provenance in bulk ([[fence-network]]; **[OPEN]** OQ-37). A Chancery counterpart volume is the first object anyone has ever offered her that might contain the buyer's account number.
- **Push.** The offer itself, the [[weighhouse-line-of-credit]] that funds the Loading Up, a runner ([[weighhouse-runner-favor]]), the back room, the water door, and the Slate — the crew's name is on it in chalk, in public, by the counter door.
- **Face.** Herself. She does not send anyone.
- **The line she will not cross.** She will not write the crew's own page up in the Book if they ask her not to. She will remember it anyway.

## 7. The written-off account — [[corp-c|AP&I]]

**Face:** [[rasheeda-novak]], Recovery Analyst, Contracts Office, thirtieth deck of [[amalgam-stack]].

- **Want.** For the arithmetic to be right, and — a want she has not articulated, because it belongs to the corporation rather than to her — for nobody to read the Coldwater filings in order. A transfer schedule that sold a hundred and four written-off accounts for a unit each is not a scandal inside the Contracts Office; it is a line item. It becomes a scandal the moment somebody outside reads it next to a concurrence.
- **Push.** [[escapee-recovery-desk]] and [[escapee-list]]: warranty telemetry, a budget for buying rumours, subcontracted collectors on a day rate, and the fact that every permit, claim, or filing the crew touch on the Hill crosses paper with a name on a list. She never initiates. Everything she does this job is triggered by something the crew did.
- **In play.** [[acquisition-04-thirtieth-deck]] is the crew walking into her building on purpose, which is the most expensive scouting in the job.

## 8. The monitor — [[upstart|Continuity]]

**Face:** [[rosalind-ekwueme]], Vice President, Client Continuity. Not a cordon; a contract.

- **Want.** For the advance-concurrence traffic to stay unexamined. Continuity buys concurrences in advance for districts where nothing has happened yet, and retroactively at the surcharge when it has ([[government]]); an outsider reading a counterpart volume in order is the only way anyone in Palisade would ever see the pattern those instruments make.
- **Push.** Monitors in the Nearspace of every gallery in [[the-lattice]], including the bay the scan set uploads to; [[the-cold-suite]]; a crisis-response cell that can be at a Kilbride warehouse in eleven minutes if a client will pay for it; and a legal department that has never lost an argument about what *for the duration of the emergency* means. Its cheapest push, and the one this job is written around, is a **phone call to the records contractor about a service level**, which is how the collection date moves.
- **Restraint.** Continuity has, at most, a cleanup report from the Stretch in which a crew of nobodies was standing in a room ([[investigation-aftermath]]), and it has connected them to nothing. It is reacting to a Domain query, not to them. If the crew never Harness into the records Domain in [[acquisition-03-the-scan-index]], this vector's push in [[acquisition-06-the-paper-barn]] and [[acquisition-08-collection-night]] does not fire at all.
- **[OPEN]** (OQ-17) nothing this vector does in this job touches what Continuity is.

## Who backstabs whom (Core p. 292, Step 4)

- **Boakye sells the same shelf list twice** — to the crew and to the Carters — for the same reason both times.
- **Barrow keeps the ambush from his own crew** until the night, because two of them would have refused it.
- **Vann sells the crew's asking** to whoever else is asking, within the day, at the standard rate, and does not consider it a betrayal because on the Hill it is not one.
- **Boyce does not warn them** what is in the volume, because she does not know — and then, having read it, has to decide within one scene whether to tell them the rest.
