# What a Page Is Worth

![[assets/jobs/acquisition/acquisition-09-what-a-page-is-worth.png]]

**Job:** [[acquisition]] · **Order:** 9 · **District:** [[gullet-market]] (default; may be [[chancery-hill]]) · **Story tag:** *everything falls to the Gullet* · **Core moment:** no

## Set piece

Delivery. The crew choose the room, and the room decides the scene.

- **The Weighhouse back room.** The volume on [[marisol-okonkwo|Tally]]'s balance, weighed, because everything is. The hour she asked for in [[acquisition-01-a-job-at-the-counter]], now. She reads faster than anyone expects and stops in the same place they stopped.
- **A meeting room behind the Hill.** [[margit-nakagawa|Margit Nakagawa]], cash on the table, a receipt she is not going to give them, and a description she checks against the object word by word: *whole and readable*. She does not ask whether they read it. She was not told to.
- **A bench in the Chancery's garden.** [[halima-boyce]], who has agreed, once, in daylight, to be seen with people who do not work for the Hill, because that is the entire content of her want.

## This is where the twist becomes a choice

The crew have already read the two pages. What happens here is that they decide who else does — and every option costs.

**If they hand the volume over and say nothing:** they are paid, the percentage is taken, the Slate comes down a little, and the Series is still Paycheck. That is a real ending and the aftermath treats it as one (Core p. 281: either pole can rise up naturally; be attentive and flexible).

**If they put the page in front of Boyce:** she reads her own signature on an instrument that released a work line eleven days before anyone was released, and she does not say anything for a while. What she says next is the pivot delivered by the one person in Palisade who can explain what the document *is* without explaining who bought it:

> *"That is my hand. I signed four hundred of those that quarter. I have never once read a schedule of persons; nobody does; the schedule is the part the buyer fills in.* ***Somebody bought a window in advance and told me what to write in it,*** *and I countersigned it, and eleven days later you were outside. I do not know whose account that is. I can find out. It will take me about a month and after that I will not have a job, and neither will you have anyone on the Hill."*

She can be asked for one thing, and she will give one thing, and the crew choose it: **the name behind the standing account**, which takes a month and costs her post — or **a standing concurrence**, which she can write this afternoon and which makes the crew the lawful party at an address of their choosing, once. She will not give both. That is the price in the book's sense (Core p. 281), and it is the moment a table decides what kind of Series this is.

**If they sell the pages instead** — to Tally, to a corporate loss-adjuster through the Book, to the Carters' client through Boakye's locker at the Interchange — the money is very good and the pages are gone, and whoever bought them knows the crew read them.

**If they put it on a feed** — the courtyard councils in [[kilbride-stretch]], a Relay Fields kid with a mast, a Halloran Circus bookmaker who owes somebody — then Palisade learns that the Chancery's record disagrees with itself. That is a public, witnessed act about the corporations' paperwork, and it moves [[secret-war-goes-public]]. It also ends any possibility of the crew ever buying anything on the Hill again.
