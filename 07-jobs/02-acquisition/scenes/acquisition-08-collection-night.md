# Collection Night

![[assets/jobs/acquisition/acquisition-08-collection-night.png]]

**Job:** [[acquisition]] · **Order:** 8 · **District:** [[kilbride-stretch]] · **Story tag:** *nobody official comes down here* · **Core moment:** **yes** · **Climax**

## Set piece

Four in the morning on a loading apron in the sprawl. Sodium light with moths in it. The arc-lamp gantry behind them in the bay, still running, still moving down run C towards a cage that is standing open. Drone-lanes crossing overhead every ninety seconds, carrying somebody's groceries. To the west, the Wall is a grey line with a glow on top; in every other direction there is prefab grid, identical, flat, unlit, for four kilometres.

And backing up to the roller door with its reversing lamp on, on a night it is not scheduled for, is a records-contractor van with a real plate.

This is the finale and it is an **escape**, not a boss fight (Core p. 291: "in others this might be an escape from some unwanted situation … the escape can take up an entire scene at the end of the job, serving as the climax of the story"). The crew have the thing. Everything now is about getting it four kilometres across an unlit grid with two other parties on the apron and a clock that is about to certify a volume that is not there.

## The ambush

The *another team is pulling the same job* complication (Core p. 285), staged exactly as the book's own worked example stages it (Core p. 291): the rival crew wanted the loot, they were willing to push into direct confrontation, and being thieves they preferred an ambush — **so if the crew got the volume first, the ambush is on the escape route.**

The Carters have watched this apron for eleven weeks and have never entered the building. They do not need to. There is one vehicle route off the apron, two pedestrian cuts through the fence, and a drone-lane underpass where the grid begins, and Barrow has people on all three because there are only three.

- **He talks first.** He always does. He will offer to buy it, at a good price, in cash, on the apron, and he means it: a crew that sells is a crew he does not have to fight, and Barrow does not enjoy hurting people. What he cannot do is let it leave with somebody else, because he was paid eleven weeks ago and half of it is spent.
- **He was told it must arrive unread.** He will ask, once, whether they opened it, and he will watch their faces, and that is the only moment in the job where the Carters' client is nearly in the room.
- **If the crew sell it, the job is over and the pivot still lands** — they read the pages in [[acquisition-07-the-coldwater-run]], and the aftermath's choices do not depend on holding the paper. What they lose is the proof, the fee from Nakagawa, and Boyce.

## Beats the MC has ready

- **The three exits.** Vehicle route, two fence cuts, the underpass. Covering all three is exactly what five people can do and nothing more.
- **Paper is heavy and paper burns.** A buckram volume is five kilos and does not fit under a coat. It fits in [[weighhouse-salvage-crate]]. It does not survive a fire, a fire-suppression discharge, or the settling lagoons.
- **Splitting it.** Somebody will suggest tearing the two pages out and letting the volume go. That works, it is cheap, and it costs the crew the only thing that made the pages provable: a page out of a counterpart volume is a page anyone can say was forged, and the Hill has a forger in every law firm.
- **Witnesses.** This is a district where nobody official comes down, which is not the same as a district where nobody is looking. If two armed groups fight over a book on an apron in front of the night shift, that is a public, witnessed event and it is a Threat toward [[secret-war-goes-public]] — more so if anything happens that no Baseline could have done.
- **The last Flashback.** Denying further Flashbacks to the Prep Sequence is the Consequence this scene is written for (Core p. 125). Spent here, at the end, it reads as preparation having run out.
