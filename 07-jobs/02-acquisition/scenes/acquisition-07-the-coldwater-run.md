# The Coldwater Run

![[assets/jobs/acquisition/acquisition-07-the-coldwater-run.png]]

**Job:** [[acquisition]] · **Order:** 7 · **District:** [[kilbride-stretch]] · **Story tag:** *nobody official comes down here* · **Core moment:** **yes**

## Set piece

The cage is three metres by two and smells of dust and warm paper. The arc-lamp gantry is three runs away and throws everything through the mesh in hard bars. The Coldwater run is eleven volumes, and the crew want one of them: buckram, a Chancery binding, a paper label with a lease number and a district on it, and inside, five hundred pages of the second copy of everything Palisade ever wrote down about the place where the Ledger is.

They came to take it. The core moment is that they **open it first**.

Nobody makes them. The volume can be carried out unopened; the employer asked for it whole and readable, not unread, and the crew were paid for delivery. But there is a lease number on the spine and it is the lease number of the camp they were in, and the job has spent five scenes teaching them that this is the only object in the city that could tell them anything about themselves. If they do not open it here, they open it on the apron in [[acquisition-08-collection-night]] or on Tally's balance afterwards, and the same three pages are in it either way.

## What is in the volume

Read as the crew read it: dull, dull, dull, and then not.

- **The remediation contract.** AP&I's lease on [[coldwater-outfall]], with the site-security clause the whole camp lives inside (BC-24). Two seals, in different inks.
- **The uninhabitability declaration**, the quarantine schedules, nine years of an inspector's signature twice a year, all in the same hand, including for two years when that inspector was dead.
- **The escapee list, as filed.** The crew's names, with no bounty against them (Bible §6) — and this is the *counterpart*, so the strikings and amendments made on the Hill's copy are not here. Names struck off one copy are still names here. The crew's names are on both.

And then two pages that were never meant to be read in the same room.

### The advance concurrence

A numbered instrument from the Office of Concurrent Jurisdiction. Filed **eleven days before** the night the Ledger was struck. It names an address by lease number, a window that begins at dusk on that night and ends at dawn, and a buyer identified only by a **standing-account number**. It is signed by [[halima-boyce]].

Under *schedule of persons and property to which this instrument applies*, in the flat language of an office that prices everything and imagines nothing, there is a line:

> *site population, work lines as scheduled, released.*

Their work line is one of the numbers on it.

### The transfer schedule

Filed by AP&I's Contracts Office under the same lease, eleven weeks after that night, and referenced on the receipt the crew read on the thirtieth deck. It assigns a block of **written-off Standing Accounts** — one hundred and four of them, the crew's among them — on to a purchaser, for a consideration of one unit each. The purchaser is identified only by a standing-account number.

**It is the same number.**

## The twist, in the crew's own terms

Nobody in the scene explains this to them, and there is nobody in the cage to explain it. What the two pages say, put together, is:

*They were not rescued and they were not forgotten. The night they stopped being property was bought in advance off a published fee schedule by somebody who filed for it eleven days early and wrote them into the schedule as released site population — and eleven weeks later their debts were sold on to the same account for a unit apiece.*

Every reading is available and none of them is confirmed by the paper. The volume does not say who the account is. It does not say why. It says nothing whatever about [[tomas-adair|Rook]] beyond a work-line number he shares with them, and nothing about what any of the buyers are (**[OPEN]** OQ-10, OQ-17, OQ-39 — this scene answers none of them, and no roll here can).

**[BUILD CHOICE]** — the package's proposal, which the GM may overrule with no change to any file: the standing account is **Continuity's**, and it is the same account behind the eighteen months of bulk provenance buying in Tally's Book (**[OPEN]** OQ-37, OQ-11). The number is the number in every scene; a table that would rather it be an AP&I counterparty, a Meliora loss-adjuster, or a shell nobody has met loses nothing.

## The complication: the run is short

*The target item was swapped for a fake, or isn't what was first thought* (Core p. 285). The volume is a volume, but pages have been lifted out of it ahead of the scan by [[ileana-boakye|Ileana Boakye]], and what is missing is not random — it is the categories her buyer pays for. Three things follow, and which of them the crew are looking at is theirs to work out:

1. **The instrument the crew most want is intact, and its two attachments are gone.** The advance concurrence names a standing-account number; the schedules that would have let anyone resolve that number to a name are among the missing pages, and now exist only in the scan set, in a Domain in Gallery Seven, behind Continuity's monitors.
2. **One page that is still there is a forgery.** The inspector's certificate for the two dead years carries two seals in different inks ([[chancery-process]] *Two Copies*). Whoever forged it did not know the counterpart existed.
3. **Somebody has been here before them, for months.** Which means the crew are not the first people to know what is in this volume — and the buyer who has the missing pages has known for weeks.
