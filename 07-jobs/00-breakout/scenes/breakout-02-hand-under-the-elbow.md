# A Hand Under the Elbow

![[assets/jobs/breakout/breakout-02-hand-under-the-elbow.png]]

**Job:** [[breakout]] · **Order:** 2 · **District:** [[coldwater-outfall]] · **Story tag:** *nobody's supposed to be here* · **Core moment:** **yes**

## Set piece

The yard. Forty meters of packed fill between the dormitory blocks and the shed row, where the count is read twice a shift and nobody stands still. Tonight nobody stands still for a different reason: four blocks have emptied into it at once and a hundred people are running for the one light they can see, which is the causeway gate three kilometers off, and the shed row and the wire are between them and it. Torches — the shift sergeant's line — sweep the yard from the shed end. Somebody has been hit already; the crowd goes around them.

Against all of that, at a walk, six people in matte grey. The crowd parts for them the way water parts for a piling. They are not looking at the crowd. They are not looking at the crew. They are looking at Rook, who is where he always is — in front of the crew, saying *stay behind me* — and who sees them coming and stops saying it.

This is the extraction. It is prepared as a situation ready to go (Core p. 290) and it is played in the present tense; there is no Flashback slot in it.

What the crew see, in order:

1. The lead responder — tall, close-cropped grey-blonde hair once the helmet comes off, a serial where a name would be ([[hanne-oyelaran]]) — reaches Rook and puts a hand under his elbow. Not a grip. The hand a paramedic uses.
2. Rook goes with her. He does not resist. He does not explain. He looks back once — at the crew — and that look is the whole of what the Bible gives him here.
3. Two responders fall in on either side of him. They are careful with him. *Undamaged* was in the contract, and it shows in the way they move him through a crowd that is trampling itself: shoulders in, a hand up, a space around him that nobody else in the yard has.
4. The lead looks directly at the crew. She does not raise her weapon. She says, in the voice she uses at cordons, *Keep moving. That way* — the shed row, the wire gap, the causeway — and then she is walking, and Rook with her, and the crowd closes.
5. If any of the crew is close enough to hear it, over his shoulder as he goes, three words: *Tally. The Weighhouse.* Nothing else. (The rest of the referral is [[breakout-05-a-name-and-a-place]]; if the crew never reach the van, these three words are the referral, and they are enough.)

What the crew do not see, and nothing in this scene supplies: why. Nobody in grey says a word about it. Rook does not. The cell was told a name and a window ([[hanne-oyelaran-challenge]], *She Was Not Told Why*). From inside a general breakout, this looks like six armed people taking one man out of a camp during a riot and being strange about it — careful, unhurried, uninterested in anyone else — and that is exactly what it is supposed to look like (Bible §5: the general breakout is cover for that person's importance; the party knows this person but not why they matter).
