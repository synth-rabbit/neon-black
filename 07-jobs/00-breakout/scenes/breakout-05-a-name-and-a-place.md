# A Name and a Place

![[assets/jobs/breakout/breakout-05-a-name-and-a-place.png]]

**Job:** [[breakout]] · **Order:** 5 · **District:** [[coldwater-outfall]] · **Story tag:** *nobody's supposed to be here* · **Core moment:** **yes**

## Set piece

Where the causeway meets the coast road there is a turn, and in the turn a grey van with its side door already open — matte, no livery beyond a serial, parked where nothing was parked an hour ago. The cell is loading: frames, a medical kit, two responders in the back already, one on the road with a hand up stopping nothing. The crowd goes past it on both sides and does not stop, because the woman by the door says *keep walking, don't stop on the road* in the voice built for frightened people, and they keep walking.

Rook is standing beside the van. Not in it. Standing on the road in camp coveralls with the number on the back, a responder on either side of him, and the lead's hand no longer under his elbow because there is nowhere for him to go that she does not want him to go. He is looking back down the causeway. He is waiting — that is how it reads — for the crew.

When they reach him, he says the last thing the Bible gives him to say (§5). The words are the job's:

> *Tally. The Weighhouse, on the Gullet quay — the counter door, not the water door. Tell her Rook sent you. She'll count it.*

And, if they ask him anything — why, who, what happens now — the thug story, told the same way he told the arresting officers, or nothing. He does not explain. He has never once explained himself ([[tomas-adair-challenge]], base tag). If a PC presses, [[hanne-oyelaran|the lead]] takes her helmet off and looks directly at them: *He's coming with us. You're not. That's the whole of it.* She is telling the truth as she knows it (*She Was Not Told Why*).

The lead looks at the crew once more, all of them, the way she looked at them in the yard. She does not raise her weapon. She does not ask their names. Nothing about them was in her contract, and letting them walk is the plan.

**Where he goes.** The Bible ends at the referral, and what becomes of Rook is **[OPEN]** (OQ-39). The scene is written so that either of the following happens, at the MC's choice, and nothing before or after it changes:

- **He leaves with Continuity.** The lead says his name — *Adair* — like a roll call; he gets into the van without help, sits, and looks at the crew through the open door until the responder pulls it shut. The van goes west along the coast road, lights off, and is gone in the haze inside a minute. The crew's last sight of him is a face in a van door. `contract-complete` 4: Cell Eleven is off site with its contract, undamaged.
- **He vanishes.** Something on the road takes the crew's eyes for a moment — the drone's searchlight sweeping the causeway behind them, the truck coming through the crowd, a tower light on the horizon as the camp's `lockdown` climbs — and when they look back, the cell is loading the last frame and Rook is not beside the van, not in it, and not on the road. The lead looks at the space where he was, says one word into a dead radio, and gets in. The van goes. Nobody saw where he went; the flats on one side, the dark on the other. The lead's `contract-complete` stands at 3, and what Continuity makes of that is not this job's.

Both leave OQ-10, OQ-17, OQ-39, and OQ-40 exactly where the Bible left them. Neither version has anyone say what he is.
