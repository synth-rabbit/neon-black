# Job 0: The Breakout

**Type:** extraction, subverted — the PCs are the ones being extracted, and the extraction is not for them (Core p. 288: "one of the PCs is sent to infiltrate a place, then needs to be extracted themselves") · **Sessions:** 1 · **Pole:** paycheck (this is the job before the first paycheck) · **Pivot twist:** no · **Districts:** [[coldwater-outfall]] → [[lowmere-sinks]] or [[kilbride-stretch]] → [[gullet-market]]

The Series opens **in medias res inside Reconciliation Facility 4** — the Ledger, [[corp-c|Amalgamated Prosthetic & Interface]]'s secret debt-labor and penal camp in the [[coldwater-outfall|Coldwater Outfall]] — on the night [[upstart|Continuity Risk & Response]] strikes it (Bible §5; Brief §7.1). Continuity's objective is one man: [[tomas-adair|Tomas Adair, "Rook"]], the crew's leader on the work line. The general breakout is cover. The crew escape inside the cover, watch Rook taken at walking pace by people who came only for him, and reach the far end of the causeway with one name — [[marisol-okonkwo|Marisol Okonkwo, "Tally"]], at the Weighhouse in the Gullet (Bible §5, §6). The session ends at her counter, with the crew theme (Brief §5.1).

Nobody hired the crew. Nothing here is a job anyone offered them; it is the thing that happened to them, and the Series' first Paycheck job ([[investigation]]) is what Tally offers at the end of it.

## Hooks

None. The job opens with the lights already out. The crew do not choose to be in it; the first player action of the Series is taken inside Block Three of the Ledger with the mains cut and the doors hanging open ([[breakout-01-lights-out]]). The hook the book asks for (Core p. 292) is supplied in reverse: the crew's own themes — [[the-ledger]], [[saw-something-i-shouldnt]], the crew kits in `04-crew/` — are written to be *why they were here*, and the Flashback slots below are where those themes are voiced.

## Goal

Get out of the Ledger alive while the strike is still the loudest thing in the Outfall; cross three kilometers of causeway or the flats beside it; reach the coast road before the count is read again; and walk into the Gullet with the one name they are given.

## Structure

1. [[breakout-01-lights-out]] — the cold open: Block Three, the count half-read, the mains cut, the doors open, grey soft-shell coming through the wire. *The line security fails in a specific way: everything on the mains dies, and the causeway gate — on its own generator — stays down.*
2. [[breakout-02-hand-under-the-elbow]] — **core moment**: in the yard, against a hundred people running, the cell walks to Rook and takes him by the elbow. The crew see who is taken and how carefully, and understand none of it.
3. [[breakout-03-the-wire]] — the escape route: the shed row, the landing-stage post, the wire, the generator shed turning over; the strike force as weather, not rescue. Set piece on [[ledger-security-system]] and [[ledger-line-security]].
4. [[breakout-04-the-causeway]] — **climax**: the gate, the truck held on the far side, three kilometers of raised road with no cover, floodlights that may come back on, and the dark past them. First sight of the Under from the wrong end. [[escapee-list]] begins.
5. [[breakout-05-a-name-and-a-place]] — **core moment**: the coast road, the van with its door already open, and Rook's last words. Where he goes after them is the MC's, and both versions are written.
6. [[breakout-06-the-long-walk]] — the Sinks (or the Stretch) at four in the morning, the Wall on the horizon, a checkpoint at the Gullet's shore end.
7. [[breakout-07-the-counter-door]] — the Weighhouse at the second hour of the morning: Bohdan in the water door, Tally at the counter, food on the table, a figure already on the Slate — and the crew theme.

→ climax: [[breakout-04-the-causeway]] → denouement: [[breakout-07-the-counter-door]] → [[breakout-aftermath]].

## Core moments

- **The hand under the elbow** ([[breakout-02-hand-under-the-elbow]]). Prepared as a situation ready to go (Core p. 290): the yard, the torches, the flow of the crowd, the cell walking against it, and Rook — who has spent the whole night saying *stay behind me* — going with them without a word. What the crew see: *who* is taken, and *how* — undamaged, unhurried, by people who did not look at anyone else. What they do not see: why. Nothing in the scene, the Challenges, or the vectors supplies it (OQ-10, OQ-17, OQ-40).
- **A name and a place** ([[breakout-05-a-name-and-a-place]]). The van at the coast road. Rook says *Tally, the Weighhouse, in the Gullet — tell her Rook sent you*, and then he either gets into the van or is not there when they look back. Both are written; the Bible ends at the referral (OQ-39).

## Flashback design

**[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-139) Brief §7.1's GM decision — open in medias res, establish the crew by Flashbacks — is implemented as follows, using the book's Flashback rule as written (Core p. 125: a player asks to use their spotlight to flash back; they may take a player action whose outcome is revealed on return to the present, or the MC forgoes the roll and grants story tags or statuses from the narration).

- **Budget: two Flashbacks per PC in this job** (`flashback_budget: 2` above), plus one shared crew Flashback at the Weighhouse. A Consequence in this job may deny a PC their remaining Flashback (Core p. 125, the heist pattern: "they ran out of time … and now must rely on their resourcefulness").
- **Slots.** Every scene carries `flashback_hooks:` — prompts, never answers. Each prompt asks for one of four things, and the four are the whole of what a Flashback in this job may establish:
  1. **Crew bonds** — the first meal on the block; who took whose shift; who lied for whom to the count. A Flashback here creates or revises a crew relationship tag (Core p. 177) or a story tag between two PCs.
  2. **The work crew under Rook** — what he taught, what he stood between you and, what he never explained. A Flashback here grants a story tag about the Ledger's routine or about him as the crew knew him (*he showed me the shed door that sticks*, *he took the count for me once*). It may not establish anything about him that the crew did not see (his file's rule: Canon and Texture only).
  3. **The night you were sorted** — intake: the truck or the barge, the number read for the first time, the first person you spoke to. A Flashback here is a Discover about the Ledger's layout, its count, or its people, and may give the PC a tag from [[the-ledger]] as a story tag for the scene.
  4. **Why you were here** — what you were doing when you saw it, who was with you, whether the others know. **The content is the player's** (Bible §6; [[saw-something-i-shouldnt]]; OQ-12). The prompt asks *where, with whom, and what it cost*; it never asks *what* in a way the job answers. A Flashback here may fill a blank tag on [[saw-something-i-shouldnt]] as a story tag for the rest of the session.
- **What no Flashback may establish**, as a rule of this job rather than of the table: why Rook matters, what he is, whether he knew the strike was coming (OQ-10, OQ-17, OQ-40); why the Ledger held all five on one crew (OQ-13 — must not fill); what the Ledger produces beyond what a shift on the line would know (OQ-38); or that any PC knew Continuity was coming.
- **Timing.** Slots are offered where the scene files place them; a player may also ask for one anywhere the book allows. The two core moments carry no slot of their own — the present tense holds them — and the referral scene carries one that opens only after Rook is gone.

## The Ledger's product

**[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-140) Bible §5: the camp "produces something generic; the camp itself is not the mystery." WP4 left the quota board with a number on it and no product (OQ-38). This job proposes one line, and the MC may strike it and leave the blank: **the Ledger reclaims metal** — the sheds strip and sort what the barges and the dredgers bring in (sludge-dredged scrap, decommissioned hardware, returned and repossessed chrome with the tags cut out) into graded feedstock that leaves by truck for the Stack's foundry. It is exactly as boring as the remediation contract says it is; the shed toolkit ([[reclamation-worksheds-toolkit]]) is the whole of the technology; and no scene in this job turns on it.

## Ties to PC themes

Session one presses no Identity, Ritual, or Itch by design — the crew have not yet chosen the motivations the Series will test — but every Flashback slot is a theme being voiced. What the job does press, on every PC regardless of which of the five they are: **the Troubled Past** ([[the-ledger]], [[saw-something-i-shouldnt]]) in every scene inside the wire; **the persistent condition** in the dark — a Bloodware PC in a camp that never fed them iron, a Howler PC with no Leash, a Doppel PC whose switch or tracker may or may not still answer an AP&I reader, a Caster PC whose discipline has had no practice for months, a Baseline PC whose replacement part was camp-serviced or not serviced at all. Each is a story tag the player may take into scene one; none is required.

## Complications
