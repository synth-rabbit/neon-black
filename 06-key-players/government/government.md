# The Chancery

![[assets/key-players/government-scene.png]]

![[assets/key-players/government-emblem.png]]

**Role:** government · **Territory:** [[chancery-hill]] (seat) · [[ferrante-basin]] · [[kilbride-stretch]] · [[coldwater-outfall]] · **Street:** *the Chancery*, *the Hill* (BC-16)

## Base concept

The government of the Palisade Metropolitan Zone: a stone-and-glass administrative pile older than any tower on the Wall, on a low hill set back from the Crest's edge, ringed by the ministries, courts, registries, licensing bureaus, clubs, foundations, and law firms that live off it ([[chancery-hill]]). It exists, it is **deeply corrupt** (Bible §3), and on the Hill that is not a scandal but the business model: everything is for sale, the prices are known, and the rate card is updated quarterly.

The thing that makes the Chancery a Key Player rather than a joke is the sentence the Bible puts next to it: **corporate security forces are exactly as legal as the city police** (Bible §3). Not more legal — *exactly* as legal. A Meliora team and a Chancery patrol have the same standing to stop you, search you, shoot you, and file the report. Which means the Chancery has lost the monopoly that makes a government a government, and has been living, for two generations, off what remains.

What remains is the paperwork. The Chancery cannot make anyone do anything. It can decide who was allowed to.

## Twist

**Source:** invented — **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-122). The Bible fixes that the government exists, is deeply corrupt, and that corporate security is exactly as legal as the police. Everything below is this package's invention.

**The Chancery does not sell policy. It sells jurisdiction.**

Somewhere in the last fifty years the Hill worked out what it actually had left, and built a department around it: the **Office of Concurrent Jurisdiction**, four floors in the police annex, staffed by clerks who are the best-paid civil servants in Palisade. Where two lawful forces might both act — which, since the corporations' security is exactly as legal as the police, is everywhere — the Office decides, in advance, which one is the lawful one for this address, this window, this incident. It issues a **concurrence**: a numbered instrument saying whose report will be *the* report and whose account of the incident is the record. Nothing about it is secret. It is a public instrument with a published fee schedule and a waiting list, and every corporation in Palisade holds a standing account.

That is the Chancery's product, and it is why the Hill has grown richer every year that it has grown weaker. Continuity's rise did not threaten the Chancery. It added a fourth bidder to an auction that previously had three polite participants who had already agreed on the price ([[spine-and-clock]]). **The Chancery is the only Key Player in Palisade that benefits from the corporate war continuing** and does not want it resolved in anyone's favour.

**And the record has started to come apart.** A concurrence is a page, and the same window can be sold to two buyers who will never be in the same room — until the day they are. The registry, which holds Palisade's leases, licences, remediation contracts, and the corporations' escapee lists, is kept on paper *because paper cannot be Harnessed* ([[chancery-hill]]) and is therefore kept by people, and the people have been selling the same page twice for years: a lease that two companies hold, a name struck off a list that is still on the other copy, a remediation contract with two seals in different inks. Nobody has audited it and nobody can — there is no second copy to audit it against, and a real audit would establish that the Chancery's own record of Palisade has not been true for a decade.

The crew's names are somewhere in that registry, on AP&I's escapee list, worth no bounty (Bible §6). Whether they are on both copies is a question with a price.

## Resources

- **The Office of Concurrent Jurisdiction** — the allocation of lawfulness. A standing account, a fee schedule, a waiting list, and a duty desk that can be woken at three in the morning for a surcharge.
- **The registry** — leases, licences, permits, remediation contracts, court records, and the escapee lists the corporations file. On paper, in the Chancery's basement, held by clerks. Profiled as [[chancery-process]].
- **The Chancery police** — real, armed, underpaid, outnumbered on their own hill by corporate security, and carrying Orison sidearms under a service contract ([[corp-b|Orison Defense Systems]]). Their command sits in a modern annex bolted onto the back of the Chancery like an afterthought. At the Foot and under the Wall, they are what is left. Profiled as [[envelope-detail]].
- **The quarterly rate card** — the price of a permit, a ruling, a patrol's absence, a name struck off a list ([[chancery-hill]]).
- **Licensing and leases** — including AP&I's remediation lease on [[coldwater-outfall]], under whose "site security" clause Reconciliation Facility 4 exists (BC-24), and the quarantine order that keeps [[ferrante-basin]] sealed.
- **The courts** — and, more usefully, the sequence in which cases are heard. Nobody buys a verdict; they buy a date.
- **Public-safety broadcasts** — the Basin's official story, curfews, emergency statements, and the department that writes them ([[ferrante-basin]]).
- **Forged paper** — produced in the law firms' back offices and functionally identical to real paper, because the real paper was also bought ([[chancery-hill]]). The Chancery does not run this trade. It is what the Chancery's seals are now worth.
- **The lower gate** — the checkpoint where the Hill's road meets the Crest ring road and the public lift-head: police, "permit assistance" kiosks that are forgers, and a café where clerks take their envelopes over coffee.

## Agenda

**Keep four bidders in the room.**

Everything the Chancery does this Series follows from that. It will **not** name the war: a spokesman who says "war" turns a bidding auction into an emergency, and an emergency is a thing the corporations handle for free. It will **not** take a side: the moment the Hill is one corporation's client openly, the other three stop paying. It **will** keep raising the price of concurrence, extend the Office's hours, and quietly triple the surcharge for retroactive instruments — the ones bought after the incident, which are now the fastest-growing line on the rate card.

Below the mayor's floor there is a second agenda that nobody has articulated: the registry must not be audited. Every senior clerk knows the record has been double-sold, none of them knows how far it goes, and all of them understand that the first person to prove it will not be thanked.

And there is a third thing, smaller and more dangerous than either: [[halima-boyce]] has started refusing concurrences.

## What the upstart's destabilization means here

Every other Key Player is trying to survive the crack. The Chancery is *selling* it.

For fifty years the Hill was bought by three parties who had agreed among themselves what it should cost (Bible §3). Continuity broke that by paying more, in cash, for things the other three assumed were settled — retroactive concurrences, advance concurrences, a retainer with the police command. Prices on the rate card have tripled in two years and are still rising, because the value of being the lawful party in the room goes up every time two lawful parties end up in the same room ([[secret-war-goes-public]]).

So the Chancery's opportunity is simple and its risk is exactly the same thing. As long as the war stays private, jurisdiction is a commodity and the Hill is rich. The moment the war is public, the Chancery is *forced to speak* — and whatever it says, it either names the war, or is laughed at for not naming it, and both of those are the end of the auction. The clock the Bible confirms is, for this Key Player, a countdown to the day their product stops existing.
