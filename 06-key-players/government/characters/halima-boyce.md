# Halima Boyce

![[assets/npcs/halima-boyce.png]]

**Handle:** — · **Affiliation:** [[government|the Chancery]] · **Role in the pilot:** the reason the corporate team in the room is the lawful one, or is not.

## Who they are

Superintendent, Office of Concurrent Jurisdiction. Late forties, twenty-two years on the Chancery police, the last six behind a desk in the annex deciding which force is legally in a room when two forces could be. Physically unremarkable, verbally precise, and — in an institution where everything is for sale — technically clean: the Office's fees are public, they go to the Chancery, and she does not personally take envelopes. Everyone on the Hill considers this a charming affectation.

The Office is her instrument and she built most of it. When two lawful bodies might act in the same place, she issues a **concurrence**: a numbered page naming whose report becomes the record. Corporate security is exactly as legal as her own officers (Bible §3), so this is not a formality; it is the whole of the difference between an incident and a crime. She has issued thousands. She has never refused one.

What has changed is the traffic. [[upstart|Continuity]] now buys concurrences *in advance*, for districts where nothing has happened, in windows that have not started yet, and the other three have begun matching. Boyce has spent two years signing pages that say, in effect, *whatever happens at this address between these hours, this company's account of it is the truth*, and lately she has started reading them in order and noticing the shape they make. Twice this year she has issued two concurrences for the same block on the same night to two different companies, because that is what the fee schedule permits and nobody wrote a rule against it. She knows what will happen the night both of them use one.

She is not a reformer and has no illusions about the Chancery. She is a career officer who has realized she is the last functioning part of a government, and she is beginning — carefully, and without telling anyone — to use the only power she has: delay, split, and refuse.

## Vector

- **Want:** To know what she is authorizing before she authorizes it. To find out what happens in the windows she sells, which means finding people who do not work for the Hill and do not work for a corporation.
- **Push:** The Office's instruments — granting, delaying, splitting, or refusing a concurrence, which can make a corporate team lawful, unlawful, or merely late — and the Chancery police who still answer to her, including [[envelope-detail|the details that answer to somebody else as well]].

## What the crew knows / does not know

**Knows:** if a corporate crew came for them with paper already filed, somebody on the Hill signed it. **Does not know:** her name, until somebody sells it to them; that a concurrence can be split or delayed; that she has begun keeping her own copy of everything she signs, in a place that is not the registry.

## Challenge

None. Boyce does not fight and will not be in a fight — she is the reason the fight is legal. If a scene needs her, use Corporate Executive (Core p. 304) with *bribe* immune and *implicate 5*, and drop the bodyguards: her protection is that removing her creates a paperwork problem four corporations would rather not have.
