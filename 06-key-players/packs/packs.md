# The Run

![[assets/key-players/packs-scene.png]]

![[assets/key-players/packs-emblem.png]]

**Role:** packs · **Street:** *the Run*; a pack *runs*; *she's on the Run* · **Territory:** [[cinder-yards]] (turf), [[marlow-blocks]] (recruiting), [[lowmere-sinks]] (hunting)

## Base concept

Howlers organize in packs that work like gangs (Bible §2). In Palisade the culture calls itself **the Run** ([[names]] §2), and it holds the [[cinder-yards]]: two square kilometres of dead rendering plants, kiln-halls and cinder flats that nothing in the city wants. Three packs, six kiln-halls on the old rail siding, a kennel each, and one alpha whose word settles disputes across the Foot.

A Howler is what the Bible says: bio-sculpt and cybernetics, shifted by a chemical trigger, dependent on a syndicate for the supply (Bible §2). Under the Wall that means a chop-shop procedure on [[suture-row]], second-hand anchors, months of healing, and then a dose at the siding once a week for the rest of your life. It is expensive, irreversible, and rare — the Run counts its members in dozens, not hundreds, and every one of them was somebody the district could spare. **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-112)

**Publicly, none of this exists.** Palisade knows extreme modding as bodyguards, sculpted entertainers, and arena fighters (Bible §3), and it knows the Yards as gang ground. A pack is read from outside as a Patched-tier street gang with a stimulant problem and a bad reputation, and the corporations' cleanup crews keep it that way; what a witness saw gets written as a Baseline with a gun, or as nothing (Bible §2, §3; [[palisade|Palisade]]). The three packs know they are hidden. They do not know how much work is being done to hide them, or by whom.

**The three packs** — **the Smokes**, who hold the Kiln Row and whose alpha is the Run's arbiter; **the Sidings**, who hold the rail spur and take the delivery; and **the Nineteens**, who came out of Block Nineteen in [[marlow-blocks]] and grew from four to eleven in a year. They are not friends. They do not fight either, because there is exactly one counter in Palisade that sells what all three need and only one voice it listens to. **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-112)

## Twist

**Source:** invented — **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-110, BC-111).

The Run's self-understanding has two loads in it, and both are wrong.

**They think the dose is cooked.** Every pack believes Leash comes out of a lab somewhere under the Wall — a chemist, a bathtub operation, a Row basement — and that the [[syndicate|Almoners]] are middlemen who mark it up because they can. The belief is not stupid; it is what every drug under the Wall is, and the Almoners have never once contradicted it. **The packs do not know [[corp-a|Meliora Bioworks]] makes it.** They have never heard the word *Accord*, they do not know the compound has a catalogue number, and the idea that the thing they inject in a rendering shed is a surgical adjunct invoiced monthly to a licensed clinic is not a theory anyone in the Yards has ever had. **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-111 — the Masquerade holds at street level; the packs are inside it, not outside it.)

**They think they took this ground.** The Run's founding story is that the packs moved into the Yards a generation ago and held them against the city. What actually happened is that nothing in the Yards was worth a corporation's attention ([[cinder-yards]]) and that a great deal of quiet effort has gone into leaving them there — cleanup crews at first light, a supply that has never once failed for fifteen years, a price that moves with something other than scarcity, and a corporation upstairs whose entire interest is that the cohort keeps taking the dose and staying observable ([[corp-a]]). The packs are not outlaws. They are a field study with a turf.

**The alpha has noticed the wrong detail.** [[teodora-sowande]] — Chimney — has been taking the delivery for eleven years and has begun to say a thing out loud that nobody else in the Yards finds interesting: *the dose never changes.* Street chemistry varies. Batches are strong, weak, cut, bad; people die of a bad week. Leash has been identical for as long as anyone can remember, in every crate, at every siding, through two supply scares and a flood. She does not have the word *factory* and she does not have the word *Meliora*. She has a consistency that street chemistry cannot produce, and she has started, very carefully, asking who could.

She is right, and finding out is the most dangerous thing anyone in the Run could do — to herself, to the packs, and to a corporation that would rather close a study than explain one. **[OPEN]** (OQ-45) What the Run would do with the answer is not decided here.

## Resources

- **Three packs and the Kiln Row** — six brick kiln-halls on the siding, barricaded in scrap, a sentry on every roof ([[kiln-row]]).
- **The cinder flats after dark** — a kilometre of open ash that belongs to nobody human until dawn.
- **Recruits from the Blocks** — Marlow kids with nothing, offered the sculpt on the Almoners' credit and the first dose free ([[marlow-blocks]]).
- **A Row chop-shop that does sculpt on credit** — the procedure, the anchors, and no records ([[suture-row]]).
- **The Sinks as hunting ground** — across the levees at night, when the packs want somewhere with nobody in it that matters ([[lowmere-sinks]]).
- **Scrap and protection money** — the reclamation concern pays the packs not to be run off, which makes the Run the workers' landlord and the concern's security at once ([[cinder-yards]]).
- **A reputation that empties a street** — the Run's most valuable asset and the one it can spend without a dose.
- **Nobody official comes to the Yards** — no police, no corporate security, no census, no utility board.

## Agenda

Pack-level, the agenda is next week: the price, the delivery, the recruits, the ground. That is genuinely all most of the Run wants, and a Series can run for a year against nothing more complicated than a kennel that cannot make the price.

Above that sits the alpha's agenda, which is new and which nobody voted for: find out who makes the dose. Chimney's reasoning is not sentimental. A supply with one seller is a collar; a supply with a manufacturer is a *negotiation*, and if she can find the maker she can go around the Almoners, and if she can go around the Almoners the Run stops being collateral. She is not trying to free anyone. She is trying to renegotiate the terms of the leash, which is the most any of them have ever imagined.

She needs people to ask questions in places a Howler cannot go — up the Wall, in a clinic, in an office, on paper. That is a crew.

## What Continuity's destabilization means for us

Every faction smells opportunity (Bible §5), and for the Run the smell is simple: if the corporations are shooting at each other, nobody is watching the Foot. Turf that has been fixed for a generation is suddenly negotiable — the reclamation concern's yards, the siding, the Blocks' courtyards, the levee road into [[lowmere-sinks]]. The packs' instinct is to take ground, and one of them is already taking it.

The Run also has, for the first time, a market for the only thing it makes, which is fear. Continuity's crisis-response business needs crises; a pack that can empty a street on a schedule is a supplier. That road ends with the Run belonging to somebody on the Face instead of somebody in the Gullet, which Chimney can see and the Nineteens cannot.

And the crack makes the alpha's question urgent rather than idle. A corporate war means audits, seizures, closed lines, and a Meliora that may decide a fifteen-year study is a liability. The Run has never once considered what happens if the supply stops — not raised, *stopped*. Chimney has, and it is the reason she is willing to talk to strangers.
