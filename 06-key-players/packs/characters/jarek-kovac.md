# Jarek Kovač

![[assets/npcs/jarek-kovac.png]]

**Handle:** "Uncle" · **Affiliation:** [[packs|the Run]] (the Nineteens); recruits on the [[syndicate|Almoners']] credit · **Role in the pilot:** the vector face — the Run at the point where it touches ordinary people.

## Who they are

Thirty, and looks younger than the rest of the pack because he has been in it for two years rather than ten. He works the courtyards of [[marlow-blocks]]: the schools at turning-out time, the queue outside the Kitchen, the stairwell landings where kids sit because there is nowhere else. He buys drinks. He carries somebody's shopping up eleven floors when the lift is out. He knows names, mothers, birthdays, and who got laid off at the Stack this week, and he uses all of it. Mothers in the Blocks warn their children about **Uncle**, which he knows, and which he has learned to make a joke of before they can.

The offer is always the same and it is always made on a bad day: the sculpt on the Almoners' credit, the anchors from a chop-shop on [[suture-row]] that will do the work with no records, months of healing in Block Nineteen with people who will feed you, and **the first dose free**. What he says is *you'll be somebody*. What is true is that a Marlow kid with nothing becomes, in four months, the most physically capable person on their street, in a body they can never give back, paying a counter for the rest of their life ([[marlow-blocks]] development 3).

He believes it. That is what makes him dangerous and what makes him a good scene. He was one of those kids; the sculpt is the only good thing that ever happened to him; the pack is the only family he has had that stayed; and he will explain all of that to a crew with total sincerity while walking a fifteen-year-old toward a table. He does not experience himself as a recruiter. He experiences himself as the person who came back for people nobody came back for.

He is also frightened, in a way he does not examine. The Nineteens are the youngest pack, the alpha in [[cinder-yards]] does not respect them, and their standing in the Run is their growth. Uncle's quota is not a quota anyone set. It is the arithmetic of being the newest pack in a culture where the only currency is bodies. He took Continuity's second offer of money for a night's violence without telling [[teodora-sowande]] ([[packs]]), for exactly this reason.

## Vector

- **Want:** The twelfth member, and then the thirteenth. Standing for the Nineteens, and a reason not to be sent back to the Kiln Row with his hat in his hand.
- **Push:** The offer, made on the worst day of somebody's week, with the money already arranged and somebody's cousin ready to vouch. Against a crew he pushes the same way: he does not threaten, he *includes* — a place to sleep in Block Nineteen, a name at the Kitchen, an introduction to the alpha, and by the third favor the crew is on the ledger too.

## What the crew knows / does not know

Any crew that spends a week in [[marlow-blocks]] meets Uncle, and probably likes him. They will be told, by three separate people, not to.

He knows what he is and would never say it; the word for it is not used in the Yards. He believes the dose comes out of a lab somewhere under the Wall and has never wondered harder than that ([[packs]] twist). He does not know his pack's standing is measured by a corporation nobody has told him about, that the credit he arranges is a lien, or that he is the collection mechanism's front end. He does not know the alpha has been asking questions, and if he learned it, he would tell the Almoners — not to betray her, but because they are the people you tell things to. **[OPEN]** (OQ-45)
