# Tally's Weighhouse

![[assets/key-players/fence-network-scene.png]]

![[assets/key-players/fence-network-emblem.png]]

**Role:** fence-network (the tenth folder, BC-7/BC-18) · **Territory:** [[gullet-market]] (the Weighhouse) · [[suture-row]] · [[relay-fields]]

## Base concept

At the bottom of the Wall, where the escarpment's run-off channel meets the shore, the port keeps its old customs weighhouse: a stone box on the quay with a loading door on the water and a counter door on the market, a great brass balance under the roof that still works, and a back room that is an office, a kitchen and a vault. It has not been the Chancery's for two generations. It is **[[marisol-okonkwo|Marisol Okonkwo]]**'s, and everyone under the Wall calls her **Tally**.

She is a fence. The word undersells it. In [[gullet-market]] — where everything that falls off the Wall lands and everything the customs sheds did not see comes in by water — a fence with a counter that both sides trust is not a criminal service, it is **infrastructure**. Dockers bring what fell. Pawnbrokers bring what was pledged. Runners bring what was lifted. Chop-shops on [[suture-row]] bring what came out of somebody. Rig-builders in [[relay-fields]] bring what they built out of Gullet parts. It is weighed, priced, chalked on the Slate, and it moves.

Around the counter is the network: two dozen runners who know every cut-through in the Labyrinth, three pawnbrokers, two lock-keepers at Lock Three whose fee is Tally's to collect, four surgeons, a handful of rig-builders, and about a hundred people on the Foot who are owed something or owe something and are therefore reachable.

**And she is the crew's one contact.** [[tomas-adair|Rook]] gave them her name on the way out of the Ledger, and she is the only name they have (Bible §5, §6). The Series' second scene is five escapees walking into the Gullet with nothing.

## Twist

**Source:** invented — **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-102), per Brief §6.4. It contradicts nothing in the Bible, and it is written specifically so that **[OPEN]** (OQ-11) — whether the fence connects to Continuity — stays undecided in both directions.

**Tally does not make her money on resale. She makes it on knowing.**

The margin on fenced goods under the Wall is thin and everyone in the Gullet knows it. Tally's margin is elsewhere. Everything that crosses the brass balance is weighed, described, and entered — what it was, what it weighed, who brought it, what they said about where it came from, and what it actually turned out to be. Twenty years of that is the **Book**: the most complete record in Palisade of what has physically moved under the Wall, and therefore of what the Wall has been losing, from where, and when.

The goods are how she samples the city. The Book is the product.

What the Book buys her:

- **Provenance, sold to people who need it.** An insurance forensics contractor who has to prove where a shipment went. A corporate purchasing agent who needs to know whether the thing on offer is genuinely off a Meliora line or a Suture Row copy. A collector on the Crest who wants a Tao-touched object with a history attached ([[gullet-market]] development 3, **[TAO-REINTERPRETED]**). None of them buys the object from her. They buy the *page*.
- **Pattern.** She knew the Bottom Bays' giveaway ring existed a year before AP&I's security office did, because chrome with tags that were present and not answering came across her balance eleven times ([[amalgam-stack]]).
- **Leverage that never has to be used.** Half the Foot has brought her something they should not have. She has never once mentioned it, which is why they keep coming.
- **The percentage.** Everything that clears through the Weighhouse leaves a fraction behind, and the fraction is not really the money.

The Book is paper. Bound ledgers on the back room's shelves, twenty years deep, in her handwriting, with the last two years mirrored — badly, incompletely — onto a rig built in the Relay Fields, because [[dessa-rahimi]] convinced her that paper burns. It has never been read by anyone but Tally.

## Agenda

- **Stay the clearing house.** Neutrality is the asset. The Almoners take a cut in the Gullet, the Cutloose pass through it, corporate agents come down to it in bad disguises, and none of them owns the counter. As the war comes down the Wall ([[secret-war-goes-public]]) that gets harder every notch, and Tally intends to be the last neutral counter on the Foot.
- **Grow the Book.** Every crew she places is a crew whose work comes back across the balance.
- **Keep the Foot owing her.** Not cruelly. Structurally.
- **Find out who has started buying provenance in bulk.** In the last eighteen months somebody has been buying pages — through three different intermediaries, in categories that do not sit together: corporate shipping losses, chrome with dead tags, and things nobody can explain. Tally has not identified the buyer and has not stopped selling. **[OPEN]** (OQ-37), and see OQ-11 below.

## Resources

- **The Weighhouse.** Two doors, a brass balance, a back room, and a reputation that means nobody bleeds inside it. See [[the-weighhouse]].
- **The Book**, and the badly-mirrored copy on a Relay Fields rig.
- **Runners.** Two dozen, mostly young, mostly Patched, who move goods and messages through the Labyrinth faster than anything on wheels. [[dessa-rahimi]] is the best of them.
- **The water door.** Lock Three's keeper, a known tariff, and a coaster berth that does not appear on a customs manifest ([[gullet-market]]).
- **Surgeons and rig-builders.** [[suture-row]] for bodies, [[relay-fields]] for hardware.
- **The Slate.** A chalk column by the counter door listing who owes the house what, in public, by name. Nobody has ever rubbed one out without paying.
- **Muscle.** [[bohdan-adeyemi]] and the water-door crew — dockers, not soldiers, and enough. See [[weighhouse-muscle]].
- **The back room.** A bed, a table, and a hot plate: the cheapest lodging in Palisade, at a price the crew will be paying for years.

## What the destabilization means here

The Wall shedding is what the Gullet lives on, and a corporate war is the Wall shedding harder than it has in fifty years.

Every notch of [[secret-war-goes-public]] puts more through her doors: corporate stock moved in a hurry, security contractors buying off-manifest, whole shipments rerouted around a cordon, people with a bag and no papers, and — the profitable part — an enormous appetite for *provenance*, because in a war everybody needs to prove where something came from and nobody trusts the corporation that says so. The stalemate held the Weighhouse to a counter under the Wall. The crack offers Tally the chance to become the thing the Foot has never had: a clearing house the Crest has to come down to.

It also offers the obvious risk, and she has said it out loud once, to nobody in particular: *the last neutral counter in a war is the most valuable object on the map, right up until somebody decides to own it.*
