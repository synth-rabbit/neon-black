# Tally's Weighhouse — Membership

What a PC in [[fence-network|Tally's network]] looks like. The Tokyo pattern (Tokyo p. 166–167). **This is the crew's default position from the end of session one**: the Bible gives them exactly one contact and no institutional standing (§6), and the network is the standing they get instead.

## Getting in

There is no joining. There is a balance, and there is the **Slate**.

You come in the counter door with something, or you come in with nothing and a name — which is how the crew arrive, on [[tomas-adair|Rook]]'s referral (Bible §5). Tally weighs what you brought, or she weighs you. She feeds you. She names a figure and writes it in chalk by the door where the whole Gullet can read it, and from that moment you are in the network, because being on the Slate *is* being in the network.

What follows is graduated and nobody announces it:

1. **On the Slate.** You owe the house. You can buy, sell, sleep in the back room, and be pointed at a surgeon who will not ask.
2. **Carrying.** You are handed a chit — paper, thumbprint, wax — and asked to take it somewhere. Nobody explains what carrying a chit means. It means you are trusted with a route.
3. **Clearing.** Your work comes back across the balance as a matter of course. Tally places jobs for you rather than to you. Both of the Series' multi-session jobs are hired through her or her network (Brief §7.2).
4. **Standing.** A line gets wiped that you did not pay. This has happened four times in twenty years.

## What the house expects

- **Everything clears through the counter.** Not exclusivity — accounting. Selling around the Weighhouse is not punished, it is *noticed*, and being noticed is the whole economy of the Foot.
- **Nobody bleeds inside.** The one rule, enforced by the entire market rather than by Tally ([[the-weighhouse]]).
- **You answer the question about where it came from.** Mildly asked, over food, and it goes in the Book.
- **Paper, not Nearspace.** Instructions move on chits. A runner who is stopped can eat one; a Harnessed message cannot be eaten ([[dessa-rahimi]]).
- **The Slate is public.** What you owe is chalked where your neighbors can read it. That is not humiliation, it is collateral, and it is why the house does not need collectors.

## What it gives you

The only thing the crew have on day one and the reason they survive to session two: a fence who will buy anything, a bed in the back room, papers that pass at a lift, a surgeon on [[suture-row]] who does not file, a berth at Lock Three, rig-builders in [[relay-fields]], two dozen runners who know every cut-through in the Labyrinth, and — the real product — access to somebody who knows what has physically moved under the Wall for twenty years and will tell you a piece of it for a price.

And neutral ground. As [[secret-war-goes-public]] advances, that gets rarer and more valuable every notch.

## Which kit

**Self kit:** [[weighhouse-credit]] *(Assets or Affiliation)* — Brief §4.4 lists "the fence's network (Assets or Affiliation)". Expected shape: power tags around the counter, the runners, the surgeons, the lock-keepers, a name that is good in the Gullet, and knowing what a thing is actually worth; weakness tags around the Slate, the percentage, being known to be into the house, and the fact that the house records everything you bring it.

**Existing kits that fit beside it** (Brief §0.1 — offer both):
- **Heist Gear** or **Guns & More Guns** (Assets, Core p. 202 range) — the network as a supply line.
- **Street Gang** (Affiliation, Core p. 199) re-flavored as the runners, for a PC who came up carrying chits.
- **Escaped Servitude** (Troubled Past, Core p. 218) — every PC in this Series has a claim on it; WP3's camp kit is the custom counterpart.
- **Investigator** (Expertise, Core p. 207 range) — the Book is a research asset for a PC who can get a page out of it.
- Compare **Ghost Market Vendor** (Tokyo p. 167) for the shape of a fence-affiliation kit: *access to the market*, *expert haggler*, *appraise rare gear*, against *fear of losing membership*. The Weighhouse version replaces the fear of losing membership with the Slate, which is worse, because you cannot lose it.

**Specials.** Megacity-style, Tokyo p. 72–74 pattern, condition-gated, prerequisite required, surviving theme replacement (Brief §2.4). Candidates for WP2/WP3:
- **On the Slate** — prerequisite: a figure chalked by the counter door. Once per session you may take something you cannot pay for — a price, a bed, a berth, a surgeon — immediately and without a roll. You take *owed-2*.
- **Carrying** — prerequisite: trusted with chits. In [[gullet-market]] you always know a route out of where you are, and the runners will not sell you.
- **What It's Actually Worth** — prerequisite: time behind the counter. When you handle an object, you learn one true thing about where it came from.

## What the network does not get you

Anything above the Foot. The Weighhouse's reach ends at the tier lifts: it buys from the Crest and sells to the Crest and has no standing on it. And the Book is not the crew's — nobody has read it but Tally, and the tier-6 Limit on [[marisol-okonkwo-challenge]] is there to keep it that way.

**MC only.** **[OPEN]** (OQ-11) Whether the house has a relationship with [[upstart|Continuity]] is the GM's and is unanswered in every file in this folder; **[OPEN]** (OQ-37) somebody has been buying provenance in bulk for eighteen months and Tally does not know who. A PC deep in the network is the most likely person to find out, which is the correct place for that thread to live.
