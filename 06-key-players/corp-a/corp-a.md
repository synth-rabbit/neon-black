# Meliora Bioworks

![[assets/key-players/corp-a-scene.png]]

![[assets/key-players/corp-a-emblem.png]]

**Role:** corp-a · **Specialty:** bio (BC-8, GM-approved) · **Street:** *Meliora*, *Mel* · **Territory:** [[meliora-terraces]] (HQ), [[aurelian-crest]], [[suture-row]]

## Base concept

Meliora Bioworks grows bodies. Tissue, organs, gene work, the pharmaceutical lines that supply half the clinics on the Wall, and — the part everyone actually means — the sculpting suites, where enough money buys a body built to a drawing. One of the Big Three, with fingers in every pie and one thing it is known for (Bible §3): the flesh half of the ladder. Meliora does not sell chrome; it sells the thing chrome is bolted to. Its campus of stepped greenhouse towers glows green above the Crest all night ([[meliora-terraces]]), its name means *better things*, and the friendlier the name the colder the company ([[style-guide]] §1.1).

The corporation's public argument is that it is the only one of the three that makes people, rather than parts. Its internal argument is narrower: a sculpt is a manufacturing process with a defect rate, and the defect is the patient's own body refusing the work. Everything below follows from Meliora solving that.

## Twist

**Source:** invented — **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-108). This is the vault's answer to *what the trigger is really for* (**[OPEN]** OQ-3, authorized invention per Brief §6.4). The GM may overrule it; nothing outside `06-key-players/corp-a/`, `syndicate/`, and `packs/` depends on the mechanism.

**Accord.** A body that has been substantially re-grown does not simply heal. Sculpted tissue arrives underdetermined — it knows it is meant to be something and not yet what — and left to itself it scars, reverts, or turns cancerous. Meliora's answer is a settling series it sells to its own theaters and to licensed clinics under the catalog name **Accord**: one dose, on the table, under the suite's template, opens a remodeling window of about forty minutes in which the new tissue converges on the drawing and then locks. The client goes under unfinished and wakes finished. It is not a drug in Meliora's mind. It is a surgical adjunct, invoiced by the case, and the third-largest line the Spire's basement runs.

**What it does off the table.** Accord does not carry a shape. It opens the window; the template decides what fills it. In a Patched body — black-market sculpt grown from cheap animal-derived stock, cut by a chop-shop on [[suture-row]], hung on second-hand cybernetic anchors that can only take load along the axes they were built for — there is no template and no supervision. The window opens anyway. The tissue converges on the only attractors available to it: the animal templates latent in the stock it was grown from, and the geometry the frame can actually bear. The body goes to the nearest shape it can hold and holds it until the compound clears. That is the shift the Bible describes (§2 werewolf) — bio-sculpt plus cybernetics plus a chemical trigger — and it is why all three are required. Sculpt without chrome tears itself apart; chrome without sculpt has nothing to remodel; either without Accord never finishes at all.

**The dependency.** An unsettled sculpt is not stable. It keeps trying to converge and cannot, and it degrades — slowly at first, then not slowly. A dose settles it for a night; the night after, it is unsettled again. This is the leash in **Leash** ([[names]] §2), and it is why the Run is a customer and not a market.

**The second floor.** Meliora's Settling Sciences division has known about the leak for its whole existence and has not closed it, because the leak is the study. A dose given once, on a table, under supervision, tells Meliora nothing about what Accord does to a body given twice a week for eleven years. The packs do — and no ethics board on the Wall would ever approve the trial that produces that data. The program that reads it is called **the Long Settle**, and its objective is a settling agent that can be dosed repeatedly on a living adult without the body coming apart. If it works, a Meliora client stops buying one body for life and starts renting a new one every few years. The ladder becomes a subscription. **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-108)

**What Meliora does not do.** It does not make Howlers on purpose, direct the packs, own the Almoners, or hold the Masquerade for them; the corporations' cleanup of Howler incidents (Bible §3) is a separate, older, unglamorous obligation that Meliora shares with the other two and resents. **[OPEN]** (OQ-43) Whether anyone at Meliora has ever settled a body into a Howler shape *deliberately*, with a template, is not answered here.

- **The pharmaceutical lines** under the Verdant Spire ([[meliora-terraces]]) — Accord among them, in a bay with its own airlock and its own headcount.
- **The sculpting suites and their templates** — the drawings are the valuable part; the compound without a template is only a window.
- **Sterile Field Units** — Meliora's bio-containment response, deployed under [[the-sterile-field]]. Under the Wall they are called *soap*.
- **Meliora Security and enclave law** — inside the Terraces the laws are Meliora's and the Chancery has no say (Core p. 41).
- **Warranty clinics on [[suture-row]]** — the legitimate face of the aftercare trade, and the reason a Meliora badge opens doors on the Foot.
- **The variance file** — fifteen years of dosing records, morgue photographs, and cleanup reports on the Run, cross-indexed by pack. It is the most valuable object Meliora owns that has no line in the annual report.
- **The Chancery's regulatory affairs** — a payroll, not a relationship ([[chancery-hill]]).
- **A standing aftercare supply contract** with a licensed relief clinic under the Wall. See [[syndicate|the Almoners]].

## Agenda

Meliora wants the Long Settle licensed and shipping while the other two corporations are looking at [[upstart|Continuity]]. That requires three things, in order: enough variance data to write a submission; a regulatory environment distracted enough to approve it; and the leak to stay exactly where it is — large enough to keep producing data, small enough that nobody follows the invoice back up the Wall. Everything Meliora does under the Wall this Series is one of those three, and the corporation's characteristic move is to solve a street problem by *funding* it rather than ending it: a clinic, a grant, a relief contract, a very reasonable person in a good coat.

Its second, slower want is a lane. The civil agreements (Bible §3) fixed what each of the three sells, and Meliora's lane is one body per customer per lifetime. A subscription body is a different business, and asking for it means reopening the agreements — which is the one thing the three have not done in fifty years ([[timeline]]).

## What Continuity's destabilization means for us

The stalemate kept Meliora in its lane, and the lane is the problem: one body, one lifetime, one sale. The crack is the first chance in fifty years to argue that the lanes should be redrawn, and Meliora intends to arrive at that argument holding a product the other two cannot answer.

Nearer than that, a crisis is cheap data. Displaced people, unregistered bodies, cleanup contracts, emergency clinics, a district nobody is counting — every one of those is a place Settling Sciences can run something it could not run in a licensed theater, and every one of them arrives with the Chancery grateful rather than curious. Meliora's executives call this *the widening* and mean it warmly.

The risk runs the other way. The thing a rival most wants right now is leverage, and Meliora's leverage is one invoice long. If [[upstart|Continuity]] — or AP&I, looking for something to trade after the Ledger — pulls the aftercare contract into the light and walks it down the Wall to a relief kitchen and from there to a kennel in [[cinder-yards]], the Long Settle stops being a program and becomes a scandal with fifteen years of documentation attached.
