# Continuity Risk & Response

![[assets/key-players/upstart-scene.png]]

![[assets/key-players/upstart-emblem.png]]

**Role:** upstart · **Street:** *Continuity* · **Territory:** [[the-lattice]] (HQ, Continuity Plaza) · [[relay-fields]] · [[chancery-hill]] · [[kilbride-stretch]]

## Base concept

Continuity Risk & Response sells the hour after. Not insurance — insurance pays out when it is over. Continuity is the firm whose vans are at the cordon before the Chancery's fire service has finished dispatching, whose people wear matte soft-shell with no logo on it and a serial number where a name would be, and whose contracts are signed by tenants and boards and district committees at the worst ten minutes of their year, in the language of the trade: *for the duration of the emergency*. Security. Crisis management. Hacking. Three lines of business that in Palisade are one business, because whoever secures a site owns what is in it, and whoever restores a Domain has been inside it (Bible §3).

It is best known for those three. It also has fingers in a dozen smaller pies to a lesser extent — executive protection, insurance forensics, data recovery, event security, disaster logistics, the boring profitable middle of a city that breaks constantly — and none of them is why anyone is afraid of it.

Ten years ago Continuity was a crisis-response contractor with four vans ([[timeline]]). Three years ago the Lattice burned, and Continuity's cells came into the data ward through the plaza gates and the cooling shafts at once, put the fire out, and did not leave; the ward is still under a continuity-of-service contract and the emergency has still not been declared over. That was the first act of Continuity's the street noticed, and the civil agreements had nothing to say about it, because the Lattice belonged to none of the Big Three. Today the Big Three keep their Domains in halls Continuity guards, and pay it to do so.

It is a **credible challenger and still too weak to take on any one of the three alone** (Bible §3). Everything below follows from that single fact.

## Agenda

Power. An equal fourth seat, or one of the three chairs, or eventually all three subjugated — the end state is deliberately not fixed (Bible §5; [[spine-and-clock]]). What is fixed is the order of operations: **the three must be weakened first**, and Continuity cannot do it in a straight fight. So it does what a security-and-crisis firm can do that an army cannot.

- **Manufacture the emergency, sell the cure.** A cooling failure, a Domain outage, a breach, a hostage situation, a labor action that turns. Continuity does not have to cause every one; it has to be first at enough of them that being first stops looking like luck. What the emergency leaves behind — a ward, a contract, a monitor in a tenant's Nearspace — it keeps.
- **Sit inside the other three.** Its clients are its targets. A firm that restores your Domain has walked your Domain. A firm that guards your executive knows their schedule. The three know this and buy the service anyway, because the alternative is buying it from each other.
- **Pick at the agreements.** The civil agreements are what keep the three from spending themselves on each other (Bible §3). Continuity's whole method is to arrange situations in which one of the three must either break an agreement or eat a loss in public. Either outcome is a good quarter.
- **Take what nobody has agreed about.** The Lattice was nobody's. The Relay Fields are nobody's. The Chancery is anybody's who bids. Continuity grows in the gaps the three left because the three agreed there was nothing in them.
- **This Series:** it struck the Ledger — [[coldwater-outfall|Reconciliation Facility 4]], AP&I's secret camp — and got what it went for. The general breakout was cover for extracting one person ([[tomas-adair|Tomas Adair, "Rook"]]) (Bible §5). The follow-through is not a second strike; it is silence, and the arithmetic of that silence: every day [[corp-c|AP&I]] does not say the word *Continuity* out loud is a day AP&I is admitting it cannot say what it lost, or from where.

## Resources

- **Crisis-response cells.** Six to eight people, soft-shell armor, breaching frames, medical kit, and the legal standing of police (Bible §3) — corporate security is exactly as legal as the city's. A cell arrives, declares a scene, and holds it. See [[continuity-crisis-response-cell]].
- **The Lattice.** Continuity Plaza and the server halls behind it: the physical home of most of Palisade's Domains, including the Chancery's, the banks', and the Hoods of half the Face ([[the-lattice]]). Held by contract, guarded by cells, watched in the Nearspace of every gallery.
- **The Cold Suite.** Harnessed intrusion cells working out of the plaza tower's cooled floors — Harnessing as a staffed service rather than a stunt (Brief §6.5; Core p. 22). See [[the-cold-suite]].
- **Monitors.** Every tenant contract includes them. They are disclosed, itemized, and billed for. Nobody reads the schedule.
- **A recruiting pipeline.** A crisis-response office in a coffin-block ground floor in [[relay-fields]], a Harnessing suite nicer than any in the district, and a standing offer to any kid who cracks something they should not have.
- **Paper.** Continuity-of-service contracts, retainers, the Chancery utility board's signature, and a legal department that has never lost an argument about what *the duration of the emergency* means.
- **The smaller streams.** Unglamorous, constant, and the reason the firm can absorb a bad month that would kill a rival.

## Motifs

- **Blue light and cold.** Server-hall blue, and a cold that gets into the teeth. Continuity's spaces are all the same temperature.
- **A lobby like a hospital.** Continuity Plaza's ground floor: soft chairs, quiet staff, a coffee machine, and a triage desk. Clients arrive frightened and leave reassured.
- **The van with the door already open.** Matte grey, no livery beyond a serial, parked where it was not parked yesterday.
- **No logo, only a number.** Cell personnel carry a serial on the chest plate and nothing else. Nobody at a cordon has ever learned a responder's name.
- **The phrase.** *For the duration of the emergency.* Printed on the contract, said at the cordon, and never followed by a date.
- **Clean hands, and a pen.**

## What the destabilization means here

Continuity is the crack (Bible §5), and it still gets to profit from it like everyone else. The opportunity it smells is not the strike; the strike is done. It is the **silence after it**.

A Big Three corporation's secret facility was hit, emptied of a hundred-odd bodies, and lost the one that mattered — and AP&I cannot report it, because reporting it means admitting the Ledger. Every hour of that silence is proof, to the only audience Continuity cares about, that the three's agreements are a paper the three will not spend a reputation to enforce. That proof is saleable: to the Chancery, as a reason to take a fourth bidder seriously; to Meliora and Orison, as a reason to buy more security from the only firm that has demonstrated it can move on a rival's site and not be named; and inside the firm, as the argument for the next one. Continuity does not need the war it is starting to be public yet ([[secret-war-goes-public]]). It needs the three to keep not talking to each other, and it has just proved they will.

## MC only — the twist

**Continuity is Bloodware-run** (Bible §2 vampire secret; §5 the secret). Within Bloodware society, anyone with power or sway knows; street-level and newly made Bloodware do not. To the corporations, the Ferrante strain is still a recurring disease that flares up and occasionally produces a clan noteworthy enough to justify wiping it out (Bible §2). Bloodware canon applies without exception: the weaknesses are **electromagnetic effects** and the **master node — the Kernel**, and no others; they need blood or iron because the nanites replicate with iron; the body is progressively nanite-rebuilt.

What that changes about everything above:

- **The patience is not corporate.** Ten years is a quarter's worth of planning to a board that does not age. *The duration of the emergency* is a joke told in a room where nobody is in a hurry.
- **The firm is the clan's institution.** The Bible's "clan noteworthy enough to justify wiping it out" is exactly what a corporation with cells, contracts, and the Chancery's signature is for: it makes the flare-up look like a business, and businesses are not exterminated, they are litigated.
- **The strike on the Ledger was for one person** (Bible §5), and Rook is Bloodware. Who inside the firm knew that, and who was told only a name, is the seam this Key Player is built on: [[hanne-oyelaran]] was told a name; [[rosalind-ekwueme]] knows what the firm is.
- **[OPEN]** (OQ-17) What it costs, and to whom, if this becomes known, is not discussed by the Bible and is not decided here. The public-war clock ([[secret-war-goes-public]]) makes the *corporate* war public and leaves this secret standing.

**Standing, corrected.** Toward [[corp-c]]: not a rivalry, an extraction — AP&I held one of theirs for reasons AP&I never understood. Toward [[tao-society]]: the only Key Player whose reach Continuity cannot audit, and the reason the wellness-contractor line item is a live internal question. Toward [[packs]] and [[changeling-cells]]: both are Masquerade-adjacent populations the corporations already clean up after (Bible §3), which makes them cover — a Howler frenzy or a Doppel's disappearance is a story the corporations tell themselves instead of the true one.
