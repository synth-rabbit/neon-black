# Corin Alvarez

![[assets/npcs/corin-alvarez.png]]

**Handle:** "Tuesday" · **Affiliation:** [[changeling-cells|the Cutloose]] · **Role in the pilot:** the person somebody is hunting, and the person somebody wants to buy.

## Who they are

Eleven days out. She took her handle the way most escapees do — the day she cut (BC-124) — and she still says it as though testing whether it belongs to her.

Physically: mid-twenties in appearance, which means nothing; unremarkable, which means everything, because an unflagged face is the most valuable thing anybody in Gallery Nine owns. Two surgical sites, both recent, both still taped: the tracker at the shoulder blade and the switch at the base of the skull. Her kill switch and tracker are out — the Sump did both in one session because there was no second chance to get her back up the stair (Bible §2: escapees removed their switches and trackers). The work was fast, it was not gentle, and she is not well. She sleeps badly, she flinches at the pump alarms, and she has not yet stopped waiting to be told what to do.

Before that she was a corporate special unit's asset: infiltration and impersonation work at a level that means she has been in rooms most of Palisade does not know exist. She remembers a fortnight of rotations, three clearances, a corridor, and a technician's voice. She does not remember a logo, and this is not modesty — she genuinely does not know who owned her, which is the ordinary condition of everyone on the Wall of Faces.

Six has her on the list, near the top, for a clean identity she cannot yet afford. Until then she is a woman in a concrete hall who is worth money to four different parties and knows it.

## Vector

- **Want:** To stop being findable. Under that, hard and unwise: to make somebody in Palisade say out loud who built her.
- **Push:** A face nobody has flagged; two weeks of a special unit's interior in her head; and a willingness to spend both badly, because she has been out for eleven days and has no idea what anything is worth.

## What the crew knows / does not know

**Knows:** somebody new is in the Galleries, hurt, and the recovery traffic under the Wall has doubled since she arrived. **Does not know** — she herself: who owned her; whether the face she is wearing is one they will look for; that what she remembers about clearances is worth more than her surgery, to buyers she would not want to meet.
