# Ferrante Basin

![[assets/districts/ferrante-basin.png]]

**Zone:** 63-64 · **Tier:** Under · **Pillar:** Cities in Ruins · **Caste band:** none (squatters at the wall: Stock) · **Story tag:** *don't touch anything*

## Central concept

A shallow bowl in the lowland east of the Sinks, four kilometers across, ringed by a concrete wall twelve meters high with a razor-crown and a Chancery watchtower every three hundred meters: the **Basin**, where **Ferrante Nanoscale** had its campus a century ago and where, a century ago, Ferrante's nanites got out ([[names]] §2; Bible §2). What is inside the wall is what the nanites left: a district digested. Buildings eaten to lace. Roads that are grey dust. A landscape the color of iron filings, where nothing grows and the drones that patrol it come back with their casings pitted. The company no longer exists (Bible §2); its name is a curse — *a Ferrante flare-up*, *the Ferrante strain* — and its campus is the second wall in a city named for one.

The Basin is the sidelined pillar's one district — Cities in Ruins (Core p. 146; [[pillars]]) — and the book's Secured Site of the kind that keeps things *in* (Core p. 43). The Chancery holds it, in the sense that the Chancery's watchtowers ring it and the Chancery's broadcasts explain it. Nobody lives inside. Along the outside of the wall, in the shadow of the towers, a fringe of Stock squatters lives on what the drones drop and what the wall's maintenance crews leave.

**What the public knows and what it does not.** The outbreak is public history: the worst industrial disaster in Palisade's memory, a nanite accident, a company destroyed, a district lost, a wall built. What the outbreak *produced* is not public. The Ferrante strain made Bloodware — transmissible nanites, iron-hungry, a body rebuilt over a lifetime ([[names]] §1; Bible §2) — and the corporations that took over the cleanup regard it as a disease that flares up and occasionally produces a clan worth wiping out (Bible §2). The public knows the word *flare-up* and thinks it means a nanite leak. The Basin is not where Bloodware live; it is where they came from, and nothing here places them anywhere (Plan WP1).

## Developments

1. **Backfired Tech — the outbreak** (Cities in Ruins, chosen) — "Nanites broke loose and digested the district into a delirious hellscape" (Core p. 165). Roughly a century ago (Bible §3 timeline): a hacked or failed experiment at Ferrante's campus — which, nobody now agrees (Bible §2: "hacked or failed") — released a self-replicating strain that ate the district's iron and everything the iron was in. The campus was gone in a week. The Basin was walled in a season. Ferrante Nanoscale was gone within the year, dissolved, bought, or buried — the records say all three (Bible §2: the corporation no longer exists). See [[timeline]].
2. **Adaptation Attempts — the barrier** (Cities in Ruins, chosen) — "A giant barrier is erected to separate this place from the Megacity" (Core p. 165). The quarantine wall is the Chancery's oldest public work still standing and its only one that is maintained: patched, watched, and paid for by a levy the three corporations have never disputed. It keeps the Basin in. It keeps the squatters out. What it does not keep out is the wind, which carries the Basin's grey dust across the Sinks on a dry day, and the Sinks' families wash it off their nets with vinegar and do not ask why.
3. **Government Overreach — the broadcasts** (Oligarchic Corruption, roll 63-36-44-3) — "Political propaganda and brainwashing becomes inescapable" (Core p. 168). The Basin has a voice: the Chancery's public-safety broadcasts, on loudspeakers along the wall and in the AR of every district under the Wall — *Do not approach the barrier. Do not handle recovered material. Report contamination. The Ferrante Basin is contained.* — repeated so long that the words have worn smooth and everyone under the Wall can recite them. The official story is airtight: an accident, a company, a wall. The story has one job, which is to be the only story, and the corporations pay the Chancery to keep telling it.

## Who holds ground here

- [[government|The Chancery]] — the wall, the towers, the broadcasts, the levy. The Basin is the Chancery's one uncontested responsibility and its most profitable.
- Nobody inside. The corporations' interest in the Basin is a cleanup interest and a secret one; nothing here places a corporate presence visibly.

## Places

- **The quarantine wall** — Twelve meters of concrete, four kilometers around, watchtowers, drone gantries, the loudspeakers, the maintenance crews' gates that open twice a year. The book's Secured Site (Core p. 43) turned inside out: getting *in* is a heist; getting something *out* is the only reason anyone would.
- **The Lace** — Inside the wall, the ruin of Ferrante's campus: towers eaten to open lattice, a research block whose walls are gone and whose glass still stands, ground that is grey dust to the depth of a man, and a silence with no Nearspace in it at all. A Fringe Zone at the end of the world. Drones patrol it. Their casings come back pitted.
- **Towerside** — The squatters' shanty along the wall's outer foot, under the watchtowers, on the Sinks' side: tarps, salvage, a market for what the drones drop, and old people who remember their grandparents' stories about the week the campus vanished. A Living Space for anyone who wants to be near the Basin without being in it, and the place where the broadcasts are loudest.

## Hooks

- A Weighhouse buyer will pay a fortune for anything from inside the Lace, and does not say what he expects it to still be doing.
- A Towerside elder has a story about the outbreak that is not the broadcast's story, and a Chancery inspector has come down to hear it.
- A maintenance gate is opening this week, and the corporation whose crew is going in is not one of the Big Three.
