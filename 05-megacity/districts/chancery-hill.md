# Chancery Hill

![[assets/districts/chancery-hill.png]]

**Zone:** 13-14 · **Tier:** Crest · **Pillar:** Oligarchic Corruption · **Caste band:** Sculpted · **Story tag:** *everything has a price list*

## Central concept

The seat of the Palisade Metropolitan Zone's government: a low hill on the Crest, a little back from the edge, crowned by the Chancery — a stone-and-glass administrative pile older than any tower on the Wall — and ringed by the institutions that live off it. Ministries, courts, the registry, the licensing bureaus. Members' clubs. Foundations. Law firms with corporate retainers and no other clients. The street calls the government **the Chancery** after the building, and means it the way you would name a shop (**[BUILD CHOICE]**, BC-16).

The city government exists and is **deeply corrupt** (Bible §3). On the Hill that is not a scandal; it is the business model. Everything is for sale and the prices are known — a permit, a ruling, a patrol's absence, a name struck off an escapee list — and the buyers are the three corporations who long ago agreed what each would pay, and now a fourth who has been outbidding them ([[spine-and-clock]]). The Chancery's police are real and armed and exactly as legal as the corporate security that outnumbers them on their own hill (Bible §3).

Around the Chancery, the Hill is Sculpted and quiet: colonnades, plane trees under grow-lamps, doormen who are visibly more than human. It is the most respectable address in Palisade. That is why the Wuji keep a house here.

## Developments

1. **Bureaucratic Dysfunction — kleptocracy** (Oligarchic Corruption, chosen) — Every interaction with an official requires a bribe. Not *may*; requires. The registry clerk's fee, the court's "expedition," the patrol sergeant's envelope — there is a rate card and it is updated quarterly. The corporations pay in retainers; the Foot pays in cash at the Hill's lower gate and is grateful to be seen.
2. **Corporate Domains — the Nearspace swarms** (Sprawling Cyberspace, roll 13-43-16-6) — The Hill's Nearspace is thick with data security: every ministry's Domain guarded by whichever corporation "donated" it, four corporate security nimbuses overlapping in the data fog, Continuity's crisis-response monitors humming beside Orison's and Meliora's. Nothing said on the Hill is private, and everyone assumes it — which is why deals are made in the clubs, off-Nearspace, by people who do not carry devices.
3. **Liars & Cheats — the forgeries** (Tech Noir, roll 13-52-26-2) — The Chancery's papers are so routinely bought that its seals have become worthless: a forged residency pass for the Face, a forged lift-permit, a forged remediation contract is as good as a real one, because the real one was also bought. A cottage industry of forgers works out of the law firms' back offices. **[OPEN]** (OQ-8, OQ-21) These are permits and passes, landlord and corporate paper — not caste papers; whether any of it is ever formalized by law is the GM's.
4. **Irregular Religion → Aldine House** (Tao Weaponized, roll 13-22-32-6) — **[TAO-REINTERPRETED]** The book's result is *followers of a specific religion begin to manifest powers from its Mythos*. There is no religion and no Mythos. There is **Aldine House**: a members' club and cultural foundation on the Hill's north side — a library, a hall for recitals, a garden, a movement-and-breath practice taught in the mornings to the Sculpted and their children. Impeccably respectable; a Chancery minister sits on its board. And on the Hill they whisper that some of the House's long-standing members can *do things* — a door that will not open for anyone else, a fire that goes out when they look at it, a man who walked out of a lift shaft. The whisper has a name, **the Quiet Hand** ([[names]] §2), and the House laughs at it. The House is the Wuji's front (Bible §2: "a secret society identifies talented people and brings them into the fold"; Bible §3: the society is "otherwise hidden"). Nothing about the House says so. The morning practice is real. So is the recruiting.

## Who holds ground here

- [[government|The Chancery]] — its seat. The mayor's office, the council, the courts, the registry, the police command.
- [[tao-society|The Wuji]] — Aldine House. Respectable, hidden in plain sight; the Quiet Hand rumor is the only tell, and it is a joke on the Hill.
- [[corp-b|Orison Defense Systems]] — the largest "donor" to the Chancery's security Domains; the Chancery's police carry Orison sidearms.
- [[upstart|Continuity]] — the fourth bidder; crisis-response monitors in the Nearspace and a new retainer with the police command.
- [[corp-a|Meliora]] and [[corp-c|AP&I]] — retainers, lobbyists, a floor each in the ministries.

## Places

- **The Chancery** — The building itself: five floors of stone around a glass atrium, the registry in the basement, the council chamber under the dome, the police command in a modern annex bolted to the back like an afterthought. A Seat of Power in the book's sense (Core p. 43), and a Marketplace in every other sense: the atrium is where the Foot queues with envelopes. The escapee lists the corporations file are held in the registry, on paper, because paper cannot be Harnessed.
- **Aldine House** — Three storeys of pale brick behind a wall and a gate, a garden of raked gravel and a single old tree, a recital hall, and a library whose reading room is the quietest place on the Wall. The morning practice happens in the garden: forty people in grey moving slowly, and one instructor who never seems to be the same person twice. A Meeting Place of the highest respectability — a corporate exec can be seen here with anyone — and a Secured Site nobody has ever tried, because nobody has ever thought of it as one.
- **The lower gate** — Where the Hill's road meets the Crest's ring road and the public lift-head: a checkpoint of Chancery police, a row of "permit assistance" kiosks that are forgers, and a café where the clerks take their envelopes over coffee. The only place on the Hill a Patched crew can stand without a reason.

## Hooks

- A registry clerk will sell the crew a look at AP&I's escapee list — their own names on it — for a price that goes up every time they ask.
- Aldine House quietly offers a scholarship to a PC who did something they should not have been able to do. There is no interview. There is a morning practice.
- A forged remediation contract with the Chancery's seal is worth a fortune to whoever can prove it is forged — or to whoever can prove it is real.
