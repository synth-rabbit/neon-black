# Palisade

**Formally** the Palisade Metropolitan Zone. **On the street:** *the Wall* for the top of the city, *under the Wall* for the bottom ([[names]] §1). An invented megacity with sprawl, heavily *Night City*-inspired, and the whole world for play: **the city is the story** (Bible §3). Nothing outside it is defined — **[OPEN]** (OQ-7).

This file is the Megacity of Series Step 2 (Core p. 149–150) at the level the book asks for: "just enough worldbuilding to get your imagination going" (Core p. 160). The districts are in [[district-directory]] and `districts/`; the future history is in [[timeline]]. The frontmatter uses the `district` schema so the whole city converts as one JournalEntry beside its districts; `zone_code: 11-66` means *all of it*.

## The shape of the city — `[BUILD CHOICE]` (BC-15)

The Bible gives the city a name that implies a geography — *Palisade*, *the Wall*, *under the Wall* — and nothing more. This is the proposal that makes the name literal. The GM may flatten it.

**Palisade is built up and down one enormous wall.** Where a high plateau drops to a coastal lowland, an escarpment runs for kilometers along the shore. Generations of flood barrier, seawall, foundation, and stacked construction have turned the cliff into a single tiered megastructure — *the Palisade*, *the Wall* — and the city grew on it, into it, and beneath it. Four tiers, top to bottom:

| Tier | Street name | What it is | Caste band ([[names]] §4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Crest** | *the Wall*, *up top* | The plateau's edge and the towers built on it: corporate headquarters, the government's hill, the gated terraces of the Sculpted. Daylight. | **Sculpted** |
| **The Face** | *the Face*, *the tiers* | Decks, galleries, and stacks cut into and hung from the cliff, linked by tier lifts and lift-cages: corporate campuses, the commercial arcades, the arena, the data ward. Warranty-card clean where a corp pays for it. | **Fitted** |
| **The Foot** | *the Foot*, *street level* | The lowland at the base of the Wall: the port and the market, the medical corridor, tenement blocks, the yards, the relay fields. In the Wall's shadow by mid-afternoon. | **Patched** |
| **The Under** | *under the Wall*, *the low city*, *the Sinks* | The sprawl behind the levees, the reclaimed marsh below sea level kept dry by pumps, the galleries inside the Wall's foundation, the quarantined basin, and the outfall wastes at the sprawl's edge. Whatever light still works. | **Stock** |

**The caste gradient runs top to bottom.** That is the whole point of the geography: your tier is your band, and the tier lifts are where everyone finds out. "There is some mobility, but the lower you are, the harder it is to climb" (Bible §3) — in Palisade, *climb* is not a metaphor. The **sprawl** is the Under spreading inland from the Foot for as far as the pumps reach: kilometers of prefab housing, automats, dead relay towers, and the drained marsh, thinning toward the outfall wastes where the city's drainage discharges and the map ends.

**[OPEN]** (OQ-21) Whether movement between tiers is *controlled* — passes, checkpoints, a lift that will not take a Stock fare — is a caste-marker question the GM owns (OQ-8). The districts write the lifts as a corporate and landlord matter, not a law.

## The city government and the law (Bible §3)

City government exists and is **deeply corrupt**. Its seat is [[chancery-hill]] and the street calls the government **the Chancery** after it (**[BUILD CHOICE]**, BC-16). The Chancery collects — from three corporations who agreed on the price, and lately from a fourth bidder ([[spine-and-clock]]).

**Corporate security forces are exactly as legal as the city police** (Bible §3). Not *more* legal: exactly as legal. A Meliora Bioworks security team and a Chancery patrol have the same standing to stop you, search you, shoot you, and file the report. In practice the corporations' forces are better paid, better armed, and present wherever a corporation owns the ground, which on the Crest and the Face is everywhere; the police are what is left for the Foot and the Under. When the two disagree, the one with the better lawyer wins. Corporate enclaves work as the book describes them — "inside a corp enclave the laws are dictated by the corporate elite, and the government has no say" (Core p. 41) — except that in Palisade the government has no say *anywhere it has been paid not to*.

## The caste system (Bible §3; Brief §6.6)

**The beginning of a formal caste system.** It started as economic and is slowly becoming formalized — in part because access to cybernetics, gene mods, and body sculpting is unequal, and what was done to your body is visible, warrantied, and monitored (Bible §3). The city's slang for the bands, top to bottom, is **Sculpted / Fitted / Patched / Stock** — bodies rebuilt to spec so well the work is invisible; corporate-issue implants, warrantied and monitored; second-hand, black-market, DIY, and it shows; factory-standard flesh, unmodified because unaffordable ([[names]] §4, BC-9, GM-approved).

**Slang only.** No legal tier, no registry, no visible marker is asserted anywhere in this vault — **[OPEN]** (OQ-8). The Bible says *formalizing*, not *formal*: the drift from economic to bodily sorting is what the [[timeline]] tracks and what the Series can push either way. The slang has a known tension: *Stock* describes unaffordable flesh, while a Baseline (hunter) may be unmodified by choice at any income — the slang is class talk, not a classification of the five, and a wealthy Baseline is not "stock" ([[names]] §4).

**Who owns the tech:** a mix — corporate, black market, DIY. No single actor controls the mod ecosystem (Bible §1). A Fitted body was bought from a corporation with a warranty; a Patched body was bought from a chop-shop in [[suture-row]] or built in a kitchen; a Sculpted body was bought with money the Foot will never see. **Extreme transformation is rare** (Bible §2): the average Palisadian has *some* work done; the five are rare, and Tao use is rarer.

## The Masquerade as Noise plus cleanup (Brief §4.1)

**[RULES CONFLICT]** (CR-4, adopted by the Brief) The book says "the supernatural exists openly, but it doesn't get headlines" because the Noise suffocates interest (Core p. 22). The Bible says Bloodware, Howlers, the Wuji, and the Cutloose are secret (§3). The Brief's resolution, cited here as required: **keep the book's Noise as the reason the Masquerade is cheap to maintain, layered with active corporate cleanup and each secret group's self-protection** (Brief §4.1).

In Palisade that means:

- **The Noise does most of the work.** "Sensory and cognitive oversaturation … drown the viewer's brain in jaded apathy" (Core p. 22). Someone climbing a tier lift shaft with their bare hands is a stunt, an ad, a new implant, a feed nobody finished watching.
- **The public knows extreme modding exists** — and sees it, legally, in three places: **bodyguards** (a Sculpted exec's security is visibly more than human), **sculpted entertainers** (the idols and dancers of [[corbel-gallery]] and [[halloran-circus]]), and **arena fighters** (the Circus' fight circuits) (Bible §3). These are the *permitted* transformations; they are also the alibi for every impermissible one.
- **Public incidents caused by the secret splats are blamed on Baselines** (Bible §2, §3). "Another baseline with a gun" is the corporate press release; the cover works because it is boring.
- **The corporations do the cleanup** for Bloodware and Howlers — they regard the Ferrante strain as a disease that flares up and occasionally produces a clan worth wiping out (Bible §2) — and **the Wuji keep themselves secret**, which the corporations prefer (Bible §3). The Cutloose keep themselves hidden because being found is death.
- **What the public does *not* know:** that any of the four exist as societies; that [[upstart|Continuity]] is what it is; whether Tao exists at all as distinct from the Wuji — **[OPEN]** (OQ-6), written as hidden.

The Masquerade's failure mode is the Series' clock: [[secret-war-goes-public]]. Note what that clock breaks and what it does not — the *corporate* war becomes public; the splats stay secret.

## Cyberspace — as written (Brief §6.5)

Cyberspace runs exactly as the Core Book describes it (Core p. 27–28); nothing in Palisade changes the rules.

- **Nearspace** — the shallow layer overlaid on the physical city: the data fog every person and machine exudes, AR alternates, local Domains reachable by walking into a building, and the data flows beneath it all (Core p. 27–28). In Palisade the Nearspace is tiered like the city: dense with corporate security on the Crest and the Face ([[chancery-hill]]), lawless and cheap on the Foot ([[relay-fields]]), and patchy to dead in the Under, where the grid is older than the pumps.
- **Domains** — the secured server estates of corporations and the government, "private land, possibly walled off or guarded" (Core p. 27). The Big Three host theirs in their HQ districts; [[the-lattice]] is where the rest of the city's Domains are physically kept, which is why Continuity took it.
- **The Tangle** — the civilized, guarded bulk of cyberspace where commerce and entertainment live, with the Hoods as its local server spaces (Core p. 28). Palisade's Hoods are sold by the tier: a Crest Hood costs what a Foot apartment does.
- **Harnessing** — attacking secure systems with a brain's processing power, in a shared metaphor (Core p. 22). Continuity's hacking specialty is built on Harnessed teams; its cyberspace resources are in its Key Player entry (WP4). Harnessing parlors on the Foot rent by the hour.

**Tao and cyberspace — one line:** **[OPEN]** (OQ-15) Whether Tao is present in Palisade's cyberspace — whether the book's "cyberspace Thin Places" (Core p. 33) have a Tao counterpart — is flagged, not decided. Two districts leave the hook and assert nothing: [[foundation-galleries]] (a cut-off Domain read as omens) and [[lowmere-sinks]] (drowned server farms that answer back).
