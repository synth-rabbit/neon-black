# Palisade — Player Primer

Everything on this page is common knowledge — what a person born under the Wall, or dropped there, would know without asking. Nothing here is a secret, a twist, or a thing an organization would kill to keep quiet. Where the vault knows more than the street does, that knowledge lives in other files. **[BUILD CHOICE]** (BC-177) This page exists so that the character-creation files can name a district or a company without opening a door to anything they should not.

## The Wall

Palisade — formally the *Palisade Metropolitan Zone* — is built up and down one enormous escarpment on the coast, tiered by centuries of seawall, flood barrier and stacked construction into a single structure the city calls **the Wall**. *Up top* is the Crest; *under the Wall* is the bottom. Four tiers, top to bottom:

| Tier | Street name | What is there | Who lives there |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Crest** | *the Wall*, *up top* | Corporate headquarters, the government's hill, gated terraces. Daylight. | **Sculpted** |
| **The Face** | *the Face*, *the tiers* | Decks and galleries cut into the cliff and hung from it, linked by tier lifts: corporate campuses, the arcades, the arena, the data ward. | **Fitted** |
| **The Foot** | *the Foot*, *street level* | The lowland at the base: the port and the market, the medical corridor, the tenements, the yards, the relay fields. In shadow by mid-afternoon. | **Patched** |
| **The Under** | *under the Wall*, *the low city* | The sprawl behind the levees, the drowned marsh kept dry by pumps, the tunnels inside the Wall's foundation, the sealed basin, the outfall wastes at the edge of the map. | **Stock** |

**Your tier is your band.** There is no law that says so and no card that proves it; the tier lifts are where everyone finds out anyway. Climbing is possible and gets harder the lower you start.

## The castes, as the street says them

Nobody official uses these words. Everybody else does. Your band is what was done to your body:

- **Sculpted** — rebuilt to specification so well the work is invisible.
- **Fitted** — corporate-issue implants, warrantied and monitored. *In good standing* is a compliment and a ledger line.
- **Patched** — second-hand, black-market, kitchen-built; it shows.
- **Stock** — factory-standard flesh, unmodified because unaffordable. *Born stock, die stock.* (A person who is unmodified by choice, at any income, is not "stock" — the word is about money, not about bodies.)

Almost everyone has *some* work done. Extreme transformation — a body that is visibly more than human — is rare, and the city sees it legally in three places: an executive's bodyguards, the sculpted entertainers of the Gallery and the Circus, and the arena fighters. When something more than human happens in public and nobody can explain it, the press release blames *another Baseline with a gun*, and the feeds move on.

## The districts

Eighteen, by tier. The name in bold is what the street says; the phrase after it is what everyone knows the place for.

### Aurelian Crest
The gated terraces at the top of the Wall, where the Sculpted live and the work is invisible because it is perfect. *Never been told no.*

### Chancery Hill
The government's hill and the respectable houses around it: registries, law firms, members' clubs, a lift-head with a police post and a row of permit kiosks. *Everything has a price list.*

### Meliora Terraces
Meliora Bioworks' greenhouse-tower campus — genomics, sculpting, a cure for anything you can afford. *Grown to specification.*

### Orison Reach
Orison Defense Systems' fortress at the Wall's end: test ranges, armories, a corporation nobody examines too closely. *The guns face outward.*

### Amalgam Stack
AP&I's vertical factory-city bolted into the cliff, where the chrome is made and the workers wear it. *The company owns the body.*

### The Lattice
The data ward: the server halls behind a plaza hung off the cliff, where most of the city's Domains physically live. Continuity Risk & Response has held it under a service contract since the Lattice fire three years ago. *Someone is always listening.*

### Corbel Gallery
The long commercial arcade cut into the Face — warranty-card clean, brand-loyal, the birthplace of every cosmetic-implant trend. *Judged by your brand.*

### Halloran Circus
The arena — the Halloran Bowl — and everything around it: sculpted fighters, idols, bookies, ringfights below the licensed ring. *The crowd wants blood.*

### Suture Row
The medical corridor at the base of the Wall: warranty clinics upstairs, chop-shops at street level, floodwater in the basements in storm season. *Stitched up, sent back out.*

### Gullet Market
The port-market where everything that falls off the Wall lands, in the run-off channel at the Wall's throat — the Labyrinth of stalls, the locks, the quay, and the old customs weighhouse. *Everything falls to the Gullet.*

### Marlow Blocks
Dense tenement blocks fed by the Marlow Relief Kitchen — meals, a clinic, small loans, and a tab everyone is on. *Everyone owes the Kitchen.*

### Cinder Yards
The dead rendering and reclamation yards: kiln rows, rail sidings, a chimney that still smokes. Gang ground — the Run's. *This is pack ground.*

### Relay Fields
The dead broadcast masts and the coffin-block stacks under them: cheap Nearspace, petty hackers, and an old defense grid that still shoots at things. *Every kid is a hacker.*

### Kilbride Stretch
Kilometres of prefab sprawl and automats inland from the Foot, where the Stock live, the robots work, and every official expects an envelope. *Nobody official comes down here.*

### Lowmere Sinks
The reclaimed marsh below sea level, half-drowned, where skill chips replace schooling and the pumps are always running late. *The pumps are running late.*

### Foundation Galleries
The galleries and flood tunnels inside the Wall's own foundation — the deepest place in the city, and the hardest to find anyone in. *The Wall has hollow bones.*

### Ferrante Basin
The walled quarantine where Ferrante Nanoscale's campus stood a century ago, sealed since the nanite disaster the city still curses by the company's name — *a Ferrante flare-up*. *Don't touch anything.*

### Coldwater Outfall
The drainage wastes at the sprawl's edge, declared uninhabitable by the Chancery and held under a remediation lease. Nobody goes there. *Nobody's supposed to be here.*

## The companies everyone knows

Three corporations with fingers in every pie and one thing each is known for; a fourth that is newer, louder, and everywhere lately; and the government they all pay.

### Meliora Bioworks
*Meliora, Mel.* **Bio** — genomics, pharma, gene mods, sculpting. The friendly Latin name on the greenhouse tower. Headquarters: Meliora Terraces.

### Orison Defense Systems
*Orison.* **Weapons.** Bland, respectable, and the first name on every corporate security contract's kit list. Headquarters: Orison Reach.

### Amalgamated Prosthetic & Interface
*AP&I, Amalgam.* **Cybernetics** — mass-market chrome, warranties, financing: the ladder itself. Every Fitted body has an AP&I warranty tag under the skin somewhere. Headquarters: Amalgam Stack.

### Continuity Risk & Response
*Continuity.* **Security, crisis management, hacking.** The firm whose grey vans arrive at an emergency before the fire service does — the Lattice fire made its name — and whose people wear matte grey soft-shell with a serial where a name would be. Headquarters: the plaza tower in the Lattice.

### The Chancery
The city government, named on the street for its hill. Deeply corrupt in the ordinary way: every permit, list, lease and licence has a price, every official expects an envelope, and the registry is kept on paper because paper cannot be hacked. **Corporate security forces are exactly as legal as the city police** — a Meliora security team and a Chancery patrol have the same standing to stop you, search you and file the report, and the one with the better lawyer wins.

### The Almoners and the Kitchen
The Marlow Relief Kitchen is a real charity — meals, a clinic, loans — and the Blocks owe it. *The Almoners*, *the Alms*, is what the street calls the people behind it, in the tone the street uses for a syndicate. What the Alms sell out of the Kitchen's back door is a street drug called **Leash**, and who buys it is the Run.

### The Run
The gangs of the Cinder Yards — the Smokes, the Sidings, the Nineteens — who call their culture *the Run* and are said to run on Leash. Fast, territorial, gang-sized, and not to be met on their own ground after dark. What the drug actually does to them is a rumor with several versions.

### The Quiet Hand
A rumor, and only a rumor: that somewhere up the Wall there is an old society of people who can do things nobody can explain, and that it has a hand in more than it should. Nobody who says the words can point at anyone. Most people say them as a joke.

## What the crew knows that the city does not

Five things a crew of escapees carry that a resident does not. They are player knowledge, not secrets the players must keep from each other — the Bible leaves how much the crew know of one another to the players.

### The Ledger
**Reconciliation Facility 4** — *the Ledger* to the people inside it — is AP&I's debt-labor and penal work camp in the Coldwater Outfall, under the remediation lease, behind wire, on a causeway across the sludge flats. It is secret; the crew were in it; nobody in the city will believe them, and nobody at AP&I will say the word. The Series opens on the night it was struck.

### The escapee list
The crew are on AP&I's escapee list. They are not worth a bounty — no assets, second-hand chrome, not worth a collector's day rate. That is the whole of AP&I's posture toward them, until they make themselves worth the trouble.

### Rook
**Tomas Adair, "Rook"** — the leader of the crew's work line in the Ledger, who acclimated them, protected them, and was the one person the strike came for. He was taken, undamaged, by people who did not look at anyone else, and before he went he gave the crew a name and a place. What he is, and why he matters, the crew do not know.

### The Weighhouse
**Marisol Okonkwo, "Tally"** — the fence at the Gullet's old customs weighhouse, on the quay: a brass balance, a water door and a counter door, a slate by the door with the names of who owes the house chalked in public. She is the one name Rook gave the crew, and the counter through which their first work will come, at a percentage.

### The Wall from below
The crew start at the bottom — under the Wall, Patched or Stock, with camp fatigues, a shed toolkit, and a debt. Everything above them is somebody else's ground.
